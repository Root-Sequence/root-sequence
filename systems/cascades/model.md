# Cascade Model

A cascade is the propagation of failure from one node to others through dependencies and connections.

## Core terms

A cascade origin is the initial point of failure.

A cascade path is the sequence of nodes and edges through which failure travels.

A direct cascade occurs when failure moves immediately along a dependency.

An indirect cascade occurs when failure propagates through multiple steps or intermediate conditions.

A branching cascade occurs when one failure affects multiple nodes simultaneously.

An amplifying cascade increases in impact as it spreads.

An attenuating cascade weakens as it spreads.

A choke point is a location where propagation can be slowed or stopped.

A containment boundary is a structural limit that prevents further spread.

## Patterns

Cascades often follow:

- dependency chains
- trust relationships
- shared infrastructure
- tightly coupled systems
- hidden connections

## Key properties

Cascades may be:

- immediate or delayed
- visible or silent
- linear or branching
- contained or unbounded

## Guiding questions

Where can failure begin?

Where can it travel?

What connections enable propagation?

What amplifies it?

What stops it?

## Principle

The structure of a system determines the shape of its cascades.
