# Coherence

Coherence is the alignment between a system’s structure, its assumptions, and reality.

Where fragility describes when systems amplify failure, cascades describe how failure spreads, and recovery describes how systems respond after disruption, coherence describes whether the system was aligned in the first place.

A system is coherent when its internal logic, dependencies, and expectations match the conditions it actually operates within. It is incoherent when it relies on assumptions that do not hold, when its structure contradicts its environment, or when its behavior diverges from its intended purpose.

Incoherence often remains invisible until stress is applied. Under ideal conditions, a system may appear stable, functional, or even optimal. When conditions change, hidden mismatches surface. What seemed stable begins to break, and fragility emerges.

In this sense, fragility is often a symptom of incoherence.

A system that assumes trust in an adversarial environment becomes fragile. A system that assumes stability in a dynamic environment becomes fragile. A system that assumes correctness in the presence of human error becomes fragile.

Coherence does not mean perfection or rigidity. It means that the system is shaped with an accurate understanding of its context. It allows for variation, failure, and adaptation without depending on unrealistic conditions.

## Core idea

Fragility often emerges where coherence is missing.

## Relationship to other system areas

- `fragility/` examines when small failures become large consequences.
- `cascades/` examines how failure propagates.
- `recovery/` examines how systems respond after disruption.
- `coherence/` examines whether the system was aligned with reality to begin with.

Coherence shapes fragility.
Fragility enables cascades.
Recovery responds to what remains.

## Questions

What assumptions does this system make?

Do those assumptions match reality?

Where does the system rely on ideal conditions?

Where does behavior diverge from design?

What only works when nothing goes wrong?

What breaks when conditions change?

## Examples

- A security system that assumes users are honest in a hostile environment
- A social system that assumes shared norms without making them explicit
- An infrastructure system that assumes uptime without planning for failure
- A personal system that assumes consistent energy or attention

In each case, the system may function until reality diverges from its assumptions.

## Directions

Future work may include:

- mapping assumption layers
- identifying mismatch between design and reality
- aligning systems with real-world conditions
- designing for dynamic environments
- integrating coherence with adaptation
