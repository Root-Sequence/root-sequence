# Systems Principles

**Document role:** Local index for cross-domain analytical systems principles<br>
**Status:** Developing<br>

This directory contains current analytical lenses. It is distinct from the historical and expressive material under [`founding-texts/`](../../founding-texts/README.md).

These are not rules. They are lenses.

They help explain why systems behave the way they do across different scales: people, relationships, communities, institutions, and infrastructures.

The goal is not to flatten everything into abstraction. The goal is to keep the bigger picture visible without losing direction, accountability, or lived reality.

## Current principles

- [`asymmetric-systems.md`](asymmetric-systems.md) → some systems shape conditions; others adapt to them. These roles are not equivalent.
- [`misclassification.md`](misclassification.md) → systems often mislabel what is happening in order to preserve stability.
- [`feedback-loops.md`](feedback-loops.md) → repeated interactions create patterns that reinforce themselves over time.
- [`reinforcement.md`](reinforcement.md) → systems do not stabilize around truth alone; they stabilize around what gets repeated and reinforced.
- [`non-reversal.md`](non-reversal.md) → ending harm means interrupting its pattern rather than merely reversing who occupies each role.

## Root Sequence lens

When looking at a system, ask:

1. Who is shaping conditions?
2. Who is adapting to those conditions?
3. What is being mislabeled?
4. What is being repeated and reinforced?
5. Who benefits from the resulting pattern?
6. Who absorbs the cost?

## Why this matters

A system can look normal while reproducing harm.

A system can also contain the seeds of repair, recovery, or transformation.

These principles help make both sides visible.
