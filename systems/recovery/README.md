# Recovery

Recovery is the process by which a system restores function, limits damage, reconstitutes itself, or continues in an acceptable form after failure.

Where fragility describes the tendency of systems to turn small failures into large consequences, and cascades describe how failure propagates through connected parts, recovery describes what becomes possible after disruption has already occurred.

A system does not need to return to its exact previous state in order to recover. In some cases, recovery means restoration. In others, it means partial continuity, graceful degradation, adaptation, or rebuilding under changed conditions. What matters is whether the system can continue without collapse, permanent loss, or total disorientation.

Recovery depends on structure. It depends on whether backups exist, whether roles are distributed, whether knowledge is documented, whether failure is visible, whether trust can be repaired, and whether alternate paths remain available when the primary path is lost. A system may appear strong until it is forced to recover. That is often when its real design becomes visible.

Recovery is not just technical. It appears in infrastructure, identity, communities, institutions, habits, and ways of thinking. A storage system recovers through backup and restore. A community recovers through repair, redistribution of labor, and trust rebuilding. A person may recover through adaptation, support, reflection, and the reorganization of internal patterns. In each case, recovery is shaped by what remains possible after failure.

The key question is not only whether something breaks. It is whether the system can respond without becoming something worse.

## Core idea

Failure happens.

Recovery determines whether the system continues.

## Relationship to other system areas

- `fragility/` examines when systems allow small failures to become large consequences.
- `cascades/` examines how failure propagates through connections.
- `recovery/` examines how systems restore function, contain damage, or continue after disruption.

Fragility describes the condition.
Cascades describe the spread.
Recovery describes the response.
