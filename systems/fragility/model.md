# Fragility Model

Fragility is the tendency of a system to turn small failures into disproportionately large consequences.

The central question of fragility is not whether a system fails, but how failure behaves once it appears.

A fragile system allows local failure to propagate into broader loss, collapse, compromise, or unrecoverable damage. A less fragile system contains failure, makes it visible, and preserves paths to recovery.

## Core terms

A node is any component within a system that carries function, value, access, responsibility, memory, legitimacy, or structural weight.

An edge is a dependency, trust relationship, pathway, or connection between nodes.

Stress is any pressure placed on the system. This may include attack, misuse, neglect, overload, contradiction, drift, decay, interruption, scarcity, conflict, or unexpected change.

Failure is the loss, corruption, compromise, distortion, inaccessibility, or breakdown of a node, edge, assumption, or process.

Propagation is the way failure travels through dependencies, trust relationships, tight couplings, or hidden connections.

Recovery is the ability of a system to restore function, limit damage, reconstitute itself, or continue in an acceptable form after failure.

Containment is the capacity of a system to keep failure local rather than allowing it to spread.

Visibility is the degree to which failure can be detected, understood, and responded to.

## Core pattern

Fragility tends to emerge when:

- too much weight is concentrated in one place
- dependencies are tight, hidden, or poorly understood
- trust is assumed without strong verification
- failure is allowed to spread
- recovery is weak, absent, or unclear
- assumptions do not match reality
- important components are invisible until they fail

## Guiding questions

What happens if this breaks?

What happens next?

What does it take down with it?

Can the system recover?

## Working principle

The opposite of fragility is not perfection. It is containment, visibility, and recoverability.

## Short form

Fragility is not the existence of failure.

Fragility is the amplification of failure.
