# Fragility

Fragility is what happens when a system turns small failures into large consequences.

This is not limited to technology. It appears in identity systems, infrastructure, institutions, communities, habits, and thought patterns. Wherever there are dependencies, assumptions, and concentrated points of weight, there is the possibility that failure travels farther than expected.

A fragile system does not simply fail. It fails through connection. A breach becomes a cascade. A missing component becomes a collapse. A local problem becomes a systemic one. The issue is not failure itself, since all systems fail eventually. The issue is what kind of behavior the system produces once failure appears.

This section of Root Sequence treats fragility as a cross-domain pattern. It asks where dependencies accumulate, where hidden assumptions live, how stress propagates, and what makes recovery possible or impossible. The goal is not to eliminate failure. It is to understand how failure behaves, how to contain it, and how to design systems that can survive stress without becoming something worse.

Small failure -> large consequence.

Or:

Small failure -> small consequence.

The difference is structure.

## In this folder

- `applications/` contains concrete examples and working uses of the model.

## Related directions

Fragility connects closely to cascades, recovery, resilience, coherence, adaptation, and system design. It is one of the main ways to study how systems actually behave under pressure rather than how they claim to behave in theory.

Fragility is closely related to cascades.

If fragility describes whether failure expands, cascades describe how it spreads.
