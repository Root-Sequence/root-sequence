# Research Questions

These questions should guide investigation before hardening assumptions into institutional rules.

## Community

- What makes people feel both connected and free?
- How much shared identity is useful before it becomes conformity?
- What rhythms support spontaneous collaboration without constant interruption?
- How do introverted, neurodivergent, disabled, chronically ill, caregiving, and socially anxious participants experience communal environments?
- Which conflicts are ordinary and which indicate structural problems?

## Interdisciplinarity

- Under what conditions do people from different practices genuinely learn from one another rather than merely coexist?
- How much shared vocabulary is needed?
- Which facilitation methods improve translation without flattening differences?
- Can art, science, philosophy, technology, politics, and craft retain their own standards while still interacting productively?

## Space

- Which architectural features produce accidental encounters?
- Which features protect solitude?
- What ratio of private to shared space works at different community sizes?
- How do noise, light, accessibility, tools, food, outdoors, transportation, and proximity to a city affect participation?

## Governance

- Which decisions need collective consent?
- Which can be delegated?
- How can authority remain legible without creating procedural overload?
- How do residents challenge decisions safely?
- What happens when values conflict?
- How do temporary visitors interact with resident governance?

## Economics

- Can the commons include people without independent wealth?
- What mix of rent, membership, grants, donations, public funding, endowment, cooperative revenue, or shared services is sustainable?
- Which funding sources distort the mission?
- Who bears financial risk?

## Labor

- Which work can be eliminated or automated?
- Which work should remain chosen human participation?
- Which work needs compensation?
- What happens when people cannot or do not contribute equally?
- How are care and emotional labor handled without making residents responsible for one another's wellbeing by default?

## Knowledge and documentation

- What should be open by default?
- What should be private by default?
- How are experiments, failures, and institutional learning documented?
- How can people participate without becoming data?
- What forms of peer review make sense outside conventional academia?

## Scale

- What is lost when 8 people become 20, 50, or 200?
- Which parts should remain small and federated?
- Is a single campus even desirable, or would a network of nodes be healthier?

## Evaluation

The goal is not to produce one "coherence score."

Use multiple forms of evidence: participant experience, retention, accessibility, labor distribution, costs, conflict patterns, collaboration, project histories, observational research with consent, and comparison across pilots.
