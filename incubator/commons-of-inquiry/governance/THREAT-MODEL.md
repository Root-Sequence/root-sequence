# Institutional Threat Model

Good intentions do not prevent bad institutions.

This document tracks ways the commons could fail structurally.

## Founder capture

**Risk:** the person who starts the project becomes its permanent authority, cultural center, owner, or interpreter.

Questions:

- Which decisions belong to founders only during bootstrap?
- When and how does authority transfer?
- Can the community remove a founder from an operational role?
- Who owns names, domains, property, money, archives, and infrastructure?

## Informal hierarchy

**Risk:** nominally horizontal structures conceal power based on charisma, expertise, money, friendship networks, seniority, communication style, race/class/gender dynamics, or control of information.

Mitigations to test:

- visible decision records;
- rotating facilitation where useful;
- multiple communication modes;
- explicit authority scopes;
- anonymous or low-pressure feedback;
- conflict and appeal channels;
- periodic power mapping.

## Cult dynamics

**Risk:** belonging becomes tied to loyalty, ideology, founder admiration, social isolation, or control over housing and livelihood.

Guardrails to investigate:

- strong external relationships encouraged;
- no required worldview;
- easy physical and social exit;
- independent income / housing paths where possible;
- no leader treated as epistemically privileged;
- clear boundaries between community, therapy, employment, and friendship.

## Economic exclusion

**Risk:** the commons becomes a retreat for people already wealthy enough to opt out of ordinary work.

Questions:

- sliding scale, stipends, fellowships, work exchange, or public funding?
- how to avoid work exchange becoming disguised underpaid labor?
- how are caregivers and people with unstable income included?
- how can costs remain legible?

## Invisible labor capture

**Risk:** a subset of residents becomes responsible for food, cleaning, mediation, accessibility, maintenance, or emotional support.

Track labor rather than assuming goodwill equalizes it.

## Productivity capture

**Risk:** grants, prestige, publications, startups, or donor expectations turn the commons into an output machine.

Possible indicators:

- people feel guilty for resting;
- status follows measurable output;
- funders shape inquiry;
- residents optimize for external recognition;
- art or care is valued only when it produces deliverables.

## Ideological capture

**Risk:** a shared political tendency hardens into mandatory agreement.

An anarchist-inspired commons can still become authoritarian in practice. Political commitments should be discussable, revisable, and distinguishable from requirements necessary for living safely together.

## Academic capture

**Risk:** credentials, jargon, publication norms, and prestige hierarchies recreate a university without admitting it.

## Commercial capture

**Risk:** the institution becomes an accelerator, recruitment funnel, landlord, consultancy, or branding project.

## Surveillance through documentation

**Risk:** a culture of research and reflection records too much about residents.

The right not to be documented should be normal.

## Scale failure

**Risk:** practices that work among eight trusted people are assumed to work among eighty.

Revalidate governance, labor, access, and safety at each scale.
