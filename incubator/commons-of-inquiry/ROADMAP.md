# Roadmap

This roadmap deliberately separates **institution-building** from **property acquisition**.

## Phase 0 — Map and recruit

Goal: determine whether there is a real community and design space worth developing.

- map precedents and failure modes;
- identify potential participants across art, science, tech, philosophy, organizing, craft, writing, ecology, and adjacent practices;
- host small interdisciplinary gatherings;
- build a public vocabulary for the project;
- learn what people actually want from a commons;
- document assumptions before turning them into rules.

**Exit test:** enough recurring interest and trust to justify a residential pilot.

## Phase 1 — Pilot gatherings

Goal: test the culture without pretending a weekend is a permanent community.

Possible formats:

- shared dinner + open inquiry night;
- one-day salon/build/art gathering;
- weekend residential pilot;
- small unconference;
- field trip followed by collective reflection.

Measure what emerges spontaneously and what requires facilitation.

**Exit test:** at least one format produces meaningful cross-pollination without exhausting participants or relying on one organizer to carry everything.

## Phase 2 — Temporary commons

Goal: live together long enough for ordinary life, maintenance, conflict, solitude, and unplanned collaboration to appear.

- 1–4 weeks;
- roughly 8–20 participants;
- private retreat space;
- communal workspace and meals;
- voluntary talks / demos / critique / reading;
- explicit labor and accessibility agreements;
- anonymous feedback and post-pilot review.

**Exit test:** participants want another iteration and the labor/economic model is not obviously exploitative or brittle.

## Phase 3 — Persistent house

Goal: test whether a durable residential node creates benefits that temporary gatherings cannot.

- perhaps 6–15 residents;
- guest / visiting participant capacity;
- workshop + library + communal room;
- resident governance;
- transparent costs and labor;
- conflict and exit processes;
- public programming that does not erase home life.

**Exit test:** the house remains desirable after novelty fades.

## Phase 4 — Networked institution

Goal: separate the idea from a single property.

Possible components:

- multiple houses or partner spaces;
- visiting residencies;
- shared digital commons;
- public archive and publications;
- workshops / studios / labs;
- mutual-aid and equipment networks;
- exchange with aligned institutions.

## Phase 5 — Campus, if earned

Only consider a larger permanent campus after smaller forms demonstrate that scale would solve real constraints.

A campus might include residences, studios, workshops, laboratories, library/archive, gardens, kitchens, communal dining, public event space, accessible guest rooms, quiet zones, and outdoor areas.

The campus is not the success condition.

**The success condition is a durable social environment in which people can live well and pursue curiosity together.**
