# Pilot 0 — Make the Feeling Real

Before designing a campus, test whether the desired **social environment** can be produced at small scale.

## Hypothesis

A small, deliberately mixed group of curious people sharing comfortable space, meals, tools, unstructured time, and optional inquiry/creation activities will generate valuable interactions that are difficult to produce through ordinary conferences, coworking, or online communities.

That is a hypothesis, not a promise.

## Suggested scale

- **8–15 people**
- **2–3 days**
- rented house, retreat space, or similarly domestic environment
- private sleeping space where feasible
- at least one quiet / low-stimulation room
- one messy-making area if the property permits it
- communal table and visible work surfaces / whiteboards

## Participant mix

Seek difference in practice, not prestige:

- artist;
- scientist / empirical researcher;
- philosopher / theorist;
- technologist / hacker;
- organizer / commons practitioner;
- writer / historian;
- craft / repair / fabrication person;
- people who cross these categories.

No one needs to represent an entire discipline.

## Structure

Keep scheduling deliberately light.

Possible anchors:

**Opening**
- needs / access / boundaries;
- what everyone is curious about right now;
- available tools and spaces;
- consent around photos, notes, recordings, and research.

**During**
- optional show-and-tell;
- optional mini talks;
- open studio / build periods;
- reading or discussion corners;
- walks;
- shared meals;
- spontaneous sessions anyone can propose;
- protected unscheduled time.

**Closing**
- what felt different here?
- what was exhausting?
- where did unexpected collaboration happen?
- what labor became invisible?
- when did people want more structure or less?
- what would make another version worth attending?

## Do not optimize for

- number of projects;
- papers produced;
- presentations delivered;
- hours worked;
- social participation;
- agreement.

## Things worth observing

With consent and minimal intrusion:

- spontaneous cross-disciplinary conversations;
- new collaborations;
- use of quiet/private space;
- accessibility friction;
- energy demands;
- distribution of domestic and facilitation labor;
- whether newcomers feel able to participate;
- whether people can disengage without social penalty;
- which spatial features actually matter;
- whether participants want to return.

## Success

Pilot 0 succeeds if it teaches us something credible about the institution.

That includes discovering that parts of the idea do **not** work.
