# Physical Space — Working Requirements

Architecture should support **both encounter and retreat**.

## Private

- real bedrooms, not permanent dormitory culture;
- doors that close;
- meaningful acoustic privacy;
- storage;
- accessible rooms;
- ability to disengage from communal activity.

## Shared living

- large comfortable kitchen;
- communal table;
- smaller eating / conversation spaces;
- couches and genuinely comfortable informal seating;
- laundry;
- outdoor space where possible;
- places that feel domestic rather than institutional.

## Inquiry and creation

- library / reading room;
- writable surfaces and whiteboards;
- seminar / discussion room;
- art studio;
- workshop / fabrication area;
- electronics bench;
- computing / networking capacity;
- flexible project rooms;
- archive / documentation storage;
- sinks, ventilation, and mess-tolerant surfaces where appropriate.

Not every node needs every facility. Shared local infrastructure may be better than duplicating expensive tools.

## Sensory and accessibility

- quiet rooms;
- low-light options;
- controllable lighting;
- acoustic treatment;
- step-free routes where possible;
- accessible bathrooms;
- seating variety;
- clear wayfinding;
- fragrance / air-quality considerations;
- remote participation for some activities;
- no requirement to pass through a loud social area to reach basic necessities.

## Spatial principles

### Chance encounters without forced sociability

Common paths can create useful collisions, but bedrooms and essential services should not require constant social exposure.

### Messy and clean zones

A fabrication project should not compete with someone reading or cooking.

### Multiple scales of gathering

Support one person, two people, six people, twenty people, and larger public events without making one giant room do everything badly.

### Public edge / private core

If the commons hosts events, consider spatial separation so public access does not turn residents' home into a permanent venue.

## Long-term campus possibilities

Only after smaller pilots justify them:

- multiple residential buildings;
- guest / residency housing;
- workshops and labs;
- studios;
- library and archive;
- gardens / food growing;
- communal kitchen and dining;
- outdoor work and gathering;
- public event / exhibition space;
- resilient energy and networking infrastructure;
- accessible mobility around the site.

The gothic-mansion fantasy is optional.

The underlying functions are not.
