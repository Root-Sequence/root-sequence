# Vision

We want to create a place where curious people can **live, think, make, investigate, experiment, teach, learn, rest, and build relationships** without requiring their work to fit neatly into a discipline, career, company, credential, or institution.

Science, art, technology, philosophy, history, craft, politics, ecology, writing, repair, social experimentation, and ordinary life are all legitimate ways of understanding and engaging with the world.

The commons exists to create unusually good conditions for curiosity.

People should be able to follow questions across disciplinary boundaries; start projects because they are interesting, beautiful, useful, strange, or worth testing; find collaborators organically; teach one another what they know; challenge assumptions; make things; document what they learn; and have enough privacy, security, time, and autonomy to do none of those things when they need rest.

## Inquiry is broader than research

A paper may come out of the commons.

So might a sculpture.

Or a piece of software.

Or a garden.

Or a political experiment.

Or a repair manual.

Or a novel.

Or a public archive.

Or a musical instrument.

Or a new cooperative protocol.

Or a three-hour conversation that changes how someone understands a problem.

Making is not subordinate to research. Art is not merely a way to communicate science. Philosophy is not merely an ethics department. Technology is not merely implementation. Everyday life is not background infrastructure.

Different practices can reveal different aspects of reality.

## The community itself is part of the inquiry

The commons can investigate questions by living with them:

- How can shared resources expand autonomy rather than constrain it?
- How can hierarchy be reduced without allowing invisible hierarchy to flourish?
- How should necessary work be distributed, reduced, automated, compensated, or rotated?
- How can residents have strong privacy inside a genuinely communal place?
- What kinds of spaces support both spontaneous collaboration and sensory retreat?
- How can disagreement remain generative without requiring ideological sameness?
- How can people without wealth, credentials, prestige, or conventional careers participate?
- What does responsible human–AI collaboration look like in a community devoted to agency?
- What ownership and governance arrangements make a commons durable without turning it into a landlord, company, or founder fiefdom?

The people living there are not automatically research subjects. Reflection and documentation require consent, boundaries, and the ability to opt out.

## The long-term image

A comfortable, beautiful, accessible place with private rooms; shared kitchens and meals; libraries; studios; workshops; electronics and fabrication space; quiet rooms; outdoor space; gardens; computing infrastructure; guest rooms; communal tables; small seminar rooms; places to get messy; places to be alone; and whiteboards where unexpected conversations can become projects.

It should feel more like **a home with extraordinary shared capacity** than an institution people merely visit for scheduled programming.

The goal is not maximum output.

> **The goal is to create conditions in which curiosity, creativity, autonomy, connection, care, rest, and meaningful work can coexist.**
