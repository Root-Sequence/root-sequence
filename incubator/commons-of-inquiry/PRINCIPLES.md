# Working Principles

These are provisional design commitments, not sacred rules.

## 1. Curiosity before discipline

Questions may cross science, art, philosophy, technology, politics, craft, ecology, history, and lived experience. Disciplines are useful toolkits, not borders.

## 2. Creation is a form of inquiry

Building, drawing, composing, gardening, repairing, performing, programming, writing, modeling, and experimenting can all produce understanding.

## 3. People are not productivity units

Rest, solitude, friendship, care, wandering, unfinished work, and changing one's mind are legitimate parts of life. The commons should not require visible output as proof of worth.

## 4. Agency includes participation, delegation, and refusal

People should be able to join communal life, work independently, ask for help, delegate bounded tasks, or opt out where participation is not genuinely necessary.

## 5. Privacy is part of community

Communal living should not mean permanent availability, surveillance, forced intimacy, or the disappearance of private space.

## 6. Accessibility is infrastructure

Design for different bodies, senses, communication styles, energy levels, neurotypes, schedules, languages, incomes, and levels of technical or academic expertise.

## 7. Shared capacity without compulsory sameness

The commons should make tools, space, knowledge, and relationships more available without demanding one politics, methodology, aesthetic, worldview, or lifestyle.

## 8. Nonhierarchy requires active design

Removing formal titles does not remove power. Track informal status, charisma, expertise, money, ownership, social centrality, and control of information. Make authority visible, bounded, reviewable, and contestable.

## 9. Necessary labor must stay visible

Cooking, cleaning, facilitation, emotional labor, maintenance, accessibility work, administration, conflict work, and repair are part of the institution. Do not hide them behind "community."

Reduce or automate unwanted burdens where appropriate, compensate labor where appropriate, preserve meaningful participation, and inspect who inherits the remaining work.

## 10. Money is a constraint, not the mission

Financial sustainability matters. Financialization does not become the purpose. The commons should resist drifting into a startup incubator, real-estate vehicle, prestige club, or pay-to-belong retreat.

## 11. Epistemic pluralism is not epistemic indifference

Different ways of knowing can coexist while claims remain open to scrutiny. Evidence, provenance, uncertainty, criticism, replication where appropriate, and willingness to revise matter.

## 12. Ideology may be studied and practiced without becoming a loyalty test

Anarchism, socialism, liberalism, commons theory, cooperative economics, indigenous governance, cybernetics, religious community, and other traditions may all offer useful concepts or warnings. No tradition gets exemption from criticism.

## 13. The institution should be forkable

Document practices, governance, failures, costs, architecture, and lessons so others can reproduce, adapt, or reject them. A successful commons should not need exclusive control of its model.

## 14. Scale only after learning

A three-day pilot can teach more than a thirty-page campus plan. Build the smallest real version that can test the next assumption.
