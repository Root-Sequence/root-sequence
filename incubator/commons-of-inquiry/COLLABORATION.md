# Collaboration

This project should be easy to enter before it is polished.

## Useful contributions

Contributors can:

- add a historical or contemporary precedent;
- document a failure mode;
- propose a pilot format;
- challenge an assumption;
- add accessibility requirements;
- model costs or ownership structures;
- contribute spatial / architectural ideas;
- document cooperative governance patterns;
- suggest research methods;
- contribute art, visual language, fiction, diagrams, or speculative prototypes;
- report experiences from intentional communities, research institutes, artist residencies, hackerspaces, co-ops, communes, or similar places.

## Evidence and status

When adding material, distinguish among:

- **documented fact** — backed by a source;
- **participant report** — lived experience, identified or anonymized appropriately;
- **interpretation** — a reading of evidence;
- **design proposal** — something we think might work;
- **open question** — something not yet resolved;
- **speculation** — intentionally imaginative exploration.

Do not turn a compelling anecdote into a universal rule.

## Disagreement

Critique ideas and structures without requiring social conformity. If a disagreement is substantive, preserve the disagreement rather than smoothing it into fake consensus.

## AI-assisted contributions

AI may help search, compare, organize, draft, model, translate, or critique. Human contributors should remain able to identify what claims rely on evidence, what was generated or transformed, and what decisions they actually endorse.
