## Why this belongs here

The [precedent map](README.md) already asks about participation, environment, maintenance, and autonomy. [Pilot 0](../pilot/PILOT-0.md) already includes visible work surfaces, protected unscheduled time, consent, and quiet space. The new trails should sharpen those questions, not create a second institutional plan.

Starting from the [alternative-institutions](../../../research/reading-trails/alternative-institutions-futures.md) and [HCI](../../../research/reading-trails/human-factors-hci-accessibility.md) trails suggests a bounded question:

> Does making work visible help people discover one another's questions without making solitude, privacy, or non-participation harder?

This is a proposed question, not an established benefit of shared spaces.

## Proposed addition to a future Pilot 0 review

Keep Pilot 0's proposed scale and safeguards unchanged. If participants agree to investigate this question, offer an optional way to display a question or unfinished artifact alongside an equally legitimate private/offline alternative. Do not require public work, demonstrations, permanent records, or an explanation for opting out.

Ask about discoverability, unwanted interruptions, sensory or access barriers, ability to withdraw material, and the work of setting up, explaining, maintaining, and tidying the shared area. Let people comment privately or decline. Do not collect identifiable interaction maps or record conversations by default.

Before any pilot, agree who can see the notes, how long they remain, how correction/withdrawal works, and whether the activity is merely event feedback or a research study requiring additional review. Participation in a gathering is not automatic consent to either documentation or research.

## Compare explanations, not institutions' reputations

| Possible observation | Alternatives to investigate |
| --- | --- |
| More conversations occur near visible work | The display helped; a facilitator prompted them; the activity was novel; the participants already knew each other. |
| Some people use the quiet area | They valued solitude; other areas were inaccessible; social demands were exhausting; the activity was simply irrelevant to them. |
| Few people display work | Privacy preference; unclear invitation; access barriers; no current artifact; no desire to participate. Do not score this as failure. |
| The area seems effortless to use | Someone may be absorbing preparation, explanation, emotional effort, or cleanup. Ask rather than infer. |

This is not a randomized comparison. Do not claim causal effectiveness or generalize from a small self-selected gathering. A useful result may be that the proposed arrangement adds pressure or that a much simpler setup meets the need.
