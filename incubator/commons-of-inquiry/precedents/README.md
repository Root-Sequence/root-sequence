# Precedent Map

The goal is not to find one perfect ancestor. It is to study **different institutional species** and extract mechanisms, tradeoffs, and failure modes.

## Initial cases to investigate

### Residential experimental education / art

- Black Mountain College
- Bauhaus and related experimental schools
- artist colonies and residential art schools
- Akademie Schloss Solitude
- ZK/U Berlin

Questions: What made cross-disciplinary encounters productive? How were residents selected? Who performed maintenance? Why did institutions change or close?

### Complexity / advanced inquiry institutes

- Santa Fe Institute
- Institute for Advanced Study
- Complexity Science Hub Vienna
- independent institutes and visiting-fellow models

Questions: What conditions create spontaneous intellectual exchange? Which benefits depend on academic prestige or funding?

### Commons / cooperative / anarchist spaces

- anarchist social centers
- worker and housing cooperatives
- community land trusts
- Omni Commons
- long-running intentional communities
- Institute for Social Ecology

Questions: How is authority distributed? How are property, money, labor, conflict, and entry handled?

### Hackerspaces / makerspaces

- Noisebridge
- hackerspaces with nonhierarchical governance
- community workshops and tool libraries

Questions: What enables permissionless making? What creates exclusion despite formal openness? How are tools and maintenance governed?

### Temporary intentional communities

- Edge City / popup villages
- unconferences
- temporary autonomous zones of learning / making
- residential summer schools

Questions: Which forms of intensity create connection, and which depend on novelty or short duration?

### Historical salons / monasteries / communes

- Monte Verità
- salons and correspondence networks
- monastic scholarly communities
- utopian and socialist communes
- scientific field stations

Questions: What can be learned from shared rhythm, solitude, hospitality, and durable place — without importing coercive structures?

### Cross-disciplinary industrial labs

- Bell Labs and other research laboratories

Questions: What did physical proximity, technical infrastructure, autonomy, and long time horizons enable? Which conditions depended on corporate monopoly, wealth, or employment hierarchy?

## Research template

For each precedent, document:

1. purpose and historical context;
2. who could participate and who could not;
3. physical environment;
4. governance;
5. property / ownership;
6. funding and resident economics;
7. shared labor;
8. privacy and autonomy;
9. disciplinary or ideological boundaries;
10. accessibility;
11. conflict and failure modes;
12. what participants actually valued;
13. what ended or transformed the institution;
14. mechanisms potentially transferable to a new commons;
15. mechanisms that should not be copied.

## Evidence boundary

A famous success story may hide exclusion, unpaid labor, conflict, exploitation, survivorship bias, mythology, or conditions that cannot be reproduced. Precedent research should actively look for criticism and failure, not only celebratory histories.
