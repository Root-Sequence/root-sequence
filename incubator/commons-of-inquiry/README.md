# Commons of Inquiry & Creation

**Status:** Seed / incubation draft  
**Target home:** `Root-Sequence/commons-of-inquiry` as a standalone repository  
**Current home:** Temporary bootstrap seed inside `Root-Sequence/root-sequence` until the standalone repository exists.

## What this is

A project to design, test, and eventually build a **residential commons for inquiry, creation, experimentation, and ordinary life**.

The commons is not only a research institute, university, artist colony, hackerspace, commune, think tank, makerspace, or social center. It draws from all of those traditions while treating none as the default.

Science, art, philosophy, technology, politics, history, craft, ecology, writing, repair, social experimentation, and everyday life are all legitimate ways of investigating and engaging with the world.

The project begins with a simple question:

> **What conditions help curious people live well, think freely, make things, learn from one another, and pursue worthwhile questions together?**

The long-term dream may look like a campus. The near-term work is much smaller: understand the design space, find people, test gatherings, run temporary residencies, learn from failures, and only then decide whether a permanent physical commons makes sense.

## What it is not

This project should not quietly become:

- a startup accelerator;
- a credentialing institution;
- a founder-centered community;
- an ideological purity project;
- a productivity commune;
- an academic prestige machine;
- a retreat available only to wealthy people;
- a place where communal language hides unpaid or unequal labor;
- a place where "openness" eliminates privacy or the right to be alone.

These are design risks to investigate, not slogans that make the risks disappear.

## Working path

1. **Map the design space** — historical and living precedents, successes, failures, governance, economics, architecture, culture.
2. **Build the network** — small interdisciplinary gatherings, salons, build nights, reading groups, art nights, shared meals.
3. **Pilot the commons** — short residential experiments with explicit consent, reflection, accessibility, and documentation.
4. **Run a temporary residency** — weeks rather than days; enough time for ordinary life and collaboration to emerge.
5. **Test a persistent house** — if the earlier experiments justify it.
6. **Grow a campus or network of nodes** — only if the institution actually benefits from greater scale.

## Relationship to Root Sequence

This belongs in the Root Sequence ecosystem because it turns recurring questions about **coherence, agency, commons, accessibility, collective intelligence, institutions, technology, and alternative futures** into a physical and social design problem.

Root Sequence should not govern the eventual community by default. If a real commons develops, its residents and participants will need legitimate authority over the institution they actually inhabit.

## Evidence boundary

Most of this repository begins as **institutional design hypotheses**. Historical examples can inform design, but analogy is not evidence that a practice will work in a new context. Pilots should be used to test assumptions before scaling them.
