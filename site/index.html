Research for a more coherent world

Root Sequence is an open research project about the connections between people, technology, institutions, and the systems we build.

We examine problems that don’t fit neatly within one field. What looks like a technical limitation might also be a question of ownership, coordination, access, or whose knowledge counts. The work follows those connections into practical projects and imagined futures, developing ideas that others can examine, question, and build on.

Root Sequence is an independent project started by Rae Lovejoy. Begin with the everyday example, read the current research, or explore selected related projects.

02 About Root Sequence

About this project.

Root Sequence studies how intelligence, technology, and social systems shape the conditions people live in.

Intelligence here includes human reasoning, machine intelligence, and the ways groups and institutions gather information, learn, and make decisions.

A service can meet its targets and still be difficult to use. A useful technology can depend on rules that make it hard to repair. Looking at either in isolation leaves part of the problem unexplained.

What the project investigates

The research follows connections between technical choices and social conditions: what a tool assumes about its users, who can change it, whose needs are missed, and what happens when it fails. It also revisits alternatives that were tried, abandoned, or never developed.

From questions to projects

The work includes research notes, essays, design proposals, and experiments. Sources, assumptions, and unresolved questions stay with the work so people can examine how a conclusion was reached and where it might fail.

Some questions lead to community tools or computing designs; others inform Coherent World and No One Noticed . Findings and problems from those projects can feed back into the research. An imagined result in a story is kept separate from evidence that something works.

Open work, separate responsibilities

Root Sequence is intended to grow through contributions instead of depending on one person. How decisions and long-term responsibility can be shared is still being developed.

Each project keeps its own permissions and boundaries. Contributing to one does not require agreement with, or responsibility for, the others.

03 An everyday example

A repairable device needs more than screws

Imagine a phone designed so its battery and screen can be replaced. The parts are removable, but replacements are not sold to the public. Repair instructions are restricted, and the software rejects components installed outside an authorized shop.

The device is technically repairable, but the system around it prevents many people from repairing it.

A hypothetical example for discussion, not an assessment of a particular product
In the device The battery and screen can be removed.
Around the repair Parts, instructions, and software access are restricted.
For the owner A possible repair can still be out of reach.

A repairable design is not necessarily a repairable system.

Here, coherence means looking beyond the object itself. Parts, instructions, software, ownership, cost, and the right to make changes all affect whether repair is actually possible.

05 Other possibilities

What could be different?

Some work begins with a possibility rather than a prediction: another way to travel, share knowledge, use technology, or spend a day.

We don’t need to invent the future from scratch. It was here all along until it was abandoned.

From Abandoned Futures ↗Opens in a new tab

That line starts an investigation. Which ideas were proposed? Why did they stop? What would have to change for another attempt? An attractive proposal still needs scrutiny.

How a possibility becomes an inquiry

  1. 01

    Recover the proposal

    Find what was actually proposed, who was involved, what evidence survives, and which conditions made it possible.

  2. 02

    Trace what happened

    Distinguish technical failure from political blockage, neglect, enclosure, coordination problems, or a good reason to stop.

  3. 03

    Test it now

    Ask who benefits, who carries the work, cost, and risk, what would need to change, and what evidence would count against trying again.

Fiction gives us a place to explore how a different outcome might feel. A scene can raise a question without proving that its imagined solution works.

06 Selected related projects

Ideas, in different forms.

This is a selected group of projects that share questions while keeping their own purposes, permissions, and decisions. The full project map includes the wider ecosystem.

Coherent World Speculative design · Private development
Possible arrangements for society and everyday life, considered together. The novel No One Noticed draws on this work, but Coherent World can explore possibilities beyond it.
No One Noticed Novel in progress · Public site not available yet
A science-fiction novel about people living through changes brought about by an advanced AI. Manuscript extracts will wait until they are ready for readers.
Coherent Computing Computing research · No public release
Design work on an operating system that adapts to different people, devices, and uses. There is no released system to download yet.
Community Infrastructure Practical design · Private development
Tools for sharing needs, coordinating help, and organizing activities within communities.
Being Human(e): An Incomplete Guide to Practical field guide · Public website not built yet
A developing field guide grounded in ordinary human experience and the repeated choice to be humane.
Museum of Ordinary Life Independent archive · Public website
Records of everyday objects, places, and personal accounts. Contributors’ permissions remain separate from the fictional Museum in the novel.
Root Sequence Wiki Project reference · Public website
A reference for project names, recurring terms, relationships, and where the longer work lives.

07 Take part

Take part in Root Sequence.

The project welcomes questions, sources, corrections, translations, alternative explanations, and tests of its ideas. For now, participation happens through public GitHub channels.

Discuss questions and ideas

Join Root Sequence Discussions to raise a research question, share an idea, or talk about a possible contribution. Discussions are public and require a GitHub account to post.
