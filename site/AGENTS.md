# Website writing instructions

These pages are for someone who has never heard of these projects. Start with what the project is, who is making it, what is available now, and where to read next. Explain specialised terms when they first appear. Keep established URLs even when a visible label changes.

## Writing reference

Use Louis Rossmann's [no_ai_slop_writing_rules](https://github.com/realrossmanngroup/no_ai_slop_writing_rules), particularly the [editing rules](https://github.com/realrossmanngroup/no_ai_slop_writing_rules/blob/main/skills/no-ai-slop/SKILL.md) and [phrase reference](https://github.com/realrossmanngroup/no_ai_slop_writing_rules/blob/main/skills/no-ai-slop/references/ai-writing-detection.md). Rae has repeatedly requested this reference.

Apply its editing guidance to Rae's project voice. Don't imitate Rossmann's personal experiences, manufacture numerical detail, force ampersands, or add hostility. Keep uncertainty when the evidence warrants it. A word list is a house-style aid, not a detector of who wrote something.

Use ordinary verbs and natural contractions. Cut stock contrast slogans, staged revelations, vague declarations of importance, intensifiers, filler transitions, em dashes in new website prose, and headings that conceal their subject. Vary paragraph length where the material calls for it. Don't replace every sentence with a fragment or force every page into three sections.

Keep meaning, sources, and real qualifications. Never invent a quote, outcome, milestone, statistic, or lived experience to make the writing sound human. Preserve quotations. Identify fictional examples as fiction. Keep AI-assistance disclosures; editing the voice doesn't change authorship or review status.

## Page structure

Put the subject and useful reading links first. Keep dates, development stages, provenance, and technical downloads in labelled details sections below the text. State draft-fiction status in plain language beside the scene. Native details must remain keyboard-accessible without JavaScript. Keep sources available and links meaningful.

Read the pages, not just the source. Check the introduction without assuming knowledge of other projects. Run `test_site.py` after edits. Its phrase checks cover selected recurring mistakes, not every rule in the reference. Inspect both narrow and wide layouts. Save a dated change record and keep README links current.

Saving source, approving prose, adopting story material, and publishing a live site are separate actions. Never fabricate an approval or expose private source. Export only the generated site files.
