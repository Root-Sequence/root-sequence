# ⚖️ Ideology — Stories That Organize Power

Ideology is not only a list of beliefs people consciously hold.

It includes the stories, categories, assumptions, aesthetics, institutions, and descriptions of “reality” through which power becomes legitimate, ordinary, invisible, or inevitable.

This folder examines ideologies that justify hierarchy, enclosure, extraction, exclusion, elite rule, technological domination, and authoritarian control. It also studies allegories and cultural works that make those structures easier to perceive.

## Purpose

Use this folder to examine:

- how political arrangements become common sense;
- how hierarchy is described as nature, competence, order, maturity, or realism;
- how domination hides inside property, management, expertise, safety, efficiency, or technological inevitability;
- how crises are used to narrow participation and expand elite authority;
- how myths of decline identify enemies and promise restoration;
- how institutions produce knowledge that reflects real evidence, professional incentives, class interests, blind spots, or combinations of them;
- how ideologies travel through culture, interfaces, organizations, technology, and ordinary language;
- how a critique can notice a real pattern while turning that pattern into conspiracy, scapegoat, or total explanation;
- how liberatory projects can reproduce domination through certainty, expertise, purity, or compulsory unity.

## Ideology is not a synonym for “ideas I dislike”

Every political project—including Root Sequence—contains assumptions, values, frames, and preferred interpretations.

Critical ideological analysis should therefore ask:

- What does this framework make visible?
- What does it hide?
- Which facts would challenge it?
- Which people become subjects, objects, enemies, experts, or saviors?
- What kind of authority does it justify?
- What material interests and institutions carry it?
- How does it explain disagreement?
- What happens when its preferred order fails?
- Can it revise itself without treating revision as betrayal?

The goal is not to stand outside ideology. It is to make framing, evidence, values, and power more visible and contestable.

## Keep adjacent traditions distinct

Fascism, neoreaction, conservatism, libertarianism, neoliberalism, nationalism, monarchism, techno-authoritarianism, eugenics, accelerationism, and elite theory may overlap in particular texts or projects.

They are not interchangeable labels.

A useful analysis identifies:

- the exact text or actor;
- the proposed social order;
- the subject entitled to rule;
- the institution that carries the project;
- the role of mass politics;
- the role of property and markets;
- the role of race, nation, gender, disability, class, and citizenship;
- the strategy for obtaining or preserving power;
- similarities and differences from neighboring traditions.

Naming a family resemblance should begin analysis, not replace it.

## Epistemic standards

Many files in this folder began as short exploratory notes. Future revisions should distinguish:

- **PRIMARY CLAIM** — what a thinker, movement, institution, or artwork explicitly says;
- **OBSERVED STRUCTURE** — documented organization, policy, funding, behavior, or relationship;
- **INTERPRETATION** — the project's reading of that material;
- **GENEALOGY** — proposed historical or intellectual relationship;
- **ANALOGY** — useful comparison that may not imply direct influence;
- **POLITICAL USE** — how later actors adopt, transform, or popularize an idea;
- **NORMATIVE CRITIQUE** — why the project judges a structure harmful;
- **OPEN QUESTION** — uncertainty or disagreement requiring more research.

Every serious claim should eventually include:

- exact source;
- date;
- context;
- quotation or precise paraphrase;
- competing interpretation where relevant;
- confidence and known limits.

Do not cite a critic's summary as though it were the primary text. Do not let an ideology's self-description settle its effects.

## Root Sequence reflexivity

Root Sequence should apply the same scrutiny to itself.

Potential internal risks include:

- treating recurring patterns as universal law;
- turning emergence into mysticism;
- treating coherence as automatic goodness;
- interpreting anti-hierarchy as absence of power;
- replacing evidence with moral confidence;
- allowing one framework to govern unrelated domains;
- treating a speculative world as proof of a political program;
- allowing Auryn's competence to become a justification for benevolent sovereignty;
- confusing connectivity among projects with one total theory of everything.

A framework becomes more trustworthy when it documents the conditions under which it may be wrong.
