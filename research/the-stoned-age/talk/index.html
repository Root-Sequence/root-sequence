01 / 12 · 0:00–1:30

The Stoned Age

Altered states, curiosity, and the long history of making meaning.

Editorial framing

Speaker notes

Open with “Please turn to page 666.” Pause. Explain that the title is an invitation to investigate, not a claim that drugs explain everything. Introduce Root Sequence as coordinator and this as an AI-assisted draft.

02 / 12 · 1:30–3:30

Four questions, not one

What happened? What does it mean? What supports it? Who gets authority?

Editorial framing

Speaker notes

Use the fictional dream example. Ask the audience to notice how quickly “it felt true” becomes “it is true.” Do not ask people to disclose drug use or intimate experiences.

03 / 12 · 3:30–5:30

Archaeology cannot interview the smoke

Material traces give us local evidence.

[S01, S02]

Speaker notes

Briefly contrast Arad and the Pamirs. Emphasize residues and context; neither supplies the origin of religion. Show conceptual windows, not fabricated excavation imagery.

04 / 12 · 5:30–7:00

An object is not a recipe

A bundle and a hair sample answer different questions.

[S03, S04]

Speaker notes

Contrast the South American bundle with Menorca hair. Keep exposure, preparation and interpretation distinct. Refer detailed analytical claims to the paper.

05 / 12 · 7:00–9:00

The ape needs more evidence

Exposure → effects → transmission → comparative explanation.

[S09]

Speaker notes

Explain the missing causal chain in the strongest stoned-ape claim. Distinguish temporary cognitive change from biological evolution. Present the published hypothesis as a hypothesis.

06 / 12 · 9:00–11:00

The idea feels brilliant

Generation and evaluation need separate measures.

[S07]

Speaker notes

Mention the distinction between reported insight and task performance. Avoid claiming all creativity declines or improves. Invite an ordinary example of an idea that improved after revision.

07 / 12 · 11:00–13:00

Meaning is already something

Personal significance is not a cosmic certificate.

[S06]

Speaker notes

Keep reports of meaning distinct from metaphysical verification. Explain that a physiological account need not dismiss personal importance. Do not describe the research as proving religion either true or false.

08 / 12 · 13:00–15:00

Nobody makes meaning alone

Stories have audiences, teachers and critics.

[S10, S11]

Speaker notes

Introduce the editorial experience-to-revision map. Context matters; it is not a universal sequence. Ask who is allowed to correct a shared account.

09 / 12 · 15:00–17:00

Who owns the explanation?

Respect includes refusal and disagreement.

[S14]

Speaker notes

Discuss the difference between acknowledging testimony and authorizing decisions over others. Introduce IPCI as one organization with a situated position, not a representative of all Indigenous peoples.

10 / 12 · 17:00–19:00

The oracle has autocomplete

Agreement is not independent corroboration.

[S15]

Speaker notes

Define the AI analogy narrowly as evaluation of compelling interpretations. Cite sycophancy research as bounded evidence. Do not equate AI outputs with human consciousness or psychedelic states.

11 / 12 · 19:00–21:00

What would change our minds?

Better sources. Better contrasts. Serious objections.

Editorial framing

Speaker notes

Name the next research steps: full archaeological extraction, study appraisal, cultural scholarship and a bounded AI question. Explain that no human experiment has been run.

12 / 12 · 21:00–25:00

Keep the question open

The universe has not supplied a foreword.

Editorial framing

Speaker notes

Close with the project invitation. Use the final four minutes for discussion: what separates meaningful experience from shared knowledge? What would a respectful correction look like? Offer paper and source links.
