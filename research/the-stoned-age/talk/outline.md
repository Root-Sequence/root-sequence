# The Stoned Age — 25-minute talk

v0.1.2 · 24 October 2026. 21 minutes speaking plus 4 minutes discussion. AI-assisted draft; review pending.

## 1. The Stoned Age (0:00–1:30)

**On screen:** Altered states, curiosity, and the long history of making meaning.

**Speaker notes:** Open with “Please turn to page 666.” Pause. Explain that the title is an invitation to investigate, not a claim that drugs explain everything. Introduce Root Sequence as coordinator and this as an AI-assisted draft.

**Visual:** [Conceptual illustration](../assets/cover.svg).

**Sources:** Editorial framing

## 2. Four questions, not one (1:30–3:30)

**On screen:** What happened? What does it mean? What supports it? Who gets authority?

**Speaker notes:** Use the fictional dream example. Ask the audience to notice how quickly “it felt true” becomes “it is true.” Do not ask people to disclose drug use or intimate experiences.

**Visual:** [Conceptual illustration](../assets/steps.svg).

**Sources:** Editorial framing

## 3. Archaeology cannot interview the smoke (3:30–5:30)

**On screen:** Material traces give us local evidence.

**Speaker notes:** Briefly contrast Arad and the Pamirs. Emphasize residues and context; neither supplies the origin of religion. Show conceptual windows, not fabricated excavation imagery.

**Visual:** [Conceptual illustration](../assets/windows.svg).

**Sources:** S01, S02

## 4. An object is not a recipe (5:30–7:00)

**On screen:** A bundle and a hair sample answer different questions.

**Speaker notes:** Contrast the South American bundle with Menorca hair. Keep exposure, preparation and interpretation distinct. Refer detailed analytical claims to the paper.

**Visual:** [Conceptual illustration](../assets/windows.svg).

**Sources:** S03, S04

## 5. The ape needs more evidence (7:00–9:00)

**On screen:** Exposure → effects → transmission → comparative explanation.

**Speaker notes:** Explain the missing causal chain in the strongest stoned-ape claim. Distinguish temporary cognitive change from biological evolution. Present the published hypothesis as a hypothesis.

**Visual:** [Conceptual illustration](../assets/ladder.svg).

**Sources:** S09

## 6. The idea feels brilliant (9:00–11:00)

**On screen:** Generation and evaluation need separate measures.

**Speaker notes:** Distinguish acute subjective insight from lower task performance. At seven days, self-rated novelty increased while convergent thinking remained lower than placebo. New to the participant does not mean independently verified originality. Avoid general cognitive-upgrade claims.

**Visual:** [Conceptual illustration](../assets/split.svg).

**Sources:** S07

## 7. Meaning is already something (11:00–13:00)

**On screen:** Personal significance is not a cosmic certificate.

**Speaker notes:** Keep reports of meaning distinct from metaphysical verification. Explain that a physiological account need not dismiss personal importance. Do not describe the research as proving religion either true or false. Note the small screened sample, supported setting and acute fear alongside significance.

**Visual:** [Conceptual illustration](../assets/orbit.svg).

**Sources:** S06

## 8. Nobody makes meaning alone (13:00–15:00)

**On screen:** Stories have audiences, teachers and critics.

**Speaker notes:** Introduce the editorial experience-to-revision map. Context matters; it is not a universal sequence. Ask who is allowed to correct a shared account.

**Visual:** [Conceptual illustration](../assets/network.svg).

**Sources:** S10, S11

## 9. Who owns the explanation? (15:00–17:00)

**On screen:** Respect includes refusal and disagreement.

**Speaker notes:** Discuss the difference between acknowledging testimony and authorizing decisions over others. Introduce IPCI as one organization with a situated position, not a representative of all Indigenous peoples.

**Visual:** [Conceptual illustration](../assets/boundary.svg).

**Sources:** S14

## 10. The oracle has autocomplete (17:00–19:00)

**On screen:** Agreement is not independent corroboration.

**Speaker notes:** Define the AI analogy narrowly as evaluation of compelling interpretations. Cite sycophancy research as bounded evidence. Do not equate AI outputs with human consciousness or psychedelic states. The cited evaluation is the 2023 version and does not describe every current model.

**Visual:** [Conceptual illustration](../assets/mirror.svg).

**Sources:** S15

## 11. What would change our minds? (19:00–21:00)

**On screen:** Better sources. Better contrasts. Serious objections.

**Speaker notes:** Name the next research steps: full archaeological extraction, study appraisal, cultural scholarship and a bounded AI question. Explain that no human experiment has been run.

**Visual:** [Conceptual illustration](../assets/steps.svg).

**Sources:** Editorial framing

## 12. Keep the question open (21:00–25:00)

**On screen:** The universe has not supplied a foreword.

**Speaker notes:** Close with the project invitation. Use the final four minutes for discussion: what separates meaningful experience from shared knowledge? What would a respectful correction look like? Offer paper and source links.

**Visual:** [Conceptual illustration](../assets/cover.svg).

**Sources:** Editorial framing
