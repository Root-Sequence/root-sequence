The Stoned Age

Altered States, Human Curiosity, and the Long History of Making Meaning

Living research paper · v0.1.1 · 14 October 2026 · AI-assisted narrative synthesis · author and disciplinary review pending

Companion provocation: How Drugs Explain the Universe. The title names a temptation this project investigates; it is not its scientific conclusion.

Abstract

Altered states offer a productive route into questions about curiosity, religious experience, cultural transmission and epistemic authority. They also invite overreach: evidence that a substance was used in a particular setting can become a story about the origin of religion; a striking present-day experience can become a theory of human evolution. This paper separates chemical evidence, reports of experience, causal explanations and judgments of truth. It brings selected archaeological findings into conversation with experimental psychology, cultural anthropology, cultural-evolution research and AI research. Its central proposal is an inquiry: under what conditions do unusual experiences become useful questions, shared meanings or unwarranted authority? The strongest available claims in this initial source set concern bounded practices and effects, not a single drug-driven origin of intelligence. A comparison with AI concerns the social evaluation of compelling interpretations, not shared consciousness or pharmacology. The project supplies an explicit claims ledger, revision conditions and an agenda for further research. Root Sequence provides coordination and accessible synthesis; it does not claim specialist authority or a novel theory of mind.

1. The joke is the doorway

“Now open to page 666 as we recite …” is an excellent beginning for a fictional book. It is a less reliable archaeological method. The humor works because a grand explanatory story is already waiting: perhaps the universe became comprehensible when somebody ate the wrong thing, or the right thing, depending on the subsequent publishing contract.

A serious inquiry begins by resisting the convenience of that story. “Drugs explain the universe” contains at least three different propositions. A substance might change a person's experience of the universe. That experience might influence the explanations they construct. Those explanations might or might not accurately describe the world. The first proposition does not entail the third.

The same distinction prevents an equally lazy dismissal. Explaining an experience partly through physiology does not show that it was meaningless, that the person is foolish, or that every thought produced during it was false. Origin and justification are different questions. We can take a report seriously while examining its interpretation.

The working thesis is therefore deliberately conditional: altered states may contribute to meaning-making by changing what is noticed, felt, remembered or proposed; their consequences depend on how people interpret, test, transmit and govern those changes. This is an organizing hypothesis, not an empirical result established by this paper.

2. Scope, method and definitions

This first edition is a targeted narrative synthesis. Searches on 14 October 2026 sought primary archaeological reports, experimental studies, cultural research, a relevant community position and primary AI research. Queries covered Arad cannabis, Pamir residues, Menorca hair, the South American ritual bundle, psychedelic creativity, mystical-type reports, REBUS, psychedelic evolution, cultural spiritual experience, collective innovation and language-model sycophancy. Sources were included for a specific claim or methodological contrast. Neither comprehensive database coverage nor a reproducible systematic-review screening process was performed. No experiments, statistical synthesis or independent peer review were conducted.

“Altered state” is a comparative description, not a single natural kind. We use it for substantial changes in experience relative to a person's ordinary waking condition. Sleep, absorption, illness and drug effects should not be collapsed into one mechanism. Even “ordinary” needs context: whose baseline, measured when, with which language and expectations?

“Curiosity” means motivation to investigate or resolve uncertainty; “creativity” concerns generating and evaluating possibilities; “intelligence” has multiple contested operationalizations; “meaning” can refer to personal significance, social interpretation or semantic content. A finding about one cannot silently substitute for all four. In particular, a creativity task does not measure the evolutionary origin of intelligence.

Evidence labels in this suite have distinct jobs. Finding reports what a cited study detected or measured, within its design. Interpretation offers an account of what a finding means. Hypothesis proposes a testable relationship. Speculation explores possibilities without adequate discriminating evidence. Normative position states what we think people ought to do. These labels are not a numerical ladder of certainty: an interpretation may be strong, while an empirical result may be fragile.

3. Four archaeological windows, not a universal timeline

At Arad, cannabinoid residues on one altar and frankincense-related residues on another support a specific account of substances burned in a Judahite shrine. This materially improves on guessing from a suggestive image. It does not establish that Judaism originated in cannabis experiences, identify a worshipper's thoughts, or settle a disputed word in scripture.S01

At Jirzankal in the Pamirs, chemical residues in wooden braziers support cannabis burning in a funerary context around 500 BCE. The authors discuss material with comparatively strong psychoactive potential. The evidence concerns a place, period and practice; the paper's title should not be read as proof that this was the first such use anywhere.S02

A roughly thousand-year-old South American ritual bundle provides evidence of multiple psychotropic plant compounds. Its significance lies in materially documented access to such substances. A collection of compounds is not automatically a recipe, proof of simultaneous ingestion, or an unbroken genealogy of a present-day ceremony.S03

At Es Càrritx in Menorca, analysis of ancient human hair detected ephedrine, atropine and scopolamine. This is closer to evidence of bodily exposure than a suggestively shaped container. Funerary context helps interpret the find, but the identity, intentions and exact experience of the people involved are not recovered by the chemical assay.S04

These cases illuminate different inferential steps. Plant presence, preparation, consumption, physiological effect, remembered experience and social meaning require different evidence. A residue can be chemically persuasive while its religious interpretation remains uncertain. Conversely, the absence of preserved residue cannot establish that nobody used a rapidly degrading substance. Preservation is a filter, not a census.

We should ask of every archaeological story: what was sampled; what was detected; how was contamination addressed; how was the sample dated; which alternatives fit the context; and where does the investigator move from measurement to reconstruction? Our collection is deliberately small and geographically uneven. It cannot support prevalence estimates, a global chronology or ranking cultures by sophistication.

4. “Drugs” is too large a drawer

Classic psychedelics such as psilocin, LSD and mescaline share important serotonergic pharmacology, especially involvement of the 5-HT2A receptor. That does not make their time courses, effects or risks identical.S05 Cannabis is not a synonym for a classic psychedelic. Nor should tropane alkaloids in the Menorca case be casually relabeled as psilocybin-like agents. The assemblage includes compounds with different pharmacological actions.S04

A responsible case record should name the substance or plant where known, the uncertainty of identification, the preparation if evidenced, the route if evidenced, and the social setting. It should distinguish a modern isolated compound from a variable botanical preparation. This paper supplies no recipes, dosing protocols or treatment recommendations.

The cultural equivalent of the same mistake is using “shamanism” as a universal container. A Judahite shrine, an Andean bundle, a Native American Church practice, an Amazonian tradition and a modern research session are not interchangeable locations for one timeless human event. A community's own categories may not correspond to an outsider's contrast between recreational, medical and religious use.

For that reason, this edition declines to retell specific Mazatec, Amazonian or Vedic traditions without adequate contextual sources and specialist checking. Soma identification, Eleusinian preparations, mushroom interpretations of rock art and universal drug-origin stories remain research questions here, not historical findings. A missing chapter is preferable to an invented bridge.

5. What would “stoned ape” have to establish?

The maximal claim is not merely that ancestors encountered psychoactive organisms. It is that repeated exposure made a consequential causal contribution to the biological evolution of distinctive human cognition. Those claims are separated by several demanding steps.

First, exposure must be established for relevant populations and periods. Second, the relevant effects must have changed behavior under those conditions. Third, the effects must have affected survival or reproduction, or a clearly specified process of cultural transmission. Fourth, the proposed pathway must explain something beyond competing accounts. A change during one person's lifetime is not, by itself, a heritable evolutionary change.

Rodríguez Arce and Winkelman offer an explicit hypothesis about psychedelics, sociality and human evolution. Their synthesis helps formulate possible mechanisms, but acknowledges the lack of incontrovertible direct evidence of ancient psychedelic mushroom ingestion. It should be read as hypothesis construction, not archaeological confirmation.S09

The strongest objection to the overclaim is consequently inferential. Modern effects do not establish ancient exposure; ancient exposure would not alone establish selection; selection would not show that the effect explains language or intelligence generally. The evidence assembled here does not meet that chain. This is not a proof that every possible contribution was zero. It is a reason to withhold the grand conclusion.

A narrower historical question is more tractable: once particular communities had particular practices, how did those practices change learning, interpretation or social relationships? Even then, we need contrasts. Communities differ in ecology, kinship, institutions and outside pressures. “They used a drug and had elaborate myths” supplies no counterfactual. Myths also need transmission, audiences, memory, teaching and authority.

6. Curiosity, creativity and the feeling of discovery

Mason and colleagues' placebo-controlled study of 60 healthy participants found a useful dissociation: acute reports of creative insight increased while deliberate task-based creativity decreased; at seven days, participants reported more ideas new to themselves, while convergent-thinking performance remained reduced. This self-rated novelty is not independently established originality. The result is time- and task-specific, not a general cognitive upgrade.S07

This contrast matters because a person may feel that an idea is important before being able to evaluate it. That observation supplies a research question rather than a verdict against inspiration. How well does confidence track accuracy? Which ideas survive later testing? Are useful questions produced even when immediate answers are wrong? Are outcomes different when people have time, trusted critics and a safe opportunity to revise?

We propose separating generation from evaluation in future studies. A participant might first produce questions, then revisit them later; independent assessors could rate novelty, clarity and answerability without knowing the condition. Accuracy, confidence, emotional salience and usefulness should be recorded separately. Any drug study would require qualified investigators, ethical review and an appropriate clinical research setting. Root Sequence has not designed or authorized such an experiment.

An accessible non-drug first step is a literature audit: extract how each study defines creativity, records expectations, handles attrition and chooses follow-up times. The result may be that apparently contradictory findings tested different constructs. That would be a valuable correction even if it yields no dramatic new theory.

7. Experience, mechanism and truth

Griffiths and colleagues studied 18 screened healthy adults in supported sessions and found persistent reports of personal and spiritual significance. Acute extreme anxiety or fear also occurred in this supported study. These are consequential reports about experience. They do not verify the cosmological content of that experience, and the sample and setting limit generalization.S06

REBUS offers a mechanistic proposal involving relaxed precision of high-level prior beliefs under psychedelics. It is one theoretical account of altered cognition, not proof that the brain has uncovered an ordinarily hidden universal reality.S08

Three levels should remain visible. Phenomenology asks what happened for the person. Mechanism asks how an experience was produced. Epistemology asks what, if anything, the experience warrants believing. No level automatically settles the others. A person may learn that a relationship matters to them without gaining privileged access to physics. A moving experience can motivate a good scientific question without itself answering it.

Likewise, emotional intensity does not determine ethical correctness. Feeling interconnected does not specify how to resolve a conflict about land, consent or scarce resources. Those questions require accountable deliberation. The project treats wonder as compatible with checking, and checking as compatible with respect.

8. From private experience to collective meaning

Luhrmann and colleagues found relationships among cultural models of mind, absorption and reports of spiritual presence across multiple settings. This work directs attention to interpretation and culturally shaped experience without requiring a drug-origin explanation.S10

Muthukrishna and Henrich develop an account of innovation through collective cultural processes, including recombination and transmission. This provides a competent comparison for stories centered entirely on a chemically inspired individual.S11 An idea becomes cumulative knowledge only if somebody can retain, communicate, evaluate and improve it. The machinery of attention and transmission belongs in the explanation.

Our proposed analytical sequence is: experience; report; interpretation; circulation; stabilization; challenge or revision. It is an editorial map, not a discovered universal law. The arrows can run backward: inherited stories may shape expectations before an experience occurs. Many experiences never enter a shared story at all.

A memorable narrative may spread because it is useful, emotionally compelling, institutionally supported, or difficult to contradict safely. Those are alternative hypotheses, not a conclusion that all religion is manipulation. Religious life also includes practices, relationships, obligations and disagreement that a focus on visionary events could obscure. This edition does not offer a comprehensive theory of religion.

Naturalistic research links recent psychedelic use with reported transformative experience and social connectedness. Its structural-equation analysis was explicitly exploratory and nonpreregistered. Because participants were not randomly assigned to those life situations, statistical mediation is not sufficient to establish the causal story.S12 Moreover, connectedness is not the same outcome as reliable group judgment. A group may feel closer while becoming less willing to hear a dissenter. The possibility should be tested rather than assumed in either direction.

9. Who is allowed to interpret?

The political question is not just who has an unusual experience, but whose report is heard, how it is classified and who receives authority from it. A priest, clinician, scientist, community elder, platform operator or participant may each claim a different kind of standing. Expertise about mechanisms does not automatically confer moral jurisdiction over a person's life. First-person access to an experience does not automatically confer public authority over others.

Consider a fictional community member who reports a vision about moving a settlement. The report may deserve patient attention. Acting on it still requires examining water, safety, competing testimony, resources and consent. Dismissing the speaker without hearing them and making everyone obey the vision are both failures of inquiry. This example is invented to expose the distinction; it is not an ethnographic description.

Our normative position is that communities need ways to acknowledge meaningful experiences while preserving refusal, dissent and correction. This includes the ability to reject an imposed interpretation. “You are resisting the truth” should not be an all-purpose answer to disagreement. Neither should “your experience is merely chemistry” be an all-purpose reason to ignore distress or significance.

10. Ethics belongs in the argument

The Indigenous Peyote Conservation Initiative describes a position centered on Indigenous cultural perspectives and distinguishes peyote issues from generalized drug-reform efforts. This is a living organization's account of its priorities, not permission to represent all Indigenous peoples.S14 A reusable open project must not treat public visibility as an unlimited entitlement to extract ceremony, imagery or knowledge.

For future commissioned cultural chapters, the project should specify the writer's relationship to the material, compensation, review arrangements, permitted reuse and any limits on disclosure. Community review does not substitute for checking empirical claims, and academic review does not substitute for consent. Those responsibilities answer different questions.

The same care applies to archaeological human remains and destructive sampling. Our editorial position is to ask whose permissions governed the work and how knowledge is returned, rather than treating remains as ownerless evidence. This draft has not audited the governance of each cited excavation and does not claim otherwise.

Challenging-experience research documents adverse as well as positively interpreted outcomes; its selected retrospective survey cannot establish their prevalence among all users.S13 An inquiry about meaning should not erase distress, impairment or coercion to preserve an uplifting arc. Nor should it require suffering to be narrated as growth. This is an educational project, not clinical guidance.

Expectancy and compromised blinding complicate interpretation in psychedelic trials. The methodological debate concerns what can be attributed to which component, not whether all research should be discarded.S16 Funding, participant selection, facilitator expectations and publication incentives belong in study appraisal alongside chemistry.

11. AI enters through the interpretation problem

AI does not need to be a drug to belong in this inquiry. The relevant comparison is what people do with a compelling account generated in interaction. A language model can help organize a report, offer analogies or produce a polished explanation. Fluency alone does not make that explanation true, and repetition across generated responses does not establish independent corroboration.

Research on sycophancy has found tested language models sometimes favor agreement with user beliefs over truthfulness. This is a bounded behavioral finding about evaluated systems, not a claim that every model always agrees.S15

Our extension is a hypothesis: when someone is strongly invested in an interpretation, an affirming AI response may increase its apparent authority. This paper supplies no clinical or population evidence for that extension. A test would have to measure belief change, distinguish supportive language from factual agreement, consider disagreement styles and protect participants. It should not provoke vulnerable participants into distress to produce an interesting demonstration.

There is a more immediate obligation for this project. Its own AI-generated prose may look more coherent and complete than the evidence warrants. We therefore expose source access limits, retain unresolved questions, separate the user's initial idea from AI-developed claims and avoid inventing an expert review. This manuscript is an object of the critique it advances.

The analogy stops well before “AI is tripping.” A system's outputs, a human's experience and pharmacological changes in a nervous system are different objects of study. Nothing in the comparison establishes machine consciousness, a shared mechanism or an evolutionary march from mushrooms to chatbots.

12. Research agenda and revision conditions

The immediate priority is deeper appraisal, not more sweeping coverage. First, fully extract the four archaeological cases: sampling, identification, dates, contamination controls, alternatives and inference limits. Second, extract creativity and spiritual-experience studies with sample characteristics, exclusions, measures, blinding and follow-up. Third, commission or invite appropriately situated cultural scholarship before expanding ceremonial histories. Fourth, develop the AI analogy only where a specific claim can be tested.

The organizing hypothesis should weaken if studies find that apparent epistemic benefits disappear under better controls, or that unusual experiences do not improve question generation or later evaluation in the conditions claimed. A historical contribution claim should weaken if ordinary transmission, institutions or ecology explain the pattern without the proposed drug pathway. Evidence of harm or exclusion should change the assessment even if participants also report meaning.

A future version should record changed claims explicitly: what was believed, what new evidence appeared, what changed and which editions were updated. No revision should quietly turn an evocative possibility into an established conclusion. Equally, a negative result should not be hidden because it spoils a title.

Conclusion

The defensible project is bigger than a single origin story because it can survive that story failing. Specific substances, practices and experiences belong to histories that deserve close investigation. Their existence does not collapse cognition, religion and culture into chemistry. The most productive question is what people make of changed experience, and how they learn to distinguish meaning, knowledge and authority.

The universe has not supplied a foreword. We can still do the research.

The source appraisal records sample, control, interpretation and access limitations, including corrections carried into this revision.
