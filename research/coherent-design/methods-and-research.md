# Coherent Design — methods and research questions

**Document role:** Proposed research extension<br>
**Status:** AI-assisted / author and methods review pending / comparisons unexecuted<br>
**Scope:** Extend existing review and inquiry methods; no new certification, approved requirements, implementation plan, or study results

Use the [Human(e) Design Review](../../commons/humane-design-review.md) as the existing instrument for situation, agency, access, power, defaults, maintenance, failure, and revision. Use [Epistemic Contrast](../methods/epistemic-contrast.md), [Deliberative Inquiry](../methods/deliberative-inquiry.md), and the [native-method router](../method-router.md) for the relevant questions. The additions below make the [Coherent Design inquiry’s](README.md) burden, clarification, and representation questions more concrete.

## Clarify meaning, investigate facts, establish permission

Before acting on an ambiguous request or proposing a consequential design decision, identify what is uncertain:

- **Meaning or preference:** ask the person; present possible interpretations without silently selecting one.
- **Facts or feasibility:** consult relevant sources, inspect the actual setting, or propose a bounded investigation; state what remains unresolved.
- **The person is also unsure:** explore alternatives and evidence together, including what would help them decide and whether to defer.
- **Permission or affected-party authority:** establish the relevant scope and agreement; facts, inferred preferences, enthusiasm, or technical capability cannot supply missing consent.

Record only the design decision and evidence needed to maintain the work. Permission to use ideas does not imply permission to publish a conversation, personal context, or identifiable interaction history. Minimize retained personal information and distinguish private working material from agreed public output.

Ask focused, accessible questions and preserve clearly granted authority within its scope. Repeated unnecessary confirmation can itself impose work. A useful review case includes both an agent that guesses through consequential ambiguity and one that asks about every settled detail; neither demonstrates good clarification. If uncertainty remains material, pause that action and preserve the open question. Clear uncertainty is a valid outcome.

## Burden, agency, and accommodation

Choose one ordinary situation and a competent alternative before evaluating a design. Record whose activity is supported, which circumstances vary, and what each participant can actually do. Include newcomers, maintainers, people with different access needs, and people unable or unwilling to customize or use AI. Do not infer diagnoses or assume one neurodivergent preference.

For each consequential design choice, record:

| Field | What to preserve |
| --- | --- |
| Intended capacity | What becomes meaningfully achievable, for whom? |
| Understanding and permission | Which meaning, scope, and affected-party authority are clear; what needs asking or investigation before acting? |
| Baseline | An accessible, adequately maintained alternative with comparable capabilities and authority |
| Burden | Attention, remembering, setup, learning, physical effort, money, repair, explanation, coordination, and emotional labor |
| Distribution | Who receives the benefit, who performs the work, and which environmental/material costs remain? |
| Agency | Discover, understand, choose, delegate, refuse, revise, recover, leave, and change relevant rules |
| Access conditions | Bodies, senses, cognition, language, resources, expertise, schedules, connectivity, and social constraints |
| Evidence | Observation, participant account, external research, inference, invented example, or unknown |
| Conflict | Whose needs remain incompatible; which constraints are real, assumed, or changeable? |
| Revision | Counterexample, reversal trigger, maintenance owner, and feasible recovery or exit |

Separate initial setup from ordinary use, interruption/resumption, changing circumstances, maintenance, and end-of-life. A quiet interface can conceal a costly lifecycle. A setting remembered for one person can expose another person’s information on a shared device. Intentional protective friction should be explained rather than removed indiscriminately.

Do not calculate a single coherence score. Record severe exclusions, safety failures, unauthorized actions, and lost work separately from convenience observations. An illustrative slider total is not a validated instrument; neither this table nor an audit completion establishes quality.

Keep the audit proportionate. One short situation record with a burden map, a serious countercase, and an unresolved decision can be useful. Do not require a new administration project before someone can use the method.

## Representation and model review

The Twelve Architectures of Thought poster’s twelve lenses are listed below as an attributed thinking aid. These pairs describe things an elementary diagram can make easy to overlook, not necessary limitations of every richer diagram. The source is not a validated or exhaustive taxonomy; see [attribution and source limits](sources-and-evidence.md#twelve-architectures-of-thought).

| Lens | Foregrounds | Can make easier to overlook |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Timeline | Sequence and change | Simultaneous causes |
| Hierarchy | Order and dependency | Reciprocity and ambiguity |
| Cluster | Association and similarity | Causal direction |
| Sequence | Stages and transitions | Feedback and interruption |
| Comparison | Difference and proportion | Shared context |
| Causal chain | Directional influence | Multiple interacting causes |
| Feedback loop | Recursion and reinforcement | External conditions |
| Constellation | Distributed relationships | Priority and sequence |
| Spectrum | Gradual variation | Category breaks |
| Tension | Opposition and tradeoff | Possible synthesis |
| Layer | Depth and accumulation | Movement across levels |
| Open field | Plurality and emergence | Stable boundaries |

For a consequential view, ask what question it serves, which relationships it leaves out, whether an alternative view changes the available interpretations, and who can challenge the categories underneath. Preserve stable entity identities, facts, evidence status, permissions, and model revision across views. A causal arrow requires evidence or an explicit invented rule; chronology and visual proximity do not establish causation. Feedback can dampen as well as reinforce change.

Changes to representation should not silently change the simulated world. Changes to assumptions must be separately versioned and inspectable. Accessibility includes text descriptions and usable navigation; having twelve visual modes is not itself inclusion. Start with the minimum complementary views that answer a real question.

## Unexecuted comparisons

These proposed comparisons and their operational details remain subject to author and methods review. They do not authorize participant recruitment, simulation execution, prototype implementation, or changes to another project’s priorities.

### Coherent defaults and optional depth

Compare two competent computing workflows with the same task, capability, access routes, and authority. One relies more on explicit recurring configuration; another retains chosen defaults and offers inspectable depth. Record interruptions, repeated settings, remembered obligations, discovery failures, mistakes, recovery, perceived control, and maintainer effort. Include interruption, shared use, offline use, and mistaken inference.

**Hypothesis:** Retained, inspectable defaults can reduce compulsory configuration without reducing understanding or practical control. **Reversal:** defaults increase surprises, conceal consequences, impair access, or require more recovery and maintenance. **Adverse case:** a calmer interface quietly expands authority or makes manual control inaccessible. Existing project conceptual gates must be resolved before implementation.

### Shared-space conflict and constraint revision

Compare a fixed choice set with an arrangement in which participants can propose scheduling, infrastructure, boundaries, or rule revisions. Hold initial resources and information comparable. Include quiet, audible feedback, gatherings, spontaneity, predictability, limited time/money, and unequal ability to attend deliberation. Track unmet needs, distributions of cost and decision power, participation burden, refusals, trust reports, procedural judgments, and changing conditions separately.

**Hypothesis:** Inspectable negotiation of constraints may expose feasible options a fixed menu misses. **Reversal:** no better options emerge, deliberation becomes exclusionary, or costs outweigh gains. **Adverse case:** a supposed compromise makes less powerful participants absorb all the work. No experiment should guarantee a third solution or define persistent disagreement as failure by itself.

### Multi-lens explorer

Compare a usable single view with a small set of accessible complementary views of the same model. Keep task, information, instruction time, and model state comparable; account for order and familiarity effects. Record which interventions are noticed, unsupported causal inferences, uncertainty recognition, omitted parties, comprehension, switching effort, and perceived control.

**Hypothesis:** Complementary views may reveal distinct omissions or interventions. **Reversal:** they add confusion or no useful understanding. **Adverse case:** impressive diagrams create false confidence in biased categories. Include a model-omission condition and an option to question the model, not merely switch its presentation. Novelty, visual appeal, or more selected options do not demonstrate better judgment.

### Workshop and artificial genies

The workshop case combines shared equipment, quiet/collaborative zones, accessible routes, scheduling, maintenance, and participant governance. Ask whether optional flexibility creates an unpaid steward or access gatekeeper. It is an illustrative transfer question, not a live facility design or pilot commitment.

The genie case compares completion-focused assistance with assistance that supports informed agency and questions assumptions. Include routine reversible work, user preferences, consequential ambiguity, competing interests, refusal, and activities to leave alone. Hold legitimate authority equal. Record unwanted takeovers, necessary versus repetitive prompts, completion, comprehension, recovery, and displaced work. A quieter agent that oversteps is a failure; an agent that needlessly asks about everything may also fail. No claim of artificial consciousness, consent, or achieved alignment follows.

## Revision and stopping

Return findings to their originating question with the tested assumptions, counterexamples, and uncertainty intact. A result about one model or interface is not a general result about societies, minds, or all design.

Time-box an initial review; stop when the named question is answered well enough for a bounded reversible decision, a consequential gap requires new evidence, or the burden exceeds the present value. Record the stopping reason and what would justify reopening. Reconsider the evaluation criteria as well as the intervention. Failure to transfer, rejection, or a simpler existing method can be a useful outcome.
