# Coherent Design — make room for possibility

**Document role:** Canonical research inquiry and working synthesis<br>
**Status:** Exploratory / AI-assisted / author review pending<br>
**Evidence:** Normative proposals, interpretive connections, metaphors, and untested design hypotheses; no validated general framework

Coherent Design asks how products, interfaces, environments, and institutions can work well as they are while making room for people to understand, change, extend, combine, or decline them.

The motivating image is **tofu**: a useful foundation with an identity of its own, open to many preparations. Add ingredients if you want. Leaving it alone must also be a legitimate choice. **DIY, but optional.**

The working ambition is **possibility without unnecessary imposition**: dependable foundations, meaningful agency, accessible depth, and relationships that can change when people or circumstances change. It concerns the conditions under which different futures become achievable, rather than one prescribed ideal outcome.

## Unburdensome is different from feature-poor

Consider an object that feels *raw*, useful, focused, and out of the way: a foundation that makes room for other purposes. Those qualities do not require missing features, and removing features does not establish them. A stripped-down vehicle is an illustrative case, not evidence of reduced ownership burden; reliability, access, repair, and accessory costs must still be examined.

Several distinctions matter:

| Term | Question it brings into view |
| --- | --- |
| Basic | How much has been provided? |
| Minimalist | Is reduction itself a design aim? |
| Modular | Can capabilities or components be changed? |
| Unburdensome | How much compulsory attention, adaptation, effort, or dependency does the relationship demand? |
| Coherent | Do purposes, capabilities, defaults, constraints, and relationships work together under actual conditions? |

These properties can overlap without being equivalent. A hundred capabilities can be quietly available; ten can require constant tending. **Complexity of capability** and **complexity of obligation** are separate concerns. Sophisticated internal design may reduce demands on someone using it, but may also create maintenance costs elsewhere.

The opposite of unburdensome is *demanding*. The goal is purposeful, proportionate, discoverable complexity that people can meaningfully navigate. It includes choosing subtraction, no customization, direct involvement, or delegation. Design effort spent on useful defaults can spare people repetitive configuration, remembering, and interruption; attention and access needs still differ between people and circumstances.

## The relationship is the design boundary

A manual window may reduce mechanism complexity and create a physical access barrier. Removing a stereo may create device-pairing and troubleshooting chores. Customization may become another unpaid job. A polished service may depend on hidden labor, surveillance, disposable hardware, or a proprietary dependency.

Unburdensomeness is therefore relational: it depends on a person’s abilities, needs, resources, expectations, environment, and changing circumstances. It also depends on other people and material systems.

**Experiential coherence** asks whether an arrangement feels understandable and empowering to its participant. **Systemic coherence** asks what labor, costs, dependencies, and harms make that experience possible. These are working distinctions, not measured constructs. The inquiry must trace the entire relationship and lifecycle rather than declare victory when friction disappears from one screen.

Not all effort is a burden to eliminate. Making, learning, care, anticipation, deliberation, and chosen participation can be valuable. Some friction protects people from consequential mistakes. A design should distinguish unwanted obligations from involvement someone values.

## A foundation that accommodates other purposes

The tofu metaphor illustrates **purposeful neutrality**: a purpose that accommodates other purposes. A platform can take a strong position on what must work while leaving people room to decide how to live with it. That position still distributes power; neutrality is an aspiration to examine, not an absence of values.

“A million possibilities” means potential configurations, combinations, and uses, including ones the original designer did not anticipate. It does not mean presenting a million settings. Useful, reliable, accessible, understandable defaults should connect to personalization, extension, and adaptation without turning any of them into prerequisites.

Modularity alone is insufficient. Connections must make conceptual and functional sense as well as physical or technical sense. Someone should be able to use one part without first understanding the entire system.

| Working principle | Its practical question | Failure to watch for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coherent defaults | What works before customization? | A “default person” everyone else must imitate |
| Complexity and abundance on demand | Can useful depth be found when wanted? | Hidden controls or obligatory administration |
| Agency-preserving assistance | Can people choose, delegate, refuse, revise, and recover? | Ease purchased by surrendering control |
| Foundational accommodation | Are differences anticipated from the beginning? | Accessibility treated as an exceptional retrofit |
| Legibility | Can behavior, constraints, consequences, and uncertainty be understood? | Transparency that becomes information overload |
| Clarity, curiosity, and meaningful permission | Are we clear about what is meant, what is known, and what is authorized? | A confident assumption substituted for clarification, inquiry, or consent |
| Adaptability and composability | Can needs and arrangements change together? | Fragility, incompatibility, or maintenance homework |
| Non-imposition | Which demands are justified by the purpose and affected parties? | A designer’s preference presented as necessity |
| Systemic responsibility | Where do work, risk, dependencies, and material costs go? | Burden displaced beyond the visible user |

These principles interact. Adaptability without legibility can confuse; access without adaptability can harden into a new norm; agency inaccessible in practice becomes a privilege; modularity without coherent defaults becomes homework. They are proposals for inquiry, not independent boxes whose scores can be added.

## Clarity, curiosity, and meaningful permission

**When unsure, do not assume: ask. When the facts are uncertain, investigate. When the person is unsure too, explore the question together.**

Uncertainty about someone's meaning, preferences, intended scope, or permission needs clarification before acting on that uncertainty. Research can improve the factual picture; it cannot establish what someone intended or authorize an action on their behalf. Agreeing with an idea, asking to develop it, and agreeing to make personal material public are different decisions.

If the person does not yet know what they want, help make the uncertainty understandable: offer questions, plausible interpretations, alternatives, relevant evidence, and the option to defer. Distinguish what is known from what is inferred or still unknown. Research may challenge either party's initial framing; it should not turn one confident answer into a substitute for shared understanding. Clarity can include a well-understood disagreement or an explicit unknown.

Curiosity means remaining open to unexpected answers, questioning one's own framing, and following useful evidence. For an artificial system, these describe inquiry behavior, without presuming subjective curiosity or experience. Asking must also be considerate: seek the clarification needed, allow time and accessible ways to respond, and avoid interrogation, unnecessary personal disclosure, or repeated requests for permission already clearly granted within an unchanged scope. While a material question is unresolved, pause the dependent action rather than fill the gap with a guess.

Consent should support agency in practice: understandable purposes and consequences, specific scope, voluntary choice, feasible refusal, and opportunities to revise or withdraw. Silence, uncertainty, dependence, or agreement to one purpose must not be treated as permission for another. Include the people whose interests or information are affected; one person's authorization may not settle a shared decision.

Consent processes themselves can be questioned and improved. Supported decision-making, shared deliberation, and better ways to express and maintain agreements may help. An allegedly better process must preserve meaningful refusal and affected-party authority; it is not a reason to bypass them. The [existing consent and refusal lens](../../concepts/humane-design.md#consent-and-refusal) and [Deliberative Inquiry](../methods/deliberative-inquiry.md) provide related starting points.

## Possibility must become usable capacity

A technically permitted action may still be undiscoverable, unaffordable, inaccessible, difficult to learn, fragile, or socially impossible to refuse. Distinguish:

1. **Possible:** the system technically permits it.
2. **Accessible:** a person can realistically discover and use it.
3. **Meaningfully empowering:** it expands what that person can do or become on terms they can accept.

This is an illustrative distinction, not a universal sequence. The capability approach is a useful intellectual neighbor; “more options” is not a substitute for substantive freedom. **Accommodation capacity** is a proposed question about how many meaningful differences an arrangement can support without rigid conformity, excessive complexity, or unequal burdens. It is not yet a defined measure.

## Coherence without conformity

Accommodation, voluntary association, and skepticism toward unnecessary domination connect this inquiry to anarchist traditions. That affinity is not exclusive to anarchism, does not summarize all anarchist traditions, and does not settle one governance form. Accessibility, inclusive design, design justice, commons research, and other traditions contribute related questions with distinct histories and disagreements.

Coherence need not mean uniform components, a single design language, or shared lifestyles and worldviews. Biodiversity rather than monoculture; an orchestra rather than a single note; a kitchen whose ingredients do not all taste like tofu. Those are metaphors for workable relationships among differences, not evidence that every arrangement is compatible or desirable.

Real conflicts remain: quiet versus audible feedback, spontaneity versus predictability, customization versus dependable consistency. Individual accommodation cannot resolve every shared consequence. A possible working loop is **recognize → accommodate → negotiate → adapt → learn**, with feedback rather than a final perfect state.

The separate [Coherence Without Uniformity / Contextual Coherence inquiry, PR #57](https://github.com/Root-Sequence/root-sequence/pull/57), at [source revision 3ab2753](https://github.com/Root-Sequence/root-sequence/blob/3ab27534c555185bcdde9467356587ea9b5a05a2/research/contextual-coherence/README.md), develops the shared-rule side of this question: protections for everyone affected, locally responsive means, interoperable interfaces, polycentric coordination, and legitimate amendment powers. Its Wiggle illustration, tradeoffs, and unexecuted comparisons remain canonical there. This connection adds no general validation or requirement to adopt one governance arrangement.

Possible tools include reversible decisions, adjustable boundaries, multiple routes, shared protocols, transparent constraints, and participation in changing the rules. The system itself must be open to legitimate revision. Options *inside* an architecture and influence *over* its architecture are different kinds of agency.

The shared-space example makes this concrete: a neighborhood needs quiet and space for gatherings. Explore schedules, acoustic changes, other venues, infrastructure, and decision procedures alongside the original choices. Ask who can decide, who is missing, who pays, and what remains unsatisfied. **There need not be a magical third solution.** Some constraints and tradeoffs persist; dissent and practical exit remain relevant.

The resulting motifs are:

- **Coherence without conformity.**
- **Abundance without imposition.**
- **Coordination without domination.**

These are normative ambitions, not properties a diagram or simulation can certify.

## Five functions, connected in both directions

Five proposed conceptual functions make these relationships easier to examine:

| Function | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Foundation | What should work reliably and accessibly without customization? |
| Possibility | What can people change, extend, combine, or decline? |
| Coordination | How can different needs and configurations coexist? |
| Governance | Who can change rules, challenge decisions, and resolve conflicts? |
| Learning | How are failures noticed, assumptions revised, and arrangements adapted? |

These are neither five mandatory components nor a hierarchy. Governance affects foundations; experience affects governance; learning can alter every function. Governance need not mean endless meetings: local autonomy, scoped delegation, agreements, appeals, and transparent protocols may serve different contexts. No single model is selected.

## Plurality of perspectives, coherence of relationships

The *Twelve Architectures of Thought* image offers a way to examine representations: design the ways people can understand relationships, as well as the relationships themselves. Layers can clarify depth while obscuring movement; networks reveal connections while obscuring priorities; feedback diagrams may hide external conditions; two-sided tensions can make alternatives disappear.

Multiple views of a shared situation could support different questions—history, dependencies, causes, participation, feedback, tradeoffs, or emerging possibilities. The [representation method](methods-and-research.md#representation-and-model-review) preserves all twelve lenses and their blind spots.

Changing a view does not repair a model that excludes important people, categories, evidence, or relationships. Participants must be able to question the underlying model as well. A **multi-lens Coherent World Explorer** is a proposed application to investigate whether representations change which interventions people notice. No benefit, implementation, or study result is claimed.

## Recursive research, design, development—and deliberation

The unit of design can expand from objects to interactions, environments, relationships, rules, conditions of possibility, and the process of designing those conditions. Designing ways for people to invent and adapt can matter as much as designing a particular finished outcome.

R&D with *Design* explicit, R&D&D, or the playful **R&D²** name the same iterative practice: research asks what is happening and unknown; design explores what might become possible and what must be accommodated; development exposes feasibility through making and testing. **Deliberation** asks whose aims and authority guide all three. These are not standardized names or a linear pipeline.

The recursive loop is **experience → understand → reimagine → experiment → evaluate → reconsider**. Reconsider the goals and measurements as well as the intervention: speed may conceal loss of agency; convenience may conceal unpaid work; an access improvement may exclude someone else; a formerly useful arrangement may stop fitting its environment.

Reflection also has costs. Use proportionate deliberation, bounded review, external memory, reversible decisions, and stopping rules. A system can remain open to reconsideration without requiring everyone to reconsider everything continuously.

Developing an unburdensome system can itself become burdensome. Overload is not evidence that an idea is correct or profound. The process should support partial understanding, external memory, rest, and the ability to leave a question open without losing it.

## What remains open

Freedom/safety; customization/simplicity; individual/collective needs; transparency/overload; stability/adaptation; decentralization/coordination; convenience/externalized costs. Each needs situated evidence, affected perspectives, and explicit limits rather than a universal optimum.

Existing research supports investigating human variation, attention, substantive freedom, governance, and representation. **This particular synthesis remains unvalidated.** It has no demonstrated cross-domain effectiveness, settled operational definition, validated audit instrument, participant results, or novelty finding. Failure to transfer between domains would be useful evidence.

The open north star is to make room for many different, revisable ways of flourishing without assuming all possibilities are harmless or viable. The framework must remain capable of discovering its own imperfections and changing without sacrificing the freedoms that make change worthwhile.
