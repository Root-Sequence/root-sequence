> What if one way to increase effective intelligence-related capability is to change the conditions under which minds operate, rather than requiring any mind to know everything?

This question is the *starting point*, not the answer. It synthesizes distinctions from cognitive science, psychometrics, social epistemology, information science, the capability approach, organizational psychology, collective intelligence, evolutionary theory, open science, and AI studies. The prose is exploratory; labels below distinguish domain evidence from hypotheses and normative proposals.

## 1. A short orientation

- **Intelligence is not omniscience.** An agent may learn and adapt across unfamiliar problems without already possessing all relevant answers.
- **Knowledge and intelligence are not identical.** Skill, prior experience, reasoning, generalization, transfer, and learning efficiency need separate treatment.
- **Individual ability and effective capability differ.** What someone can do depends partly on resources, accessibility, environment, time, authority, and support.
- **A group can possess necessary information without integrating it.** The relevant problem may be epistemic routing, credibility, coordination, or incentives—not missing individual brilliance.
- **More capability is not necessarily more freedom.** Assistance can improve outcomes while increasing lock-in, dependence, power concentration, or exclusion.
- **An intelligence commons is a design inquiry.** The goal would be shared conditions for finding, testing, creating, and using knowledge, not obligatory openness or a single collective mind.
- **Root Sequence routes and synthesizes; fields establish mechanisms; applied projects test local implications.** An attractive interdisciplinary resemblance is not proof of a universal law.

## 2. Where the question began: animals, AI, and the misleading ladder

Animal intelligence includes human intelligence: humans are animals. Animal cognition spans individual learning, sensory specialization, tool use, social learning, culture, and collective behavior; it is not reducible to collective cognition. Likewise, not all collective behavior warrants calling a group an intelligent agent.

Artificial general intelligence (AGI) concerns broad adaptive intellectual capability, though operational definitions vary. Artificial superintelligence (ASI) refers to artificial capabilities greatly exceeding human ones across a broad range, not to ethical superiority or omniscience.

**Universal intelligence** has a more precise technical history than "a level after AGI": Legg and Hutter (2007) proposed a formal expected-performance measure across many computable environments. Hutter's AIXI is a theoretical, uncomputable ideal. That formal research program is not an AGI → universal intelligence → ASI development sequence. [Legg & Hutter 2007](https://arxiv.org/abs/0712.3329); [Hutter 2007](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0701125).

**Generality** and **high performance** are not interchangeable. A chess engine's expertise is not universal adaptability; a human generalist's capacity to investigate an unfamiliar machine is not mastery of every domain. Chollet (2019) proposes evaluating intelligence partly in terms of *skill-acquisition efficiency* under constraints of prior knowledge, experience, task scope, and generalization difficulty. This is a research proposal, not a settled definition. [Chollet 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.01547).

Working question: is generality better measured by a repertory of solved tasks, by speed and transfer of learning, by ability to find and combine resources, or by some multidimensional combination? Avoid prematurely converting these into a single scalar.

## 3. Intelligence as capacity, process, and situated accomplishment

Distinguish:

1. **Latent capacity:** what an individual could learn or reason through under appropriate conditions.
2. **Enacted process:** perception, exploration, pattern recognition, modeling, inference, experimentation, and revision.
3. **Demonstrated task performance:** success on particular problems with particular supports.
4. **Effective capability:** what can realistically be accomplished given tools, collaborators, resources, time, permissions, and institutional constraints.
5. **Substantive agency:** whether the actor can choose, refuse, challenge, revise, and influence conditions that affect future opportunities.

Do not use "intelligence" to collapse all five. The fifth is not a psychometric ability; a system may be effective while reducing another person's agency.

**Illustration:** Two equally capable troubleshooters may obtain very different outcomes when one has documentation, test tools, quiet, time, replacement parts, and authority to act while the other has none. Their performance difference cannot automatically be assigned to their underlying cognitive differences.

The *capability approach* developed by Amartya Sen and others distinguishes possessing resources from having genuine opportunities to achieve valued functionings. "Conversion factors" capture personal, social, and environmental conditions influencing the usability of a resource. Translating that approach into cognition is **our research analogy**, not a claim that Sen developed a theory of AI or a complete account of intelligence. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Capability Approach](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/capability-approach/).

A productive diagnostic reframing is: not only "What is missing from the person?" but "What is missing from the circumstances under which this person's abilities could become effective?" The opposite error is to deny real differences in ability or suggest environment alone explains all performance.

## 4. A navigable chain of epistemic capability

Knowledge can exist without becoming practically useful. A proposed chain:

**Exists → discoverable → accessible/comprehensible → evaluable → actionable → revisable.**

At each point there are potential failures:
- An answer exists but is inaccessible behind fees or institutional restrictions.
- A person could understand the answer but lacks the field name or search vocabulary.
- A document is findable but inaccessible through language, presentation, or disability barriers.
- A claim is legible but hard to evaluate because its provenance and uncertainties are concealed.
- Knowledge is adequate but the actor lacks material means or authority to act.
- An intervention can be enacted but its consequences cannot be challenged, audited, or reversed.

This chain is heuristic; inquiry also loops, skips stages, and changes its question. Do not treat it as a necessary developmental sequence or an actual measurement scale.

This closely complements the existing [Epistemic Discoverability and Knowledge Routing](../../concepts/epistemic-discoverability.md): someone may grasp a phenomenon without knowing how specialists name it. Marcia Bates's *berrypicking* model also emphasizes evolving questions during searching rather than assuming one fixed query. [Bates 1989](https://pages.gseis.ucla.edu/faculty/bates/berrypicking.html).

## 5. Cognitive offloading and human–AI thinking

**Cognitive offloading** means using external action or supports to alter a task's information-processing demands. Notes, reminders, calculators, diagrams, teammates, and AI assistants can play this role. Risko and Gilbert's review covers determinants and consequences and emphasizes metacognition. [Risko & Gilbert 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2016.07.002).

Distinguish related concepts:
- **Offloading:** shifting particular memory or processing burdens.
- **Scaffolding:** structuring support so a task or learning process becomes possible.
- **Extended/distributed cognition:** theories and analyses concerning cognition spanning persons, tools, representations, and settings.
- **Augmentation:** expanding practical performance through assistance.
- **Collaborative reasoning:** joint investigation, challenge, synthesis, and revision.
- **Metacognition:** monitoring and regulating one's own thinking.

They overlap but are not synonyms. External support may be indispensable to a task without the entire network being one person, consciousness, or legitimate authority.

**Two outcomes must be measured separately:** (a) immediate quality and efficiency of assisted work; (b) retained learning, understanding, confidence calibration, and independent or substitutable capability after assistance changes or disappears.

A 2024 systematic review and meta-analysis of 106 experimental studies found human–AI groups performed better than humans alone on average but worse than the better of humans alone or AI alone, with important task-dependent variation. Collaboration is not synonymous with synergy. [Vaccaro, Almaatouq & Malone 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-024-02024-1).

Research question: when does offloading remove needless work, when does it scaffold growth, and when does it erode understanding or lock users into a single provider?

## 6. Transactive memory: nobody needs every answer

**Transactive memory** addresses how groups distribute knowledge and maintain workable information about who knows what, who can retrieve it, and how to coordinate. Daniel Wegner developed this approach; research with close couples illustrates expertise differentiation and dependence on a shared memory arrangement. [Wegner 1987](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4612-4634-3_9); [Wegner, Erber & Raymond 1991](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1774630/).

The generalist's contribution need not be deepest expertise in every domain. It may be:
- recognizing that an unfamiliar problem crosses domains;
- identifying relevant specialties and first sources;
- translating terms while preserving distinctions;
- discovering missing information and overlooked actors;
- making uncertainty legible;
- bringing conflicts to the appropriate expert, community, or evidence;
- checking whether the collaboration produces better answers.

This is **epistemic navigation**, not a new psychometric factor. It can be useful yet unreliable: poor routing, misplaced trust, shallow analogies, bad translation, or overconfident synthesis can make outcomes worse.

A crucial revision to the original dialogue: **"Nobody understands the whole problem" is not itself necessarily the pathology.** In sufficiently complex systems, no individual may be able to. What matters is whether relevant partial knowledge can be brought into warranted relationship without requiring one omniscient central authority.

For a focused treatment of how knowledge moves (or fails to move) from **expertise routing** through **critical uptake** and **decision authority**, see [From knowledge to uptake](from-knowledge-to-uptake.md). It also develops boundary objects, epistemic labor, and problem-discovery tests without introducing a new universal construct.

For the next research layer—**what makes reliance on distributed knowledge warranted** when no participant can check everything—see [Epistemic trust and collective verification](epistemic-trust-and-collective-verification.md). This companion distinguishes expert testimony, epistemic vigilance, scientific verification, division of cognitive labor, trust calibration, and legitimate authority; it includes the 2026 ICML HiddenBench result as a bounded comparison, not proof of a universal intelligence theory.

## 7. Hidden profiles: when the group already knows enough and fails anyway

Classic **hidden-profile** research examined groups whose members held different pieces of relevant information. Stasser and Titus (1985) found that unstructured discussions disproportionately sampled shared information and could preserve a collective error rather than expose private evidence. [Stasser & Titus 1985](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.48.6.1467).

Different failure mechanisms should remain separate:
- Knowledge is absent.
- Knowledge exists but cannot be found.
- Expertise is present but the group does not know where.
- A source is available but its credibility is unfairly discounted.
- A relevant person is not invited or cannot participate.
- Distinct observations are flattened into a misleading consensus.
- Coordination overhead overwhelms the information benefit.
- The group has sufficient factual information but disagrees about values, authority, or distribution of consequences.

Compare deliberation procedures that preserve initial independent judgments, expose previously unshared information, invite contradictory evidence, and permit anonymous or accessible contributions. The question is not how to make everyone agree, but how to make important differences discoverable and usable.

## 8. Epistemic injustice: whose knowledge can count?

Miranda Fricker's [Epistemic Injustice: Power and the Ethics of Knowing](https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780198237907.001.0001) (2007) identifies:
- **Testimonial injustice:** prejudicially lowering a speaker's credibility.
- **Hermeneutical injustice:** structural, prejudicial gaps in shared interpretive resources that unfairly impede making sense of consequential experiences.

These terms are more precise than "someone wasn't listened to." Sometimes testimony is correctly rejected on evidentiary grounds, or a concept is missing without structural prejudice; not every misunderstanding is epistemic injustice.

**Example (invented):** A disabled resident identifies a route access failure that official throughput indicators omit. Officials refuse to investigate because they presume resident knowledge is unreliable. The unfair credibility discount exemplifies testimonial injustice. If the institution's categories cannot even represent the relevant burden and marginalized participants have lacked a fair role in shaping those categories, a hermeneutical dimension may also be present. Whether either classification applies depends on the actual circumstances and evidence.

Further research:
- Kristie Dotson studies silencing and the harm of failures to receive testimony appropriately. [Dotson 2011](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1527-2001.2011.01177.x).
- José Medina studies active ignorance, epistemic arrogance, and productive friction between different social perspectives. [Medina 2013](https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199929023.003.0001).

**Key connection:** a system can have immense information-processing ability while having a severe deficiency in how it recognizes, invites, and evaluates relevant knowledge. This can be both an injustice and a source of model error.

Do not turn inclusion into the assumption that all claims carry equal evidentiary weight. Credibility assessment should attend to competence, proximity to the phenomenon, incentives, track record, and source quality while correcting for relevant prejudice.

## 9. Intelligence as infrastructure

Libraries, languages, schools, archives, accessible interfaces, shared standards, documentation, peer review, maintenance, communications, community support, reliable computation, and rights of participation can all enable cognition without themselves being individually intelligent agents.

Potential layers:
1. **Knowledge:** preservation, documentation, translation, access.
2. **Discoverability:** vocabulary bridges, cross-disciplinary mapping, search and browsing.
3. **Comprehension/accessibility:** disability access, understandable representation, attention/time requirements, learning support.
4. **Coordination:** ways to find expertise, exchange evidence, preserve useful disagreement, and share work.
5. **Verification:** transparent provenance, reproducibility where possible, correction, audit, criticism.
6. **Material support:** devices, networks, power, equipment, labor, housing and care conditions allowing participation.
7. **Governance:** privacy, ownership, consent, right of challenge, alternative routes, and rules for changing the rules.
8. **Continuity:** maintenance, migration, repair, durable memory, lawful deletion and forgetting.

These are **infrastructures supporting intelligence-related capabilities**—not proof that a library, country, or database is a single mind.

A question worth studying: how much variation in problem-solving opportunity stems from unequal access to these conversion conditions, rather than differences in the cognitive capacity of individuals?

## 10. From a knowledge commons to an intelligence commons

The term **intelligence commons** is a *working design question*, not a claim of conceptual novelty, an established legal regime, or a plan to expose all data.

It asks: how might useful knowledge, tools, methods, learning opportunities, interfaces, and coordination capacities be shared and governed so that more people can discover, scrutinize, create, and exercise knowledge without depending on one gatekeeper?

Predecessors include libraries, free/open-source software, open science, science shops, community networks, cooperative knowledge infrastructure, and knowledge-commons governance. Charlotte Hess and Elinor Ostrom edited [Understanding Knowledge as a Commons](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262516037/understanding-knowledge-as-a-commons/) (2007; later paperback 2011).

Key design distinctions:
- **Open knowledge ≠ unconsented data extraction.**
- **Shared infrastructure ≠ one universal registry.**
- **Open weights ≠ necessarily open-source AI.**
- **Collective capability ≠ collective consciousness.**
- **Expert participation ≠ technocratic decision authority.**
- **Accessibility ≠ mandatory participation.**
- **Interdependence ≠ domination; independence ≠ ideal self-sufficiency.**
- **Publication ≠ verification; representation ≠ endorsement.**
- **Coordination ≠ consensus; accuracy ≠ legitimacy.**

A serious commons needs stewardship, resources, incentives, privacy boundaries, contributor rights, dispute procedures, protection against capture, repair, maintenance, and sustainable alternatives—not only downloads or an attractive declaration.

For a more explicit governance treatment, including the crucial distinction between public knowledge and consent-sensitive knowledge, see [Intelligence commons](intelligence-commons.md). This is a research/design proposal, not a charter or a new organization.

## 11. UNESCO and the AI-industry tension

The [UNESCO Recommendation on Open Science](https://www.unesco.org/en/legal-affairs/recommendation-open-science), adopted 23 November 2021, addresses open scientific knowledge, infrastructures, engagement beyond professional science, inclusion, training, and capacity-building. It also refers directly to collective intelligence through citizen/community engagement. It is an international recommendation and implementation framework for member states, **not a legal command requiring every AI company to release all model weights or proprietary data**. [UNESCO implementation](https://www.unesco.org/en/open-science/implementation).

The overlap with an intelligence commons is substantial, but the two are not identical: the UNESCO text focuses on science; the current inquiry also concerns everyday cognition, social participation, AI interfaces, material capability, and agency.

For a concrete, dated AI transparency comparison: Stanford's [2025 Foundation Model Transparency Index](https://crfm.stanford.edu/fmti/) reported lower average transparency among its assessed developers in 2025 than in 2024, with especially limited disclosure about training data and compute. An index measures its chosen indicators; it does not by itself adjudicate all safety arguments or motives of each company.

Tradeoffs requiring genuine evaluation include privacy, security, dual-use capability, commercial incentives, replication, research accessibility, concentrated computational resources, and the difference between model openness, source access, audit rights, and meaningful end-user power. The [Open Source AI Definition 1.0](https://opensource.org/ai) is a relevant open-source governance baseline.

Practical question for firms and policymakers: **what knowledge, documentation, rights, infrastructure, and independent research access would make AI benefits inspectable and contestable without ignoring legitimate privacy or safety constraints?**

## 12. Intelligence changes the environment that produces intelligence

Cognition, technologies, cultures, and institutions are recursive:
- agents act using inherited capabilities and constraints;
- their actions create tools, institutions, records, incentives, and environmental changes;
- those altered conditions change what later agents can notice, learn, afford, preserve, and accomplish;
- feedback can improve access or make dependence and harmful incentives durable.

Connect carefully to *niche construction*, *cumulative cultural evolution*, *organizational learning*, and *path dependence*. Human cumulative culture is not the only animal culture; the claim that uniquely human culture is best characterized by open-endedness rather than accumulation is a recent scholarly interpretation, not consensus. [Morgan & Feldman 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-02035-y).

This intersects [Intelligence Ecology](../../concepts/intelligence-ecology.md), which asks what behaviors environments reward or select. A complementary inquiry asks **what capacities environments enable**. These must not be merged automatically: selecting a behavior, making a skill available, making a person free to exercise it, and producing a socially desirable outcome are four different properties.

## 13. Capability and agency without compulsory self-sufficiency

Individual "epistemic independence" is not absolute independence from tools or other people. Dependence is ordinary and often necessary. The issue is whether relationships become coercive, unchallengeable, extractive, or irreplaceable.

Compare *supportive interdependence* with *dominating dependence*: how are roles negotiated, how are burdens distributed, who may challenge an expert, whether alternatives are viable, how refusal works, and who inherits care or maintenance labor. A disabled person's need for ongoing assistance must not be treated as proof of reduced moral standing or deficient agency.

Practical test: when assistance improves an immediate task, does the person also gain the ability to understand the answer, question it, retain relevant skills when desirable, request a different form of help, or choose not to participate? Retained unaided performance is one outcome among several—not a universal virtue or requirement.

A capable AI may improve the conditions of human judgment, but capability does not itself confer legitimate authority.

For a source-aware decomposition into **which mechanisms fail** (not merely whether a group fails), and the first computed analytic counterexample where an apparently better protocol does worse, see [Mechanisms of collective knowledge](mechanisms-of-collective-knowledge.md). The Coherent World extension consists of bounded **paper probes**, not validated experiments or implemented scenario rules.

## 14. Collective intelligence: where claims can fail

A collection of specialists may achieve broader task coverage through coordination without becoming a single AGI. The relevant question is about **system-level functional capability**, not whether group members merged into one mind.

Counterexamples and competing explanations:
- More people or compute—not a new emergent ability—explain the result.
- Coordination overhead consumes the alleged gain.
- Shared error becomes stronger with faster communication.
- Hidden-profile information stays hidden.
- A central synthesizer distorts inputs or becomes a point of failure.
- Impressive performance on the trained scenario fails to transfer.
- Protected dissent becomes ornamental or causes costly procedural deadlock.
- A "good" aggregate result disguises harm to a subgroup.
- An AI output is mistaken for evidence or for legitimate authorization.
- A system preserves short-term performance by creating long-term maintenance debt.

Compare against competent single-agent and simpler coordination baselines, not a weak straw-person. Monitor retained capability, transfer, opportunity distribution, resource use, dissent and reversibility separately; never collapse them into a "coherence" scalar.

## 15. Candidate cross-project research questions

**R1 — Generality:** Which task families and novelty conditions actually distinguish general learning from the accumulation of specialized skills?

**R2 — Situated performance:** What proportion of observed performance changes when accessible information, tools, time, permission, and environmental support change while individual capacities are held reasonably stable?

**R3 — Distributed knowledge:** Under what arrangements do transactive memory and structured exchange integrate unique relevant information better than unstructured discussion?

**R4 — Epistemic injustice:** When does biased credibility allocation or inadequate institutional vocabulary suppress evidence relevant to an actual decision, and how do we distinguish those failures from justified evidentiary disagreement?

**R5 — Human–AI complementarity:** Which work benefits from automation, which from scaffolding, which from independent challenge, and which from deliberately minimizing AI intervention?

**R6 — Intelligence infrastructure:** Which investments make knowledge more reachable, understandable, assessable, and actionable without imposing excessive new burdens?

**R7 — Commons governance:** How can shared knowledge and computational resources remain available, contestable, privacy-preserving, reparable, and resistant to capture?

**R8 — Recursive ecology:** How do interventions change the selection pressures and opportunities governing future inquiry, learning, and agency?

**R9 — Scale and boundary:** What qualifies an organized collective as an intelligent system rather than a group of agents with useful coordination? Which definitions survive adversarial cases?

**R10 — Institutional legitimacy:** Who decides aims, acceptable harms, distribution of benefits, refusal, and revision—even when an AI predicts outcomes unusually well?

**R11 — Epistemic trust:** How can non-specialists use distributed expert knowledge without demanding impossible universal self-verification or surrendering the ability to challenge claims?

**R12 — Collective verification:** Under what conditions do provenance, independently produced evidence, appropriate replication, and cross-domain review actually improve warranted reliance instead of merely multiplying citations and confident summaries?

## 16. Proposal: two bounded Coherent World experiments

Coherent World already provides the appropriate fictional recombination and consequence laboratory, **not** a validated real-world social model. Its current Neighborhood text toy has scripted roles and authored rules, **not independently reasoning autonomous agents**. The immediate frontier remains the Neighborhood v0.2 briefing, and new scenarios must not displace it.

**Experiment A: Distributed intelligence under constraint.** Give fixed specialists and one generalist partial knowledge of a neighborhood transport/access problem. Compare solo problem solving; isolated specialists; unstructured communication; structured evidence exchange and protected dissent; human–AI supported exchange. Use equal time/resource budgets where feasible. Inject an unexpected maintenance constraint, a credible minority observation, a provider failure, and an unfamiliar transfer task.

**Experiment B: Same capacities, different conditions.** Hold participant cognitive capabilities constant as appropriate while changing knowledge discoverability, accessibility, materials, permissions, incentives, communication, and centralized vs decentralized control. Observe performance and whose future options expand or contract.

A future protocol should freeze:
- task set, true/unknown/unmodeled facts, and decision stakes;
- agent information boundaries and capability budgets;
- interventions and counterfactual baselines;
- metrics for accuracy, novel-task transfer, effort, resource cost, provenance quality, harm distribution, autonomy, and resilience;
- null criteria (no group benefit, unmeasured confounding, no transfer, failure of justified inclusion);
- clearly invented causal rules, versions, preserved histories, and explicit consent boundaries.

**A simulation showing a result demonstrates only the consequences of its own stated rules.** It must not be presented as proof about humans, AI, or society without independent validation.

| People use external memory/actions to reduce task cognitive demands | Empirical/review | Supported within cognitive offloading literature; consequences vary |

| Coherence and intelligence are identical | Category error | **Do not assume**; capability, coordination, wellbeing, and legitimacy differ |

## 18. Intellectual and ethical boundaries

- Never collapse animal cognition, artificial computation, collective behavior, human experience, and ecological adaptation into a single undifferentiated thing.
- Never infer consciousness, suffering, personhood, or moral legitimacy from functional adaptation alone.
- Never assume that a person lacking jargon lacks a valid observation, or that every observation is a sufficient technical explanation.
- Never presume that a larger network is smarter, more equitable, or more resilient.
- Never treat openness as entitlement to others' private, Indigenous, community-held, or consent-sensitive knowledge.
- Avoid the assumption that individual self-sufficiency is the target; support interdependence without domination.
- Preserve meaningful uncertainty, dissent, contested classifications, and the ability to revise the research itself.
- Measure the burden of review and maintenance; preserving every thought in every project is itself a failure mode.

## 20. Preserve what gave rise to the inquiry

The October 2026 working dialogue moved through a sequence that must not vanish into polished exposition:

1. **Animal intelligence → artificial intelligence → alien / augmented / collective / ecological / emergent / distributed intelligence.** These labels mix origins, organization, capabilities, and relationships; they are not a single taxonomy.
2. **An animal category includes humans.** Human intellectual achievements are also cultural, collective, and ecological histories, not demonstrations of individual omniscience.
3. **"General intelligence" sounds impossibly dense if it means knowing everything.** Separate generalizable *learning and adaptation* from encyclopedic knowledge; universal intelligence has its own technical meaning.
4. **The resourceful generalist troubleshooting an unfamiliar machine.** Curiosity, systems thinking, metacognition, creativity, experiment, criticism, learning, and navigation of external resources; connections must survive verification.
5. **Cognitive offloading describes human–AI collaboration.** Distinguish useful delegation, shared inquiry, learning, overreliance, and loss of agency.
6. **No individual understands an entire complex problem.** Distinguish inevitable partiality from preventable failures to combine relevant evidence.
7. **Intelligence as capacity, process, and effective accomplishment.** Context, bodily conditions, accessible tools, money/time, institutional permission, and material support change observed performance.
8. **Intelligence as infrastructure.** Knowledge's existence, discoverability, comprehension, evaluation, actionability, and repair are different barriers.
9. **Transactive memory.** One useful ability is knowing where knowledge lives and how to route a question there; that itself can fail.
10. **Epistemic injustice.** Not only who can speak, but whose testimony is believed, whose experiences can be named, and whose evidence can alter a system.
11. **Intelligence changes its own selection and enabling environments.** Feedback can make capability more reachable or trap people in dependence.
12. **Animal and collective precedents.** Social learning and coordinated behavior can exist without one omniscient mind; do not anthropomorphize.
13. **An intelligence commons.** Shared capacity with plural governance, consent, privacy, maintenance, and meaningful alternatives—not maximal collection.
14. **UNESCO/open-science tension with proprietary AI.** Normative recommendations, independent transparency measurement, actual safety/privacy tradeoffs, and the distribution of access.
15. **Coherent World experiments.** Compare structured cooperation and environmental enabling conditions; seek counterexamples and null results.
16. **Interdependence, not compulsory independence.** Assistance can support agency; dominating dependency is a separate problem.
17. **The recurring question:** Not only what an intelligence can do, but what kinds of environments allow diverse intelligences and people to develop, act, and flourish.

**Working formulations to preserve, not declare established findings:**

> Intelligence without omniscience.  
> Nobody needs to know everything; someone needs to be able to find, question, and combine the knowledge that matters.  
> A group can collectively have the relevant information and still fail to use it.  
> The conditions for exercising intelligence matter alongside individual capacities.  
> A civilization's peak computational intelligence is not the same as intellectual opportunity for its inhabitants.  
> Better coordination need not require a universally capable or universally legitimate center.  
> Offload burdens without unnecessarily offloading judgment and agency.  
> Capability does not confer legitimacy.  
> Contrast before convergence.  
> What kind of world allows intelligences to flourish?

These are preservation cues for future research and prose review; they are **not** quotations attributed to identified private individuals or evidence of novelty.

## 22. Current frontier: warranted reliance across imperfect knowers

This inquiry has reached a natural **pause-and-test frontier**, not the end of the research. Our next step is **not** a new master theory or automatic propagation to every Root Sequence project. It is a set of discriminating questions:

- When an individual cannot personally verify a specialized claim, what combination of evidence, expert trust, independent review, and correction makes reliance reasonable for that particular task?
- How can a source-routing system distinguish genuine independent corroboration from multiple derivatives of one unverified report?
- Does structured multi-agent discussion improve *unique information elicitation* under controlled budgets, or merely add words, time, and compute?
- When is an AI explanation actually **verifiable**, as opposed to fluent, plausible, and reassuring?
- Can an accessible collective knowledge system preserve uncertainty, dissent, source provenance, consent, and the right to challenge its own questions?
- Who has the power to redesign the epistemic infrastructure when its categories, incentives, ranking, or authority allocation stop serving its participants?
- When do verification processes themselves impose disproportionate labor, exclusion, surveillance, or latency?
- What would a competent native-field baseline discover without this interdisciplinary synthesis, and what non-obvious correction would the synthesis add?

**Provisional priority:** compare one bounded hidden-profile/source-dependence case against an established information-sharing protocol and competent ordinary review. Predeclare assumptions and failure criteria before examining outcomes; separate model behavior from real-world empirical evidence. The [2026 HiddenBench study](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/li26ej.html) offers a relevant prior benchmark, not a source of universal constants.

**Current homes:** the core question, research sources, and original conceptual path remain under this research directory; complementary mechanisms stay in their existing conceptual homes. The private [Coherent World paper experiment proposal](https://github.com/Root-Sequence/coherent-world/pull/22) can stress-test bounded fictional translations **only after** the established Neighborhood v0.2 briefing frontier. Appropriate local applications to Community Infrastructure, Coherent Computing, Being Human(e), Liberated Intelligence, and other projects are prospective, not automatically authorized or already implemented.

**Preservation discipline:** keep the generative question, counterarguments, and revision history available; do not copy the whole body of work into every repo, or treat document count as intellectual progress. A useful partial answer may remain open without becoming another compulsory maintenance surface. No new project, product, governance policy, world canon, or declared scientific breakthrough is implied by this research map.
