AI Commons investigates how AI-related capabilities could become accessible, accountable, and collectively maintainable without requiring corporate ownership, investor returns, compulsory participation, or unaccountable centralized control.

It connects evidence about existing institutions with alternative designs, speculative scenarios, and bounded real-world experiments. Its purpose is to identify arrangements people can use, question, adapt, refuse, and sustain. It does not establish one universal AI institution.

Imagined futures may generate research questions and design requirements. They do not establish feasibility, legitimacy, or safety by themselves.

## 1. Scope: begin with the capability people need

[Intelligence Commons](intelligence-commons.md) owns the broader inquiry into knowledge, libraries, expertise, learning, tools, and cooperation. This companion focuses on AI research, models, computing, assistance, evaluation, maintenance, and governance. The parent inquiry should remain useful without AI.

For each proposed service, describe the need before choosing the technology. Compare AI with conventional software, documentation, human support, and changes to the institution creating the difficulty. Communication assistance might help someone express a need; a simpler form or an accessible human service might remove the difficulty altogether. Several supports may be necessary at once.

The normative target is useful assistance without compulsory dependence. Success might mean less AI, less autonomy, or a smaller institution. An AI commons is not automatically public data, unrestricted model release, an incorporated nonprofit, or an artificial collective mind. [Liberated Intelligence](https://github.com/Root-Sequence/liberated-intelligence) retains its focused inquiry into agency, delegation, ownership, and non-domination. Relationships among intelligences, including AIC, are distinct from shared infrastructure.

## 2. Three questions that need separate answers

| Question | Evidence or decision needed | Failure that another dimension cannot excuse |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Technical reliability | Task-specific testing, error analysis, security review, and operating limits | A helpful system invents a service requirement or leaks a private note. |
| Legitimate authority | A defensible, bounded authorization process involving affected parties and rights | An accurate system performs surveillance its operator had no right to request. |
| Institutional accountability | Independent challenge, correction, remedy, removal, and continuity arrangements | A provider acknowledges harm but cannot be compelled to repair it. |

These are analytical distinctions for this proposal. NIST's AI RMF 1.0 supplies an existing risk-management route through GOVERN, MAP, MEASURE, and MANAGE, including lifecycle review and consideration of viable non-AI alternatives.[^nist] It does not validate our institutional design or settle the technical alignment of highly autonomous systems.

Proposed safeguards include scoped permissions enforced outside a model's generated reasoning, staged deployment, independent evaluation, incident response, and a usable fallback. Their effectiveness must be demonstrated in the intended environment. A shutdown control alone does not establish containment, and institutional virtue cannot certify a model's behavior.

## 3. Compare ownership through actual decision rights

For every arrangement, identify who supplies resources, appoints and removes operators, chooses research, approves uses, controls records, hears appeals, and can stop or replace a service. Beneficiaries and affected nonusers need a route to challenge decisions. Expertise, funding, and maintenance are relevant contributions; none should confer unlimited authority.

| Candidate arrangement | Potential advantage to investigate | Countervailing failure to test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Central public institute | Pooled specialist capacity, continuity, and broad access commitments | Political budget retaliation, surveillance demands, bureaucratic exclusion, one point of failure |
| Local cooperative provision | Close knowledge of needs and replaceable local decisions | Majority exclusion, weak specialist capacity, unequal local resources, unpaid administration |
| Federated public/community institutions | Shared expensive resources with bounded local authority | Slow response, opaque interinstitutional power, incompatible permissions, harms exported across boundaries |
| Human and conventional-software provision | Lower model-specific exposure and potentially clearer responsibility | Insufficient staffing, inaccessible procedures, discretionary gatekeeping, dependence on individual staff |

These are hypotheses, not findings about every institution of each type. Compare equally competent versions before varying resources and administrative capacity. A federation must be allowed to lose the comparison.

Proposed decision separation: infrastructure stewards maintain services; researchers investigate; evaluators assess risk; service institutions authorize bounded uses; independently constituted review can contest both operators and governors. Appointments, removal, conflicts, emergency powers, review deadlines, and who pays for review need explicit local rules. A majority cannot simply vote away individual protections or authorize harm to outsiders. Participation should be compensated and accessible, with asynchronous routes and delegation that can be revoked; self-government must not require endless meetings.

## 4. Independence has several layers

| Layer | Proposed test | What remains unresolved after passing it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mission independence | Can a sponsor be refused and an unwelcome result published without surrendering the agenda? | The service may still rely on proprietary tools. |
| Operational independence | Can essential support continue after losing an API, host, administrator, or model provider? | Replacement hardware and energy may remain concentrated. |
| Material independence | Can equipment, energy, repair, manufacturing, and supported labor be sustained without one supplier's unaccountable leverage? | Scarcity, ecological burdens, and conflicts over allocation remain. |

Interdependence does not disappear. The inquiry concerns where dependence becomes unaccountable leverage, and which alternatives are materially feasible. A transitional service using a proprietary model should name that dependency and its exit costs. It should not claim complete independence because the server or institution is publicly owned.

## 5. Resources, livelihoods, and the ability to stop

A proposed resource ledger should include supported labor, compensation or material provision, training, accessibility and care work, independent evaluation, compute, electricity, water, networking, hardware replacement, supply chains, manufacturing and repair, security, incident response, reserves, and retirement. Record local and upstream burdens, uncertainty, and competing uses. An empty water estimate is an evidence gap, not zero consumption. A volunteer's time is a cost even when the budget records no payment.

Possible funding hypotheses include multiyear public allocations, contributions from participating institutions, or direct commitments of labor, equipment, and energy. Each creates its own accountability problem: who can withhold support, under what process, and with what effect on dissent? Funding diversified across institutions may still share one political or commercial dependency.

One design candidate separates evaluation budgets and staff continuity from launch approvals or deployment volume. Researchers could publish a negative result or suspend work while retaining support for replication, repair, documentation, and transition. Reviewers would remain subject to independent performance review, conflicts rules, appeals, and removal for misconduct. Protection from retaliation should not become permanent immunity or a requirement to fund every project forever.

The revealing cases are costly refusals: rejecting a restrictive grant, delaying a popular launch, retiring an unsafe tool, or concluding that a non-AI service is adequate. Specify how people obtain essential support and maintainers retain a livelihood during those choices. These are requirements to investigate, not promises that a budget label will achieve them.

## 6. Access, permission, refusal, and release

A proposed communication service should preserve original words alongside separately identified generated interpretations. Correcting an interpretation must not overwrite the source or silently become consent. Drafting does not authorize sending. Service access does not authorize training, advertising, employment assessment, or surveillance reuse of personal notes.

Meaningful exit includes usable export, corrections, source attribution, retained permissions, replacement assistance, and an accessible non-AI route. Record the time, costs, service delays, and help required. An alternative that exists on paper but lacks staffing does not satisfy this design goal. Withdrawal cannot undo every prior disclosure; deletion limits and downstream copies must remain visible.

Openness should support inspectable claims, reproducible evaluation, and appropriate reuse. Private records remain permission-bound. For potentially dangerous capabilities, any restriction needs a specified risk, proportionate scope, independent scrutiny, appeal, and review date. Neither unrestricted distribution nor permanent exclusive control follows automatically from calling something a commons. Legal enforceability and effective technical controls require separate investigation.

## 7. Inspected precedents: components rather than complete models

The Swiss AI Initiative's own public description reports ETH Domain support and CSCS compute allocations, and explicitly lists industry partners.[^swiss] This establishes an institutional example of shared AI research resources. It does not establish donor independence, public control over decisions, audited resource flows, or a fully noncommercial supply chain.

NSF's archived NAIRR-OC solicitation describes the NAIRR Pilot as a public-private initiative connecting researchers and educators to computing, datasets, software, models, and expertise.[^nairr] This is a resource-sharing precedent and a useful contrast: broad research access can coexist with commercial dependencies. A program description does not establish the operational terms of each contributing resource or participants' practical ability to leave.

Neither example demonstrates this proposal. A next evidence pass should inspect governance instruments, resource agreements, publication conditions, independent complaints, labor arrangements, and unsuccessful or retired services. Missing here are evaluated cooperative AI cases, affected-user evidence, a budget comparison, ecological accounting, and a technical safety literature review. Those gaps prevent a ranking of the institutional alternatives.

## 8. Transition research: move backward and test forward

The Government Office for Science describes backcasting as working from a preferred future toward the present.[^futures] Our proposed extension pairs it with forward tests and permits the destination itself to change:

**Desirable condition → necessary supports → present constraints → smallest useful experiment → observed consequences → revised pathway and destination.**

Keep several futures in view: a small local service with shared evaluation, a public institute with strong exit arrangements, and a federation. For each, ask what people gain, who bears burdens, and what becomes impossible. Do not select assumptions after seeing which arrangement wins.

### Proposed first present-day test: portability of communication assistance

Use synthetic notes and a fixed task packet with two replaceable tools and a conventional-software/non-AI workflow. No participant recruitment or real private notes are authorized by this seed.

1. Preserve original notes, a generated interpretation, a correction, a source reference, and explicit permissions as distinct records. Include an ambiguous request, an incorrect service claim, and a deliberate refusal.
2. Change tools and disable the first provider. Examine whether the replacement can recover records and permissions without the old administrator's goodwill or an active paid account.
3. Perform the same tasks through the non-AI workflow. Record usable outputs, errors, clarification needs, staff effort, costs, delay, and loss of information separately.
4. Test sending and reuse boundaries. Stop on unauthorized transmission or permission loss; preserve a minimal synthetic failure trace and repair the design before continuing.
5. Report what moved successfully and what failed. A successful export is evidence about that fixture and tool version, not long-term independence or benefits for disabled participants.

A later participant study needs a separate protocol, voluntary consent, privacy and accessibility provisions, appropriate review, supported participation, and credible comparison conditions. Practical work belongs with an explicitly adopting applied project; this note assigns none a new deliverable.

It is an AI-assisted review draft, not a replacement commons charter or evidence that an institutional design works.

Coherent World is a private speculative/consequence workspace. It may transform these public questions into alternative resource and authority arrangements. Its findings would return as proposed requirements or missing mechanisms, not empirical evidence or policy authorization. Private adaptations are not required reading for this note; no unpublished narrative details are included here.

The next bounded questions are:

- Which publication and budget rules permit an evaluator to refuse its own sponsor, and how are evaluators themselves challenged?
- What survives provider loss in the synthetic portability test, and who does the recovery work?
- How much non-AI fallback capacity is required under normal demand and an outage, including unequal access needs?
- Which technical failure, governance abuse, or maintenance cost would make a simpler service preferable?

Use [Epistemic Contrast](../methods/epistemic-contrast.md), [Deliberative Inquiry](../methods/deliberative-inquiry.md), and [Learning to Revise the Rules](learning-to-revise-the-rules.md) for deeper methods.
