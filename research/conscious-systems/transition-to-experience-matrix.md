# CS-Q06–07 — Transition to Experience Matrix

**Document role:** Third bounded research pass  
**Status:** v0.1-dev; AI-assisted, author review pending  
**Questions:** What distinguishes nonconscious processing from experienced processing? Is the transition graded, abrupt, or scale-dependent?  
**Evidence status:** Provisional synthesis and theory-specific failure conditions grounded in selected studies; not a validated detector of consciousness

## Provisional answer

There is no established single event that converts processing into experience. The strongest current comparison instead suggests a **layered transition problem**:

1. sensory or other first-order information is processed;
2. recurrent interaction may stabilize and organize content;
3. some content may become broadly available across the system;
4. the system may represent its own relation to that content;
5. the larger system must retain enough differentiated causal organization to support any of these processes.

Different theories assign consciousness to different items in that sequence—or reject the sequence as the wrong level of description. The same observation can therefore be a proposed constituent under one theory, an enabling condition under another, and a downstream consequence under a third.

The present evidence does not justify choosing one layer as the universal transition. It does justify rejecting three shortcuts:

- **More activity is not automatically more consciousness.** Activity can be feedforward, recurrent, widespread, pathological, task-related, or report-related.
- **A threshold in a report is not automatically a threshold in experience.** Graded evidence can become a binary choice at a later decision stage.
- **A reliable marker is not automatically the mechanism.** A correlate can be necessary in one task, downstream in another, or a proxy for an enabling capacity.

## What each approach says changes

| Approach | Proposed transition | Expected shape | Most diagnostic contrast | What its usual marker cannot establish alone |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Global neuronal workspace (GNW)** | Selected content undergoes recurrent amplification or ignition and becomes broadly available to multiple systems.[^mashour] | Often modeled as nonlinear or bifurcation-like conscious access after graded accumulation | Local sensory processing without broad availability versus late, sustained, distributed availability without requiring an immediate report | A P3b, prefrontal signal, or widespread activation can reflect decision, task relevance, working memory, or report rather than experience |
| **Recurrent processing theory (RPT)** | Feedforward processing becomes phenomenal content when recurrent interaction occurs within sensory systems; wider broadcast supports access and report.[^lamme][^fahrenfort] | Content-specific emergence after local feedback; it need not be one global all-or-none event | Preserved feedforward discrimination with disrupted local recurrence, compared with local recurrence when content is experienced | Recurrence is widespread in the brain and can support attention, segmentation, or prediction without being sufficient for experience |
| **Higher-order theories (HOT)** | A first-order state becomes conscious when the system represents itself as being in that state.[^brown] | Relational: first-order strength and higher-order confidence can each vary, while the relevant relation may be present or absent | Matched first-order performance with different subjective awareness, confidence, or metacognitive access | Prefrontal or metacognitive activity can be required for judging and reporting experience rather than for having it |
| **Integrated information theory (IIT)** | Experience is identical to a system's maximally irreducible intrinsic cause-effect structure, not added at a later processing stage.[^tononi] | In principle graded in quantity and structured in quality; the relevant system boundary is theory-dependent | Systems or states matched in input-output function but differing in intrinsic causal organization | Perturbational complexity, connectivity, synchrony, and behavioral capacity are empirical proxies, not direct measurements of the formal quantity or proof of experience |
| **Predictive/interoceptive processing** | Hierarchical generative inference, precision weighting, and bodily prediction shape perceptual content, affect, and selfhood.[^seth] | Usually graded and context-sensitive | Change priors, precision, or bodily signals while holding sensory input approximately stable | Predictive inference occurs in both conscious and nonconscious processing; without an added condition it does not specify the transition to experience |

This is not a balanced five-way tournament. GNW, RPT, and HOT make relatively direct rival claims about the transition. IIT proposes a different identity and system-organization claim. Predictive and interoceptive processing currently contribute more clearly to an account of **what is experienced** than to a general criterion for **whether experience occurs**.

## Shared-case matrix

Each row holds the case approximately constant and asks what would do explanatory work under each approach. These are theory-derived interpretations, not findings that the cited studies uniquely establish.

| Shared case | GNW | RPT | HOT | IIT | Predictive/interoceptive |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Masked stimulus: early processing survives but the target is reported unseen** | The feedforward signal did not reach stable, system-wide availability | Masking interrupted the recurrent sensory interaction needed for phenomenal content | The first-order state was not represented in the required higher-order way, or the higher-order state misrepresented it as absent | The stimulus did not produce the intrinsic cause-effect change corresponding to that content, even if it altered local activity | The evidence or precision assigned to the target remained insufficient to stabilize the perceptual hypothesis; this alone does not explain why the result was nonconscious |
| **Near-threshold target: similar discrimination, different visibility judgment** | One trial crossed an ignition or availability threshold and the other did not | Local recurrence differed despite similar forced-choice performance | The contrast directly targets the relation between first-order discrimination and higher-order awareness | Subjective difference requires a difference in intrinsic causal structure, though ordinary imaging cannot establish that structure | Priors, evidence, and precision can yield different posterior confidence or content from similar performance; an added consciousness criterion remains necessary |
| **Clearly visible but task-irrelevant stimulus with no immediate report** | Content should still be available beyond sensory processing, but report-specific P3b or executive activity need not appear | Local recurrent content can occur without global task access; this is a favorable test case | A higher-order representation should occur even if it is not converted into a report | Report demand should not determine whether the relevant causal structure exists | Perceptual inference can proceed without action or report; this does not distinguish conscious from nonconscious inference by itself |
| **Dreaming or anaesthetic unresponsiveness followed by an experience report** | Internally generated content can enter a workspace without sensory connection or motor output | Recurrent content-generating activity can persist despite loss of responsiveness and external access | Dream content must be available to an appropriate higher-order representation during the episode, even if later memory is incomplete | The brain can retain sufficiently differentiated and integrated causal organization while disconnected from the environment | Internally generated predictions can dominate attenuated sensory input, making this a strong content-generation case but not a complete transition test |
| **Unresponsiveness with a complex versus simple response to direct cortical perturbation** | Complexity may reflect preserved capacity for distributed availability, but it is not specific to GNW | A distributed response may include recurrent processing, but the measure does not isolate sensory recurrence | The measure does not demonstrate that a higher-order representation occurred | Differentiated, integrated responses are predicted to track capacity for consciousness; empirical complexity remains a proxy rather than formal integrated information | Global response complexity does not by itself identify predictive content, precision, or interoceptive inference |
| **Sustained suprathreshold perception in the 2025 adversarial test** | Predicted content-specific prefrontal ignition at onset and offset; limited prefrontal representation and little offset ignition challenged the tested implementation | Local recurrence was not the preregistered target, so the result does not directly decide RPT | Some higher-order variants share prefrontal commitments, but the study did not test the full theory family | Predicted sustained posterior content and synchronization; content was sustained in posterior areas, but the missing sustained posterior synchronization challenged the tested implementation | The study can constrain where content is represented, but was not a direct test of a predictive-processing transition criterion |

## Empirical anchors and their limits

### 1. Masking separates feedforward processing from later dynamics

Fahrenfort, Scholte, and Lamme reported preserved early extrastriate activation for seen and unseen texture targets, while EEG components associated with reentrant processing were absent under effective masking.[^fahrenfort] This is compatible with RPT's central contrast.

It does not establish that the missing signal was experience itself. Masking can alter stimulus strength, attention, segmentation, decision evidence, and confidence. A recurrent signal can also be necessary for constructing a reportable object without being sufficient for phenomenality.

### 2. Subjective visibility can vary while forced-choice performance is matched

Lau and Passingham used two masking conditions with similar discrimination accuracy but different visibility reports and found a difference in mid-dorsolateral prefrontal activity.[^lau] This is useful for HOT because it separates first-order task performance from a subjective judgment.

The inference is disputed: matched percent correct does not guarantee matched evidence distributions or decision criteria, and a prefrontal difference may support introspection or response selection. The study is a candidate contrast, not proof that prefrontal higher-order representation creates experience.[^criterion]

### 3. Report requirements contaminate proposed correlates

**Methods-review qualification:** The [IA-01 extraction](pitts-2014-extraction.md) supplies category-specific estimates and limits the broad summary below. This comparison does not establish absence of global availability.

Pitts, Metzler, and Hillyard crossed awareness with task relevance. A mid-latency posterior negativity was associated with visible stimuli even when they were not immediately reported, whereas a robust P3b appeared with task relevance and report access.[^pitts] This weakens any simple identification of the P3b with consciousness.

It does not cleanly vindicate RPT. The authors noted that the remaining posterior component could reflect object-based attention, and classifying a stimulus as consciously seen without a trial-level report requires its own inference.

Sergent and colleagues later modeled trial variability in an auditory no-report condition and found late bifurcation-like activity around 250–300 ms that predicted sampled reports; explicit report added executive activity, and P300 was not required.[^sergent] This supports a report-independent GNW-style transition more strongly than a P3b criterion, while still depending on reports sampled from some trials to validate the model.

### 4. Unresponsiveness does not fix the experiential state

In a within-subject study of 39 healthy men, 69.7% of interviews after dexmedetomidine- or propofol-induced unresponsiveness and 64.4% after NREM-sleep awakenings included reported experiences; most were disconnected and dream-like.[^valli] Reports were collected after arousal, so forgotten experiences remain invisible and the timing of remembered content within an unresponsive interval is not exact.

Sarasso and colleagues applied TMS-EEG during propofol, xenon, and ketamine unresponsiveness in 18 volunteers.[^sarasso] Propofol produced local low-complexity responses, xenon global but stereotyped low-complexity responses, and ketamine complex wake-like responses; vivid disconnected dreams were reported after ketamine but not the other agents. This supports separating responsiveness from capacity for experience and shows why both integration and differentiation matter. It does not make perturbational complexity a direct readout of experience, and absent recall after propofol or xenon is not proof that no experience occurred.

### 5. Direct theory comparison can challenge both sides

The Cogitate Consortium preregistered divergent predictions from specified biological implementations of GNW and IIT, testing 256 participants with fMRI, MEG, and intracranial EEG.[^cogitate] The results supported some predictions of each but challenged both: the tested IIT account lacked its predicted sustained posterior synchronization, while the tested GNW account showed limited representation of some conscious dimensions in prefrontal cortex and little predicted offset ignition.

This is stronger than comparing theories after results are known. It still tests particular implementations and measurements rather than eliminating the broader theory families or settling the metaphysical identity claims behind them.

## What would count against each approach?

| Approach | Result that would lower confidence | Result that would not be enough |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **GNW** | Repeated, well-supported content experience with intact local recurrent processing but no broad availability, ignition-like dynamics, or capacity for flexible cross-system use | Absence of P3b or motor report alone |
| **RPT** | Verified perceptual content persisting after a selective causal disruption of the relevant local recurrent interaction, with feedforward processing and alternative recurrence measured | Finding recurrence in an unconscious condition, because the theory requires the right content-specific recurrence rather than recurrence anywhere |
| **HOT** | Experience persisting while the relevant higher-order representation is selectively abolished—not merely confidence, reporting, memory, or executive control | Reduced metacognitive accuracy or prefrontal activity alone |
| **IIT** | A controlled change in the system's intrinsic irreducible cause-effect structure without the predicted change in experience, or vice versa | Failure of one complexity, connectivity, or synchrony proxy |
| **Predictive/interoceptive account as a complete theory** | Matched predictive inference and precision dynamics across experienced and nonexperienced processing with no additional criterion available | A case where priors fail to alter one particular content; predictive accounts permit context- and hierarchy-specific effects |

The asymmetry matters. Some of these failure conditions are currently more operational than others. A theory whose decisive quantity cannot yet be measured may remain conceptually live while contributing less to near-term discrimination.

## CS-Q07: abrupt, graded, or scale-dependent?

The evidence supports a mixed answer:

- **Inputs and evidence accumulation can be graded.** Stimulus strength, precision, confidence, and recurrent activity vary continuously.
- **Network dynamics can bifurcate.** GNW-style models and some trial-level data show late high- and low-activity regimes rather than one smooth average.[^sergent]
- **Reports impose thresholds.** A yes/no response turns graded internal evidence into a discrete observation.
- **Contents can differ dimensionally.** Clarity, stability, spatial extent, affect, self-relatedness, access, and later memory need not change together.
- **State capacity and particular content are different scales.** A brain may retain capacity for disconnected experience while losing responsiveness to one target or to the environment.

Therefore “Is consciousness graded or all-or-none?” is probably underspecified. Future work should identify **which dimension, at which spatial and temporal scale, under which operational measure** appears graded or discontinuous.

## Best next discriminating design

The most tractable first contest is **RPT versus GNW versus HOT** in perceptual consciousness. IIT and predictive/interoceptive accounts should contribute measurements and constraints without pretending that the same experiment directly tests their complete claims.

### Phase 1 — observational separation

**Fifth-pass correction:** The following dimensions are a design sketch, not a fully independent factorial manipulation. Visibility is an outcome, expected reports alter task relevance, and the proposed neural measures are not yet validated selective readouts of each mechanism. See the [inference audit](inference-audit.md#repairs-to-our-proposed-design) before interpreting the prediction table as a discriminating experiment.

Use a within-person visual task crossing:

1. **visibility:** strongly masked, individually calibrated near-threshold, and clearly visible;
2. **task relevance:** target relevant versus irrelevant to the current task;
3. **evidence channel:** immediate graded report, unpredictable delayed probe, and no immediate report;
4. **memory:** later occurrence, content, source, recognition, and confidence tests.

Record time-resolved activity capable of distinguishing early feedforward processing, local recurrent dynamics, late distributed availability, and report-related activity. Fit individual-trial models rather than only averaging “seen” and “unseen” trials.

### Phase 2 — causal perturbation

Only after the observational signatures are replicated, use a preregistered perturbation that aims separately at:

- the timing of sensory feedback or recurrence;
- later frontoparietal availability;
- metacognitive evaluation.

No perturbation is perfectly selective. The design must therefore measure sensory discrimination, attention, confidence, working memory, motor preparation, and later memory rather than interpreting any reduction in report as a loss of experience.

### Divergent predictions to preregister

| Result | RPT | GNW | HOT |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Local recurrent content without broad availability, followed by a valid surprise report | Favors | Challenges a strong necessity claim | Compatible only if the required higher-order representation was also present |
| Broad availability with no local recurrent content representation | Challenges | Could favor, if the broadcast genuinely contains the percept | Compatible if higher-order representation targets an adequate first-order state; otherwise unclear |
| Matched sensory representation and access, but awareness changes with higher-order manipulation | Needs an alternative explanation | Needs an alternative explanation if broad availability is truly matched | Favors |
| Confidence changes while content, access, and later memory remain stable | Compatible; confidence is downstream | Compatible; confidence is downstream | Challenges versions that equate the manipulated confidence process with the constitutive higher-order state |

## Decision after the third pass

**Fourth-pass qualification:** A surprise report may introduce retrieval, reconstruction, and global availability after the original target interval. It cannot by itself establish earlier experience without broadcast. The [system boundary pass](system-boundary-matrix.md#correction-to-the-previous-proposed-experiment) adds this temporal limit; the prediction table above is a conditional proposal, not an operationally decisive test.

1. **Answer CS-Q06 provisionally:** no unique transition has been established. Local recurrence, broad availability, higher-order representation, and system-level complexity are serious but differently scoped candidates.
2. **Answer CS-Q07 provisionally:** the transition can appear graded or abrupt depending on the dimension, scale, and measurement. Binary report is insufficient to decide.
3. **Treat GNW, RPT, and HOT as the first directly discriminating comparison.** Do not force IIT or predictive processing into predictions they do not presently make at the same level.
4. **Reject P3b, recurrence, prefrontal activity, or complexity as stand-alone consciousness detectors.** Each requires a theory-specific bridge and rival explanations.
5. **Move next to CS-Q16:** state the candidate system boundary for each layer before proposing a full experiment.
