# CS-Q01 — Definition Stress Test

**Document role:** First bounded research pass<br>
**Status:** v0.1-dev; AI-assisted, author review pending<br>
**Question:** What is the smallest definition of subjective experience that does not depend on report, intelligence, selfhood, or memory?<br>
**Evidence status:** Conceptual decision supported by selected empirical edge cases; not a measurement standard or finding

## Starting proposal

> **Subjective experience is a state in which there is something it is like for a system at that moment, whether or not the system can report, interpret, or later remember it.**

This proposal identifies the target phenomenon without building one favored mechanism, behavior, cognitive ability, or biological implementation into the definition. It is intentionally thinner than a theory of consciousness.

## First correction: state and moment are too restrictive

The initial wording silently assumes that experience is an instantaneous state. Consciousness might instead require a process extended across an interval, and determining the minimum interval is itself an open question (CS-Q11). The revised target is:

> **Subjective experience is present when, during some interval, there is something it is like for a system—whether or not it can report, interpret, control, or later remember that experience.**

This remains a phenomenological target definition, not an operational test. “There is something it is like” points to what needs explanation; it does not explain how experience arises or tell an observer how to detect it.

## What the definition commits us to

- **Report is evidence, not a requirement.** Inability to speak, move, or follow a command does not by itself exclude experience.
- **Memory is downstream.** Later retrieval can provide evidence about an earlier experience, but later forgetting does not retroactively erase it.
- **Processing is not sufficient.** Detection, learning, physiological response, or information use can occur without establishing that anything was experienced.
- **Self-reflection is optional.** An experience need not include the thought that *I am having an experience*.
- **Intelligence is separate.** Planning, language, problem solving, and adaptive behavior neither define nor automatically demonstrate experience.
- **Implementation remains open.** The definition does not settle whether biology is necessary or whether an artificial system could qualify.

## Edge-case stress test

| Case | What the definition says | What the evidence can establish | Remaining problem |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ordinary waking report | Usually include the reported interval as experienced. | Immediate, specific report is strong evidence when task and demand confounds are controlled. | Report can misclassify, reconstruct, or reshape experience. |
| Dreaming | Include a dream if experience occurred during sleep, even if externally disconnected. | Awakenings and reports can correlate reported dreaming with preceding neural activity.[^siclari] | A forgotten dream is indistinguishable from no dream by later report alone. |
| Drug-induced unresponsiveness | Do not equate absent response with absent experience. | Participants have reported internally generated experiences from experimentally induced unresponsive periods.[^radek] | Arousal and interviewing may select experiences that were close enough to recovery to be encoded. |
| Amnesia | Permit experience without later declarative recollection. | Durable perceptual learning can remain despite severe impairment in consciously remembering the material.[^squire] | An implicit trace proves prior processing or learning, not that the earlier event was experienced. |
| Masked or subliminal perception | Do not infer experience from above-chance processing alone. | Stimulus information can guide responses and traverse cortical regions on trials reported as unseen; later dynamics differ for seen trials.[^salti] | “Unseen” may combine genuinely nonconscious processing with weak experiences misclassified by a thresholded report. |
| Infants and nonverbal humans | Do not exclude them because report or mature self-reflection is absent. | Neural and behavioral similarities can support graded inference; infant work has reported delayed neural responses interpreted as candidate markers of perceptual consciousness.[^kouider] | The inference imports assumptions from verbal adults and cannot supply direct confirmation. |
| Nonhuman animals | Do not make language or human-like intelligence prerequisites. | Converging behavioral, neural, pharmacological, and evolutionary evidence can alter confidence. | No single behavior, physiological response, or complexity score establishes experience; species differences weaken simple analogy. |
| Artificial systems | Do not exclude or include them by substrate alone. | Theory-specific causal and computational properties could supply conditional evidence. | Fluent self-report, capability, and human-like behavior can be generated without an accepted link to experience; there is no independent ground truth. |
| Split, dissociated, or distributed systems | The definition allows experience but does not determine how many subjects exist. | Independent access, integration, interference, and intervention patterns may constrain possible boundaries. | “A system” already assumes the unit whose experience is in question, risking circularity. |
| Alleged contentless awareness | The definition does not require sensory or conceptual content. | Reports and state comparisons can motivate the hypothesis. | “Something it is like” may itself count as minimal content, making the category unstable. |

## Failures exposed by the stress test

The revised definition survives the edge cases as a **target**, but it does not yet serve as a scientific criterion. Four unresolved assumptions remain:

1. **Subject boundary:** What makes some interacting processes one candidate experiencer rather than several—or none?
2. **Temporal boundary:** What duration and integration are required for one experience?
3. **Evidence bridge:** Why should a report, behavior, neural pattern, or causal organization raise confidence that experience occurred?
4. **Weak-experience problem:** How can a study distinguish no experience from a faint, unstable, unattended, or rapidly forgotten experience?

These are research questions rather than reasons to define experience as reportability. Folding the available evidence into the definition would make systems without that evidence unconscious by stipulation and prevent the question from being investigated.

## Evidence policy

No observation in this program should be called a direct measurement of subjective experience. Each assessment should instead record:

1. the target interval and proposed experiencer;
2. first-person evidence, if available;
3. behavior and task performance;
4. physiological or neural measurements;
5. memory of occurrence, memory of content, and implicit traces separately;
6. the theory that connects those observations to experience;
7. plausible nonconscious and weak-experience alternatives;
8. what result would reduce confidence in the inference.

Evidence can converge without becoming certainty. Ethical precautions may be warranted at a lower evidential threshold than a scientific claim that consciousness has been demonstrated.

## Decision after the first pass

**Retain the revised definition as the provisional target for CS-Q01. Do not treat it as an operational definition or completed answer.**

It succeeds at preventing report, memory, responsiveness, intelligence, selfhood, and substrate from being mistaken for consciousness itself. It fails—productively—to identify the relevant system, temporal grain, or evidence bridge. Those failures route the next work to CS-Q03, CS-Q11, CS-Q16, and CS-Q26–28.

The [next bounded pass](experience-memory-evidence-matrix.md) uses CS-Q13–14 to construct an evidence matrix for four possibilities:

1. experience with content memory;
2. experience with only occurrence memory;
3. experience without later retrievable memory;
4. nonconscious processing that leaves an implicit or physical trace.

The matrix states which observations are compatible with more than one possibility rather than forcing a verdict.
