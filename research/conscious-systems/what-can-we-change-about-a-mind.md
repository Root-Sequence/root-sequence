Changing brain chemistry can change perception, attention, mood, memory, and awareness. But chemistry is not a detachable control knob: it participates in electrical signaling, metabolism, cellular maintenance, network activity, and bodily regulation. If consciousness depends on this interacting system, the next question is **which changes alter experience, which abolish it, and which leave it possible in a different form?**

This is a proposed research question, not an answer that organization alone is sufficient. A process could depend on both its organization and properties of the material realizing it. Even if some biological parts can be replaced or compensated for, it would not follow that *any* substrate could sustain experience.

## First specify what is being preserved

“Consciousness” can mean several things. This inquiry initially asks whether **there is any subjective experience at all**: whether anything is felt from the system's own perspective. It then asks separately about:

| Dimension | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Contents and character | What is experienced, and how does it feel? |
| Connectedness | Is the experience of the surrounding world, or internally generated, as in a dream? |
| Responsiveness | Can the system act or answer when prompted? |
| Recall and report | Can it later remember or describe an experience? |
| Selfhood and agency | Is there a sense of self, and can it make choices? |

These dimensions cannot stand in for one another. In an experimental sedation study, participants reported dream-like experiences associated with periods of unresponsiveness; an absence of response alone was therefore an unreliable sign that experience had ended in that setting.[^radek] Conversely, a missing later report could reflect impaired recall. We need multiple kinds of evidence, while acknowledging that none provides direct access to another system's point of view.

## What could be varied?

The practical method is to compare interventions that disturb different components of an interacting system, ask what else they change, and look for convergent results. Each row is a **proposed line of inquiry**, not a claim that one variable can be isolated cleanly.

| Candidate variation | What it might reveal | Main obstacle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Change pharmacological or sleep state | Whether different routes to altered experience share a network or temporal signature | Drugs and sleep change many processes at once; unresponsiveness is an imperfect outcome measure |
| Perturb recurrent signaling and coordination | Whether a particular interaction pattern is needed for some forms of experience | A measured correlate may be a consequence of experience, or of attention or report |
| Alter sensory connection or bodily input | Which experiences require current input from the world, and which can be internally generated | Dreaming without current external input does not show that the body or its history is irrelevant |
| Compare development, injury, and compensation | Which relationships can be reorganized while experience persists | Changes over time may recruit several compensating mechanisms |
| Compare very different nervous systems or artificial architectures | Which candidate properties generalize beyond one implementation | Experience outside report-capable humans has no agreed independent ground truth |

One useful tool is the **perturbational complexity index**: Casali and colleagues measured how richly a TMS-evoked cortical response spreads and differentiates across several human states, including wakefulness, dreaming, sleep, sedation, and disorders of consciousness.[^casali] This makes complexity a candidate *indicator* worth testing. Its ability to distinguish states in those samples does not show that the index itself, or any single abstract property it approximates, causes experience or transfers unchanged to an artificial system.

## Make the theories disagree before looking at results

For any proposed necessary condition, specify in advance: the target dimension of consciousness; the physical change; predicted effects on reports, behavior, and physiology; and at least one rival explanation. Test the proposal with different interventions and measurements. If disrupting a candidate repeatedly removes a marker, first ask whether the intervention also removed the ability to report or remember. If a candidate persists when experience appears absent, examine measurement limits and whether the candidate was specified precisely enough.

A 2025 preregistered adversarial collaboration tested divergent predictions of Integrated Information Theory and Global Neuronal Workspace Theory about visual consciousness in 256 participants using three recording methods. Some predictions received support, while central predictions of both were challenged.[^cogitate] This is a model for comparing risky predictions, not a verdict that the theories' deeper claims are settled by one experiment.

The most difficult case is an artificial system. Matching input-output behavior, verbal self-description, or a chosen complexity score would not establish equivalent causal organization or subjective experience. A credible assessment would need explicit, rival theory-dependent predictions and a careful account of what its measurements can and cannot tell us. We should keep **capability, consciousness, moral consideration, personhood, and legitimate authority** as separate judgments.

## What a useful first study would look like

1. Choose one narrow contrast, such as connected versus dream-like experience during states of impaired responsiveness.
2. Map two competing theories to different, preregisterable predictions for that contrast.
3. Identify at least two distinct measurements, the report and memory confounds, and an intervention or natural variation that could discriminate between predictions.
4. Record results that would weaken each account as well as those that would support it. Do not promote a correlation to necessity or sufficiency without an argument that survives the alternatives.

The longer-range question of substrate independence remains open. The ethical commitment is also separate from any empirical finding: uncertainty about possible artificial experience gives reason to examine welfare and avoid creating potentially harmful conditions while the evidence is incomplete. It does not grant a system authority over people, nor does it prove that a present system has experiences.

**Next research pass:** Make a compact comparison of established theories, candidate mechanisms, discriminating predictions, and known measurement failures before selecting a first test. Author review is needed for the framing, scope, and any stronger claim about what “the whole system” requires.
