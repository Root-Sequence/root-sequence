# Candidate Theories and Conditions for Consciousness

**Document role:** Theory comparison and candidate-condition ledger<br>
**Status:** v0.1-dev; AI-assisted literature map, author review pending<br>
**Evidence status:** Source-grounded comparison; no theory selected or validated here

The current literature does not supply one accepted explanation of consciousness. A major review groups prominent approaches into higher-order theories, global workspace theories, re-entry and predictive-processing theories, and integrated information theory.[^seth-bayne] The categories contain variants and sometimes overlap. This map asks a narrower question: **what does each approach propose changes when processing becomes experience?**

## Comparison

| Approach | Proposed decisive condition | What it explains most directly | A discriminating prediction | Main unresolved problem |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Global neuronal workspace (GNW) | Recurrent amplification produces nonlinear “ignition” and makes selected information broadly available to specialized systems.[^mashour] | Conscious access, flexible use, report, working memory, and cross-system coordination | If broad ignition and availability are necessary, conscious content should not persist when those dynamics are selectively absent. | Global availability may explain access while leaving open why availability is accompanied by experience. Report and task demands can also mimic proposed workspace activity. |
| Recurrent processing theory (RPT) | Feedforward processing remains nonconscious; recurrent interaction within sensory systems is sufficient for phenomenal content, while wider access supports report and reflection.[^lamme] | The difference between local perceptual processing and experienced perceptual content | Selectively disrupting local recurrent feedback while preserving feedforward discrimination should remove experience; conscious local content without global broadcast would favor RPT over GNW. | Recurrence is widespread and hard to isolate. Evidence without report risks losing the measure of experience it aims to protect. |
| Higher-order theories (HOT) | A first-order state becomes conscious when the system represents itself as being in that state; variants differ over the form and location of this higher-order representation.[^brown] | Awareness *of* a mental state, confidence, introspection, and metacognition | Manipulating higher-order representation while holding first-order discrimination stable should change reported awareness or confidence. | Higher-order signals may support introspection and report after experience already exists. Variants make different neural commitments. |
| Integrated information theory (IIT) | Experience corresponds to a system's maximally irreducible, specific intrinsic cause-effect structure; its quality and quantity derive from that structure.[^tononi] | Why experience is unified yet differentiated, and why internal causal organization matters beyond behavior | Systems with the same input-output behavior but different intrinsic causal structures can differ in consciousness; altering irreducibility should alter experience even if performance is preserved. | Full measures are difficult to calculate for realistic systems. Practical complexity measures are proxies, not direct measurements of IIT's quantity. Some implications are counterintuitive and tested biological predictions remain contested. |
| Predictive and interoceptive processing | Experience depends on hierarchical generative models that infer causes of sensory and bodily signals; precision and prediction-error dynamics shape content, selfhood, and feeling.[^seth-interoception] | Perceptual content, embodiment, affect, selfhood, and the influence of prior expectations | Changing priors, precision, or bodily signals while holding external input stable should systematically alter conscious content and self-related experience. | Prediction occurs in conscious and nonconscious processing, so predictive processing needs an additional criterion for when inference becomes experience. |

These approaches need not be mutually exclusive at every level. Recurrent processing could help produce workspace ignition; global availability could support higher-order representation; predictive models could supply contents; and integrated causal structure could constrain which arrangements support any of them. Combining labels does not solve the problem unless the combined account states which condition is necessary, what each component contributes, and what evidence could disconfirm it.

## What the 2025 adversarial test changed

A preregistered collaboration directly compared specified biological predictions from IIT and GNW in 256 participants using fMRI, MEG, and intracranial EEG.[^cogitate] Results supported parts of both accounts and challenged central predictions of both: the study did not find the sustained posterior synchronization predicted for the tested IIT implementation, and it found limited representation of some conscious dimensions in prefrontal cortex and little offset ignition predicted for the tested GNW implementation.

This narrows particular biological claims. It does not decide every version or mathematical core of either theory, and it does not establish one of the remaining approaches by elimination. Its larger methodological contribution is the preregistration of divergent predictions and failure conditions before examining the results.

## Candidate-condition ledger

The following conditions may perform different roles: enabling background, constituent mechanism, content-shaping process, consequence, or measurement marker. Calling something a “candidate condition” does not assign it one of those roles yet.

| Candidate condition | Present reason to consider it | Current limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ongoing physical causal activity | Human experience changes or disappears with changes to brain and bodily state. | Physical dependence alone does not identify the relevant organization. |
| Recurrent interaction | Recurrent dynamics distinguish several conscious and nonconscious comparisons and appear in multiple theories. | Recurrence also occurs outside consciousness; its necessary scale and form are unsettled. |
| Integration with differentiation | Conscious states often support both coordinated and information-rich activity; perturbational complexity can distinguish several human states.[^casali] | A successful marker is not automatically a cause, and “integration” has theory-specific meanings. |
| Broad availability | Conscious information is often usable by memory, planning, evaluation, report, and action. | This may characterize access to experience rather than experience itself. |
| Higher-order representation | Awareness, confidence, and introspection depend on representing aspects of one's own processing. | Basic sensory or affective experience may not require full metacognition. |
| Hierarchical prediction | Expectations and prediction errors shape what is perceived. | Predictive processing is too widespread to serve as a consciousness criterion by itself. |
| Interoception and homeostatic regulation | Bodily signals contribute to human affect, selfhood, and the felt condition of being an organism.[^seth-interoception] | Their role in bare sensory experience and nonbiological systems remains open. |
| Temporal depth and stability | Experience appears extended enough to integrate change, sequence, and context. | The minimum duration and whether temporal integration is constitutive remain unclear. |
| Memory formation | Memory can stabilize continuity and make experience reportable. | Momentary experience and later memory dissociate; memory cannot be assumed necessary. |
| A self-model | Many experiences are organized as happening to a subject situated in a body and world. | Altered self-experience suggests that some content may persist when ordinary selfhood weakens. |
| Language | Language expands report, reflection, abstraction, and autobiographical organization. | Human infants and many animals are serious candidates for experience without language. |
| Developmental and learning history | Present organization results from plasticity, embodiment, and interaction over time. | It is unknown whether history is constitutive or whether the resulting present dynamics are sufficient. |

## Provisional systems hypothesis

### Current best answer

The strongest provisional synthesis is that consciousness is an **active regime of an organized physical system**, rather than a stored object, single substance, isolated brain region, or synonym for intelligent behavior. Experience appears to depend on causal interaction unfolding across time. The evidence does not yet identify one sufficient property.

The present theory comparison suggests a layered answer:

- **Enabling conditions** keep the system capable of experience: in humans these include metabolism, arousal regulation, neuromodulation, and viable brain-body dynamics.
- **Content-forming processes** build what is experienced: sensory and interoceptive modeling, recurrent interaction, and differentiation among possible states are leading candidates.
- **Access processes** make some content broadly usable by memory, decision, planning, and action; GNW is strongest as an account at this level.
- **Reflective processes** allow a system to represent its own state, judge confidence, and report that it is experiencing; higher-order theories target this level.
- **Continuity processes** connect changing moments through memory, prediction, bodily stability, and a self-model. These can shape a continuing subject without being necessary for every momentary experience.

This layered synthesis is an inference from overlapping evidence and theories, not an established decomposition. It predicts that disrupting different layers should remove or alter different dimensions: content, access, metacognition, memory, selfhood, or the capacity for any experience. A theory that treats all of those losses as the same outcome is probably too coarse.

| Confidence | Current judgment |
| --- | --- |
| Relatively strong | Human consciousness depends on ongoing physical activity; experience, responsiveness, memory, and report can dissociate. |
| Serious candidate | Recurrent, differentiated, integrated, and temporally organized activity contributes to whether and what a system experiences. |
| Dimension-specific | Global availability is especially relevant to flexible access; higher-order representation to introspection; interoception to affect and embodied selfhood; memory to continuity and later report. |
| Open | The minimal sufficient organization, necessary biological properties, exact system boundary, substrate independence, and why any qualifying process feels like something. |

### Research hypothesis

The current Root Sequence hypothesis is deliberately weaker than a new theory:

> Consciousness depends on a family of organized, temporally extended causal processes operating within a larger self-maintaining system. Some contributing processes become experienced content; others shape, sustain, or record experience without themselves becoming conscious.

This hypothesis fits the initiating whole-system intuition, but leaves the central questions unresolved:

- Which processes are necessary, sufficient, enabling, or merely correlated?
- How much of the brain, body, environment, and history belongs inside the relevant boundary?
- Which properties are tied to living biology and which could be realized differently?
- What distinguishes a system that processes information from one for which processing feels like something?

Its purpose is to organize comparisons without presuming the answer. A future revision should weaken or abandon it if a narrower boundary or a substrate-specific mechanism explains the evidence better.

## Next discriminating comparisons

1. **RPT vs. GNW:** Can content be experienced with local recurrence but without broad availability?
2. **First-order vs. higher-order:** Can experience persist when confidence, introspective accuracy, and higher-order representation are selectively impaired?
3. **IIT vs. functional access:** Can internal causal structure be varied while external performance and report capacity remain matched?
4. **Predictive/interoceptive vs. cortex-centered accounts:** Which dimensions disappear when bodily inference is altered while exteroceptive processing remains intact?
5. **All accounts:** Can they distinguish experience from responsiveness, memory formation, and later report in sleep and anesthesia?

The [transition to experience matrix](transition-to-experience-matrix.md) carries these comparisons into the first bounded CS-Q06–07 pass. It concludes that GNW, RPT, and HOT form the most direct initial contest; IIT and predictive/interoceptive approaches contribute differently scoped system and content claims that should not be forced into the same transition test.
