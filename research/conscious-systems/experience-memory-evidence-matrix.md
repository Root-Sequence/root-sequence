# CS-Q13–14 — Experience, Memory, and Trace Evidence Matrix

**Document role:** Second bounded research pass<br>
**Status:** v0.1-dev; AI-assisted, author review pending<br>
**Questions:** Can experience occur without later retrievable memory? What is preserved when someone remembers only that an experience occurred?<br>
**Evidence status:** Provisional answers and inference rules grounded in selected studies; not a validated test of consciousness

## Provisional answers

### CS-Q13: Can experience occur without forming any later retrievable memory?

**Probably yes if “memory” means later declarative or episodic retrieval. The stronger claim that no memory process or trace formed is not established.**

The clearest present evidence comes from studies that test people during an event rather than relying only on later recollection. In one prospective anesthesia study, 12 of 260 participants gave a volitional hand response to verbal commands after intubation, five used a second squeeze to indicate pain, and none of the 253 participants later questioned reported explicit recall of intraoperative events.[^sanders] This supports a dissociation between connected consciousness at the time and later explicit recall.

It does not show that the episode formed no memory of any kind. Understanding a command and selecting a response may require transient maintenance; the response itself is a physical trace; and an inaccessible memory may differ from a memory that was never encoded. The defensible conclusion is therefore:

> **Experience and later declarative retrieval can dissociate. Whether experience can occur without any retention process whatsoever remains open.**

### CS-Q14: What is preserved when someone remembers only that an experience occurred?

At minimum, the later person has an **occurrence judgment**: *something was experienced*. That judgment may preserve a state tag, gist, source, confidence, familiarity, or feeling of knowing without recoverable sensory or narrative content. It may also be reconstructed from bodily feelings, contextual knowledge, expectation, or an implicit trace rather than retrieved from the original episode.

Serial-awakening dream research explicitly distinguishes reports of dream experience with content, dream experience without recall of content, and no experience.[^siclari] The middle report is evidence that people can judge occurrence without supplying content. It does not decide among three explanations:

1. a contentful experience occurred and its contents became inaccessible;
2. a minimal or apparently contentless experience occurred;
3. the later occurrence judgment was inferred or constructed without reliable memory of a prior experience.

“I remember experiencing something” should therefore be recorded as its own observation, not automatically translated into “a contentful experience was stored and then forgotten.”

## Terminology rule

The verb *experience* is ambiguous between merely undergoing an event and consciously feeling it. This inquiry should use separate verbs:

| Term | Meaning here |
| --- | --- |
| **Underwent** | A physical, chemical, environmental, or computational event happened to the system. |
| **Processed** | The event changed activity or information use within the system. |
| **Experienced** | There was something it was like for the system during the relevant interval. |
| **Encoded** | The event changed the system in a way that could potentially support later use. |
| **Remembered** | Information about the earlier event became retrievable later. |
| **Reported** | The system produced an observable claim or response about the event. |

A system can undergo and process an event without experiencing it. It can also retain an encoded effect without consciously remembering the event. Using *experienced* for all three collapses the question before investigation begins.

## Memory is not one variable

The claim “there was no memory” needs to specify at least:

- **Transient maintenance:** Was information held long enough to shape the ongoing experience or response?
- **Encoding:** Did the event change the system in a potentially reusable way?
- **Consolidation:** Did that change become durable?
- **Occurrence memory:** Can the person later judge that something happened or was experienced?
- **Content memory:** Can any sensory, affective, conceptual, spatial, or narrative detail be retrieved?
- **Source memory:** Can the person identify when, where, or how the remembered information arose?
- **Recognition or familiarity:** Does the material later seem previously encountered without recollection?
- **Implicit trace:** Does earlier processing alter later performance, physiology, expectation, or choice without conscious recollection?

Failure at one stage does not demonstrate failure at every other stage.

## Four rival cases

| Case | During the target interval | Later evidence | What it would establish | Central ambiguity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A. Experience with content memory** | Content was experienced. | Occurrence and some content can be retrieved. | Strong evidence for that reported content, especially with immediate and specific reporting. | Reports can still reconstruct, omit, or reshape details. |
| **B. Experience with occurrence-only memory** | Some experience occurred; whether it was richly contentful remains open. | The person judges that experience occurred but retrieves no specific content. | Occurrence judgment survived content loss or absence. | The judgment could be a feeling of knowing, gist, minimal experience, or later inference. |
| **C. Experience without later declarative retrieval** | Experience is supported by contemporaneous report or flexible, intentional response. | No later occurrence or content recollection is available. | Experience need not produce durable retrievable declarative memory. | Transient, implicit, or inaccessible traces may still exist; contemporaneous response remains an inference. |
| **D. Nonconscious processing with a trace** | An event was processed but not experienced. | Priming, learning, physiology, behavior, or system change appears later without recollection. | Processing and durable influence do not require conscious remembering. | The trace cannot by itself decide whether a weak or forgotten experience accompanied processing. |

These cases describe different relations among variables. They are not a four-level scale of increasing consciousness.

## What common observations discriminate

| Observation | Strongest implication | Cases still compatible | What it cannot establish alone |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Immediate, specific first-person content report | Strong evidence for A during the reportable interval | A; later forgetting could turn the same episode into C at a delayed test | Complete accuracy, mechanism, or later memory |
| Real-time response to a novel command | Online access to the command and flexible response selection | A, B, or C; D is weakened but not logically eliminated | Phenomenal character or durable memory |
| Later “something happened” judgment without details | Direct evidence for an occurrence judgment | B, but also reconstruction after C or D | That content was experienced, encoded, or later lost |
| No later explicit recall after a verified online response | Dissociation between online access and later declarative retrieval | C most directly | Absence of every implicit, transient, or inaccessible trace |
| Recognition without recollection | Some retained familiarity or item information | A–D depending on the original interval | Conscious experience at encoding |
| Priming, perceptual learning, autonomic change, or altered choice | Earlier processing changed later function | A–D | Experience; Squire et al. found durable perceptual learning despite severe impairment in conscious remembering, demonstrating this exact limit.[^squire] |
| A theory-linked neural pattern | The measured process occurred | A–D unless the theory supplies and survives a discriminating prediction | Consciousness merely by correlation |
| No report, response, marker, or later trace | No accessible evidence remains | C or D remain conceptually possible | Whether experience occurred at all |

## Evidence anchors

### Connected response without later recall

The isolated forearm technique preserves movement in one hand while neuromuscular blockade prevents movement elsewhere. Sanders et al. used it to query participants after intubation during intended general anesthesia. The observed command responses, pain-indicating responses, and absent postoperative explicit recall provide unusually direct evidence for **online connected processing without later declarative retrieval**.[^sanders]

The inference is strong but not perfect. A volitional response is still behavioral evidence, not access to phenomenality itself. It also addresses experience connected to the external environment, not every form of internally generated experience.

### Experience reports during unresponsiveness

Radek et al. interviewed participants after arousing them during controlled dexmedetomidine or propofol administration and after recovery. Eighty-four percent of 93 interviews included reports attributed to periods of unresponsiveness, most of them internally generated dream-like experiences.[^radek] This shows why motor unresponsiveness and later interview timing must be separated from consciousness, but the arousal procedure and retained report still select experiences that remained accessible enough to describe.

### Occurrence-only dream reports

Siclari et al. asked awakened participants to classify the immediately preceding interval as dream experience with recallable content, experience without content recall, or no experience.[^siclari] The categories operationalize the distinction behind CS-Q14 and associate the report types with preceding brain activity. They do not prove that a “white dream” contains a preserved memory of a contentful dream; the category is defined by the participant's later occurrence judgment.

### Trace without conscious remembering

Squire et al. found that five people with hippocampal or larger medial temporal lobe lesions retained one-trial perceptual learning for degraded images despite severe impairment in remembering the images and test format.[^squire] This demonstrates that lasting functional change and conscious recollection can dissociate. Because participants consciously viewed material during study, it does **not** demonstrate that the original learning event was phenomenally unconscious; it demonstrates that the later trace is not evidence of later conscious remembering.

Masked-vision work adds a different case: stimulus location information can guide above-chance responses and propagate through parts of cortex on trials reported as unseen, while reported seeing is associated with additional later dynamics.[^salti] Even here, “unseen” can combine genuinely nonconscious processing with weak experiences falling below a report threshold.

## The no-trace boundary

An experience that leaves absolutely no effect—no report, changed state, disposition, behavior, memory, physiology, or measurable relation—would be empirically indistinguishable from no experience. Science could not confirm or disconfirm that individual event.

This does not prove that trace-free experience is impossible. It marks an epistemic boundary:

> **A possible difference becomes scientifically investigable only when it makes, or reliably relates to, some observable difference.**

The research program should therefore seek converging traces without redefining those traces as consciousness itself.

## Best next comparison

The strongest design separates online access from later memory within the same episode:

1. obtain a time-locked online report or response that is difficult to explain as a fixed reflex;
2. test occurrence, content, source, recognition, and confidence separately at multiple delays;
3. measure implicit effects without treating them as proof of experience;
4. include a matched condition with processing but no reported awareness;
5. specify how each candidate theory links the online and later measures to experience;
6. prestate which result would weaken each interpretation.

For this project, the practical next contribution should be a structured extraction of existing dream-awakening, anesthesia, amnesia, and masked-perception studies before proposing a new human experiment.

## Decision after the first pass

1. **Answer CS-Q13 provisionally “yes” for later declarative retrieval, not for every possible memory process or trace.**
2. **Treat occurrence memory as a distinct metacognitive observation, not impoverished content memory by default.**
3. **Treat implicit or physical traces as evidence of processing, not sufficient evidence of experience.**
4. **Mark completely trace-free experience as presently outside empirical adjudication.**
5. **Require future studies to record online access, occurrence memory, content memory, source memory, recognition, implicit effects, and physiological measures separately.**
