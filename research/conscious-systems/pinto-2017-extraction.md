# Pinto 2017 — Divided access and subject unity

**Question:** When information cannot be compared across brain systems, does that establish separate conscious subjects?

**Status:** AI-assisted, author review pending. Bounded primary-study audit, not a systematic review or theory verdict.

Source: [Pinto et al. (2017), Brain 140, 1231–1237](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/aww358), including the publisher-linked supplement. Main-text methods, results, and discussion inspected; supplementary pp. 1–3 inspected. Remaining supplementary analyses and underlying data not audited; no reanalysis.

**Supplement check:** eye-position exclusions ranged from 22.4% to 68.75% across Experiment 1 participant/response conditions. Authors report the same overall pattern with excluded trials included. Eye criteria covered fixation through stimulus offset; the experimenter watched for behavioral cueing (supplement pp. 1–3).

## What follows, and what does not

The contrast between individual-stimulus reporting and cross-field comparison is worth explaining. It does not supply a direct count of experiencing subjects. We must keep three questions separate:

1. Can information influence a response channel?
2. Can information from two sources be compared within the tested task?
3. Are those contents experienced by one subject or more than one?

The first two have task outcomes here. Moving from those outcomes to the third requires a theory linking access, comparison, control, and experience. Calling either successful reporting or failed comparison a decisive subject-count test would assume that link rather than test it.

Failure to reject chance is not evidence of exactly zero transfer. Likewise, a nonsignificant response-mode interaction is not an equivalence demonstration. Neither qualification erases the observed pattern; both limit its precision.

## Alternatives and unresolved checks

| Possible account | Why the present observations do not settle it | Useful discriminating evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One subject with restricted comparison access | A unified subject need not access every relation between contents | Independently justified predictions about cross-content access and reports |
| Multiple experiencers with coordinated responses | Shared or coordinated output need not establish shared experience | Tests that separate output coordination from content integration |
| Residual pathways or learned behavioral cues support performance | Callosotomy is not removal of every possible communication route | Channel-specific controls, timing, and structural evidence |

These are candidate explanations, not findings that cueing occurred or that either unity account is correct. The authors discuss and argue against cueing; inspecting the experimenter controls alone cannot establish that every possible cue was excluded. The present audit has not evaluated the full subsequent scholarly debate.

The small patient sample and uneven experiment coverage limit generalization. Many trials improve estimates for a particular patient and task; they do not create more independent patients. Substantial exclusions warrant checking retained counts and sensitivity, without assuming selection bias has been demonstrated.

## Decision and next check

Retain this as evidence against treating the familiar visual-field/response-hand mapping as universal. Do not label it proof of either one mind or two minds, or use it alone to select a consciousness theory.

Next, extract retained trial counts and uncertainty for the key within/across-field contrast, then audit the published cueing critique alongside the authors' response. Candidate debate entry: [Unifying control over the body: consciousness and cross-cueing in split-brain patients](https://academic.oup.com/brain/article/141/3/e15/4812597). Its argument has not yet been audited here. No new human experiment is proposed in this pass.
