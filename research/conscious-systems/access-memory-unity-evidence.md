# Access, memory, and subject unity — evidence consolidation

**Status:** AI-assisted exploratory synthesis; author review pending, 2026-09-23. Selected sources, not a systematic review. No participant data reanalysis or new human experiment.

**Contribution:** Distinguish information transfer from recollection and subject unity; record the limited overlap between AWARE II recordings and survivor interviews. Consolidates the follow-up discussion without treating repeated summaries as independent evidence.

## DID: a concrete design qualification

In Huntjens 2012, participants rated all test stimuli for personal relevance in the reportedly amnesic identity during session 2. Reaction-time testing followed one week later in session 3 (Materials and Procedure). Thus the critical words were not previously unseen in that state. Irrelevant items were also encountered: exposure alone does not automatically explain the differential response. The follow-up question is how exposure, familiarity, and autobiographical relevance were separated. This is a design qualification, not a demonstrated confound or refutation.

The selected identities also had to switch on request and sustain the task. Nine analyzed participants and those selection requirements limit generalization. Keep recognition, free recall, source memory, reliving, and perceived ownership separate.

## AWARE II: participant linkage before temporal linkage

The abstract reports 567 arrests, 53 survivors, 28 interviewees, and 11 interviewees with memories/perceptions suggestive of consciousness. These denominators describe different subsets; the interview proportion is not a population prevalence estimate.

The published-paper indexed excerpt states that two of the 28 interviewees had EEG data and neither belonged to the group with explicit cognitive recall. Locator: paragraph immediately before “Part two: Pilot sub-studies of implicit learning and brain …”; [indexed copy](https://www.anthromed.org/bitstreams/77461563-9295-4e51-b591-b4a402ec8cdd/download). Direct download was blocked; page context, supplements, and participant-level records remain unverified.

Accordingly, these observations do not directly link a recalled experience to a contemporaneous EEG pattern in the same person. Missing recordings also do not demonstrate absence of brain activity. Neither a neural cause of these particular recollections nor experience independent of brain activity follows from this overlap limitation.

For future extraction, distinguish:

1. **Participant linkage:** physiology and report from the same person.
2. **Temporal linkage:** evidence places the experience within the recorded interval.
3. **Interpretive validity:** the measured signal discriminates experience from alternative processes.

Accurate recall of independently timed stimuli could constrain timing, but target exposure, response options, chance performance, and alternative access must also be examined. The abstract's single auditory identification is insufficient to resolve these issues without its protocol and denominator.

## Consequences for subject-unity claims

Information transfer, successful comparison, coordinated output, and shared experience are different claims. Two communicating systems can coordinate outputs; that conceptual counterexample does not establish whether either system is conscious. Likewise, two processors are not evidence of two subjects, and shared memory is not evidence of one.

Assign each measure its validated target: capacity, content, access, memory, or unity. A measure useful for one target must not silently become a test of another. Physical or chemical implementation does not by itself decide whether experience occurs.

Connected twins, DID, and split-brain surgery should remain separate cases. The present evidence neither identifies a universal subject-count test nor licenses counting identities, brains, or response channels as conscious subjects.

## Decisions and bounded follow-up

- Retain the existing [experience–memory conclusion](experience-memory-evidence-matrix.md): evidence supports awareness without later explicit recall, not absence of every memory process or trace. The anesthesia example was already documented there; repeating it is not new evidence.
- Keep the [Pinto audit](pinto-2017-extraction.md) as the detailed split-brain record. Subsequent cueing/subcortical critiques raise alternatives; a balanced audit still requires the authors' replies and retained trial counts.
- Prioritize obtaining an accessible final AWARE II paper and supplements to verify participant overlap, target exposure, and EEG timing/artifact criteria. Until then, label the overlap finding as excerpt-verified, not a completed extraction.
- Do not expand the theory taxonomy or propose participant recruitment in this pass. Novelty and independent scientific review remain unestablished.
