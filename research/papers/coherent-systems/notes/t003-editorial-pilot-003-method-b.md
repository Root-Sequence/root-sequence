# T-003 Editorial Pilot 003 — Method B

**Method:** capability-oriented analysis  
**Case:** NDSS  
**Status:** EDITORIAL OUTPUT / NOT A COMPLETE CAPABILITY-ASSESSMENT STUDY  
**Evidence:** frozen NDSS packet only

## B-01 — Formal availability of an online service is not equivalent to capability to use it

**Packet basis:** NDSS exists because some users are digitally excluded or need assistance; 18% said they would not have submitted without NDSS.

**Relevant opportunity:** practical opportunity to initiate/complete an HMCTS online process.

**Conversion factors:** digital skill, health/disability, procedural complexity, access to support.

**Consequence:** the online route becomes a substantive opportunity for some users only when support is available.

**Possible response:** evaluate HMCTS digital access in terms of supported real opportunity, not website availability alone.

**Uncertainty:** no estimate for all eligible nonusers.

---

## B-02 — Digital skill is only one conversion factor

**Packet basis:** only 20% were classified as low digital capability; many others sought help because the process was complex or uncertain.

**Relevant opportunity:** completing the process correctly/confidently.

**Conversion factors:** procedural knowledge, cognitive burden, reassurance, case complexity.

**Consequence:** defining exclusion as "low digital skill" would miss many people whose practical opportunity is constrained for other reasons.

**Possible response:** assess support need through the full task context, not a digital-skills proxy alone.

**Uncertainty:** relative contributions of each factor are not quantified.

---

## B-03 — Channel options become capabilities only when they can be converted into use

**Packet basis:** some face-to-face options required long travel/waits or were unavailable; users had heterogeneous reasons for preferring remote or in-person support.

**Relevant opportunity:** receiving support through a usable channel.

**Conversion factors:** mobility/health, geography, travel time, phone confidence, wait time, local partner supply.

**Consequence:** a nominal in-person option can fail to be a substantive opportunity.

**Possible response:** measure actual conversion conditions for each channel and preserve multiple feasible routes where needed.

**Uncertainty:** user values/preferences may change if constraints change.

---

## B-04 — Service discoverability is itself a conversion factor

**Packet basis:** many users were surprised support existed; non-SSCS users had weaker referral pathways; most interviewed users knew no alternatives beyond friends/family.

**Relevant opportunity:** reaching support at all.

**Conversion factors:** information, referral structure, institutional contact pathway.

**Consequence:** identical underlying need can produce different capability depending on whether the user encounters the support route.

**Possible response:** treat discoverability/referral as part of substantive access, not communications afterthought.

**Uncertainty:** unmet need among nonusers is unknown.

---

## B-05 — Financial and educational disadvantage may compound conversion difficulty, but causality is not established here

**Packet basis:** aggregate profile includes low income, unemployment, and low/no formal qualifications.

**Relevant opportunity:** navigating digital/administrative processes.

**Conversion factors:** possible material resources, literacy/experience, time/support.

**Consequence:** these conditions may matter to practical access.

**Possible response:** investigate rather than assume; avoid designing support around a single demographic stereotype.

**Uncertainty:** packet does not establish causal effects of income/education on NDSS access.

---

## B-06 — Supported action can itself be a capability; independence should not be presumed superior

**Packet basis:** advisers sometimes supported users to complete forms and sometimes entered/submitted information; users valued not having to rely on family.

**Relevant opportunity:** completing a consequential process with acceptable support.

**Conversion factors:** availability/trust/quality of assistance.

**Consequence:** the meaningful freedom may be "can complete the process with chosen support," not "can complete it entirely alone."

**Possible response:** distinguish supported agency from dependency imposed by poor service design; ask users what forms of assistance they value.

**Uncertainty:** long-term user capability after adviser-led completion is unknown.

---

## B-07 — Reliance on family/friends is an available resource for some users, but not necessarily an equivalent opportunity

**Packet basis:** users often knew of no alternative support besides friends/family; some valued NDSS because it reduced reliance on relatives.

**Relevant opportunity:** access help with privacy/choice.

**Conversion factors:** social network, relationship quality, privacy, willingness to ask.

**Consequence:** "family can help" should not be assumed interchangeable with a formal support route.

**Possible response:** preserve an institutional support option while studying what kinds of support users actually prefer.

**Uncertainty:** family support experiences are not systematically described.

---

## B-08 — Privacy conditions affect the capability to use support safely

**Packet basis:** reports of non-private environments and discomfort discussing sensitive information.

**Relevant opportunity:** meaningful use of support without unacceptable disclosure.

**Conversion factors:** physical setting, remote-work conditions, communication channel.

**Consequence:** technically available support can be practically unacceptable.

**Possible response:** include privacy as a condition of usable support.

**Uncertainty:** frequency not quantified.

---

## B-09 — Delivery-partner capacity is a social/institutional conversion factor

**Packet basis:** face-to-face feasibility depended on geographic partner supply; partners also absorbed additional work/cost.

**Relevant opportunity:** access to support.

**Conversion factors:** staffing, training, partner geography, funding, interpreters, appointment time.

**Consequence:** user capability depends partly on institutional capacity outside the user.

**Possible response:** evaluate partner capacity as part of access rather than locating "digital exclusion" solely in individuals.

**Uncertainty:** full resource model missing.

---

## B-10 — Service-scope ambiguity may constrain users' capability even when the digital task is completed

**Packet basis:** users frequently needed procedural/legal/emotional help; partners reported expectations they could not always meet.

**Relevant opportunity:** progress through the actual administrative/legal journey, not merely interact with a form.

**Conversion factors:** procedural knowledge, legal support availability, emotional/cognitive support, signposting.

**Consequence:** improving form access may not resolve the capability needed to progress the wider process.

**Possible response:** define the supported capability and create reliable transitions to other support where NDSS scope ends.

**Uncertainty:** packet cannot determine ideal service boundary.

---

## B-11 — Next-step clarity affects future opportunity after the appointment

**Packet basis:** 20% were not clear on next steps; some were unsure who had contacted them.

**Relevant opportunity:** continue progressing after support ends.

**Conversion factors:** information, process legibility, follow-up access.

**Consequence:** a submitted form can coexist with weak capability for the next stage.

**Possible response:** make post-appointment process/navigation support explicit.

**Uncertainty:** causal effect on later outcomes unknown.

---

## B-12 — Observed users are not the same as the population lacking capability

**Packet basis:** support pathways skew heavily toward SSCS; eligible nonuser population and unmet demand are unknown.

**Relevant opportunity:** obtaining support.

**Conversion factors:** discoverability/referral before entry to the dataset.

**Consequence:** service-user satisfaction cannot establish capability for people who never reached the service.

**Possible response:** study non-use, failed referrals, and unmet support need.

**Uncertainty:** central.
