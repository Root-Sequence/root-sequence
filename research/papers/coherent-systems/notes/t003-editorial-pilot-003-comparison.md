# T-003 Editorial Pilot 003 — NDSS comparison

**Status:** EDITORIAL METHOD-OWNERSHIP RESULT / NOT INDEPENDENT VALIDATION  
**Case:** UK National Digital Support Service  
**Methods:** A service/accessibility · B capability-oriented · C CSH · D Root Sequence synthesis  
**Analyst/coder:** same AI-assisted research process  
**Important contamination:** the analyst saw the source report's recommendation/conclusion material during case vetting. Therefore this document does **not** score discovery or prediction of withheld recommendations.

## Executive result

Pilot 003 strongly supports the emerging **Root Sequence-as-router** model.

The three native analyses each captured a distinct layer well:

- **Method A** was strongest on service journey, channel availability, privacy, handoffs, capacity, operations, and measurable service fixes.
- **Method B** was strongest on the difference between nominal access and substantive opportunity, conversion factors, supported agency, and the limits of treating digital skill as the whole problem.
- **Method C** was strongest on purpose, control, service boundaries, whose knowledge counts, affected-but-not-involved actors, and open legitimacy questions.
- **Method D** was strongest at showing how those findings interact through referral structure, adaptation, feedback, continuity, burden shifting, and future paths.

But Method D did **not** produce a clearly evidence-supported, decision-relevant mechanism that was substantively absent from A/B/C.

Current result:

> **RS added cross-method synthesis and question routing, not a new native service-analysis method.**

That is a positive result for the narrowed Root Sequence role and a near-null result for any stronger "Dynamic Coherence method" claim.

## Method A findings: ownership and unique value

Method A produces the most immediately implementable service-design findings:

- referral/discovery measurement;
- practical channel coverage;
- walk-in handoff reduction;
- service-scope clarity;
- privacy/accessibility controls;
- complexity-sensitive appointment design;
- follow-up clarity;
- network capacity measurement;
- partner cost/responsibility mapping;
- organizational identity/hand-off legibility;
- outcome measures beyond submission.

These should **not** be relabeled as Dynamic Coherence findings.

They are ordinary competent service/accessibility work.

### A-specific value

The concrete decomposition of:

- journey;
- channel;
- environment;
- staffing;
- handoff;
- privacy;
- operational capacity;

is clearer and more actionable than a high-level systems synthesis.

---

## Method B findings: ownership and unique value

Method B adds an important conceptual correction that service review alone can understate:

> **A service option is not a substantive opportunity merely because it exists.**

It makes the conversion structure explicit:

    formal option/resource
        +
    personal factors
        +
    social factors
        +
    environmental/institutional factors
        ↓
    substantive opportunity

This clarifies:

- why face-to-face can be nominally present but practically unreachable;
- why moderate digital skill does not guarantee capability to navigate the process;
- why discoverability is part of access;
- why supported action can be genuine agency;
- why family help is not automatically equivalent to chosen formal support;
- why service-user satisfaction cannot represent people who never reach the service.

### B-specific value

Root Sequence's earlier "agency envelope" intuition is substantially better understood as **routing to capability analysis** for this part of the case.

RS should not claim this insight as new.

---

## Method C findings: ownership and unique value

Method C makes questions explicit that A/B can leave implicit:

> **Who gets to define the service problem, allocate resources, decide the boundary, and speak for people outside the observed pathway?**

It exposes distinctions among:

- user benefit;
- institutional purpose;
- resource control;
- frontline knowledge;
- user research;
- user decision authority;
- partner participation versus partner power;
- service scope versus excluded needs;
- involved actors versus affected actors.

### C-specific value

This is the strongest prior-art home for several questions Root Sequence had started calling:

- power over conditions;
- revision power;
- boundary choice;
- affected parties;
- legitimacy.

RS should **route to CSH**, not reinvent CSH under coherence language.

## The most interesting candidate add: support-layer compensation

D-04 deserves preservation as a **research question**:

> When a support layer successfully helps people use a difficult system, which barriers should remain supported and which should be removed from the underlying system itself?

This connects:

- capability;
- accessibility;
- service design;
- institutional boundary;
- adaptation;
- hidden support labor;
- path dependence.

But the frozen packet does not include sufficient evidence about the underlying HMCTS digital-service designs.

Therefore the correct output is:

> **new evidence needed**

not:

> NDSS is masking inaccessible services.

This is a good example of what RS synthesis may do well: **generate a cross-boundary question that requires returning to native evidence.**

---

## A second useful candidate: referral structure shapes evidence

The packet supports:

- SSCS has a stronger referral pathway;
- SSCS dominates NDSS use;
- the evaluation cannot determine whether this is need or signposting.

RS adds the hypothesis:

    referral structure
        ↓
    who reaches service
        ↓
    who appears in service data
        ↓
    what staff become experienced at
        ↓
    future service design / referrals
        ↺

Only the first two links are well-supported by this packet.

The rest must remain hypothetical.

This is a useful **feedback-loop candidate**, not a finding.

## Research-method lesson

Pilot 003 supports this sequence:

    messy case
       ↓
    ROUTER
       ↓
    native service/accessibility analysis
       +
    capability analysis
       +
    critical boundary/power analysis
       ↓
    RS synthesis asks:
        how do these findings interact over time?
       ↓
    new cross-boundary questions
       ↓
    return to native methods for evidence

This is importantly different from:

    messy case
       ↓
    Dynamic Coherence Method™
       ↓
    answer

The latter is not supported by the pilots.

## What the router got right

The preregistered Method Router predicted that this case contains several question shapes simultaneously:

- substantive human opportunity → capability approach;
- service/accessibility barriers → service/accessibility methods;
- boundary/power/affected parties → CSH;
- path dependence / future possibility → RS synthesis.

The outputs behaved accordingly.

This is **editorial support for the router architecture**, not validation.

---

## What the router still lacks

### 1. Service design / public-service research is too generic

"Competent service review" needs stronger native references and methods.

Future Reading Trail work should cover:

- service design;
- inclusive service design;
- digital inclusion;
- public-service accessibility;
- journey mapping;
- assisted digital support;
- service standards.

### 2. Capability analysis needs method fidelity

The editorial prompt captures core ideas, but capability scholarship is broader and internally diverse.

A future independent comparison should involve someone competent in the capability approach.

### 3. CSH needs method fidelity

A twelve-finding AI adaptation is not equivalent to a real boundary critique with stakeholder participation.

### 4. Participation is underdeveloped

The packet reports research **about/with** service users, but the router needs a stronger route to:

- participatory design;
- co-design;
- participatory action research;
- disability-led design;
- lived-experience expertise.

---

## No normative winner

This pilot does not establish:

- how large the delivery network should be;
- that remote or face-to-face support is better;
- that NDSS scope should expand;
- that users should become independently able to complete forms;
- that local triage should replace central triage;
- that one governance structure is legitimate.

Those remain design/governance questions requiring additional evidence and participation.

- cross-method synthesis;
- explicit "NO ADD" outcome;

"Dynamic Coherence" further away from being a candidate standalone review method.

### Route

- substantive opportunity → capability approach;

- accessibility/service mechanics → native accessibility/service methods.

### Add research

- participatory/co-design route;
- stronger service-design method route;

- a fourth AI-only comparative case immediately;
- prompt-tune until RS produces an N2 finding;
- call the router validated.

## Editorial conclusion

Cloudflare taught us that RS should not replace technical incident methods.

NDSS teaches the same lesson in a richer human/institutional case:

> **RS should not replace capability analysis, accessibility practice, or critical boundary critique either.**

The potentially durable contribution is the layer that notices:

> **these are several different questions coupled in one lived system.**

Then routes each question to the method that can actually answer it.

That is much more modest than a universal coherence method.

It also looks increasingly like what Root Sequence is actually for.
