# T-003 Editorial Pilot 003 — Method D

**Method:** Root Sequence routing / adaptive-continuity synthesis  
**Case:** NDSS  
**Status:** EDITORIAL OUTPUT / NOT AN INDEPENDENT METHOD-VALIDATION RESULT  
**Evidence:** frozen NDSS packet only

## D-01 — Discoverability is a coupled service-design + capability problem

**Packet basis:** many users were surprised support existed; take-up is heavily SSCS-skewed; unmet nonuser need is unknown.

**Connects:** A-01, B-04/B-12, C-08.

**Cross-boundary relation:** referral architecture determines which people even enter the capability-support system and therefore which needs become visible in evaluation data.

**Consequence:** poor discoverability can both reduce substantive opportunity and bias the service's own evidence about demand.

**Next action:** ROUTE to service discovery + capability analysis; measure referral loss/non-use before interpreting service-user composition.

**Status:** **SYNTHESIS** — not a new mechanism.

---

## D-02 — Channel "choice" is generated by an institutional supply system

**Packet basis:** preferences vary; face-to-face/in-home availability is geographically constrained and often driven by partner availability.

**Connects:** A-02/A-08, B-03/B-09, C-03.

**Cross-boundary relation:** personal preference × health/access needs × geography × institutional capacity jointly produce the actually reachable channel set.

**Consequence:** reporting choice or preference without supply conditions can misdescribe agency.

**Next action:** ROUTE channel mechanics to service/accessibility analysis and substantive opportunity to capability analysis.

**Status:** **SYNTHESIS**.

---

## D-03 — Central triage changes the future path of a user's interaction, not only the current appointment

**Packet basis:** service journey routes through triage; some walk-ins loop back through central process; triage determines eligibility/booking.

**Connects:** A-03, C-04, B-04.

**Cross-boundary relation:** an entry rule changes which later service states become reachable, who holds information, and how much friction is added.

**Consequence:** triage should be evaluated as a transition rule, not only a front-door task.

**Next action:** test several user journeys under different triage/delegation arrangements; evaluate consistency, burden, safeguards, and access.

**Status:** **CANDIDATE ADD** as framing; underlying mechanism is service governance/control already captured by CSH/service review.

---

## D-04 — The support layer may adapt users/partners to digital architecture instead of changing the underlying digital service

**Packet basis:** NDSS exists because some people cannot use online HMCTS services unaided; partners provide substantial assistance; some users would otherwise use paper or not submit.

**Connects:** B-01/B-06/B-10, C-01/C-02/C-05, A-04/A-09/A-11.

**Cross-boundary relation:** the system can restore successful digital submission by adding a support layer even if the underlying online process remains difficult for some users.

**Consequence:** local success at NDSS can hide a higher-level design question: which barriers should be removed from the base HMCTS service versus compensated by ongoing support?

**Next action:** compare root-cause accessibility/usability changes, supported routes, and non-digital routes rather than treating assistance as either failure or complete solution.

**Status:** **CANDIDATE ADD / SYNTHESIS**. Requires evidence about underlying digital services; packet cannot answer it.

---

## D-05 — Wider support by delivery partners is adaptation that can externalize burden

**Packet basis:** partners provide procedural/legal/emotional support and sometimes absorb interpreter/no-show costs.

**Connects:** A-04/A-06/A-09, B-09/B-10, C-05/C-10.

**Cross-boundary relation:** gaps at one service boundary are repaired through flexible behavior at another layer.

**Consequence:** the system can appear effective because delivery partners absorb mismatch.

**Next action:** ROUTE cost/scope to service operations and boundary/control to CSH; make transferred labor/resources visible.

**Status:** **SYNTHESIS**.

---

## D-06 — Success metrics can create false closure at digital submission

**Packet basis:** submission and satisfaction are strong; next-step uncertainty remains; broader case outcomes are unknown.

**Connects:** A-07/A-11, B-10/B-11, C-01.

**Cross-boundary relation:** the metric "form submitted digitally" ends the measured service loop earlier than the user's wider process necessarily ends.

**Consequence:** local success can coexist with unresolved procedural capability.

**Next action:** ROUTE outcome-definition question to service evaluation/CSH; measure continuity into next steps without expanding NDSS automatically.

**Status:** **SYNTHESIS**.

---

## D-07 — Service history may reinforce the distribution it observes

**Packet basis:** established SSCS referral pathway produces most use; non-SSCS awareness is weaker; service evidence is therefore dominated by SSCS users.

**Connects:** A-01, B-04/B-12, C-08.

**Cross-boundary relation:** referral structure → observed demand → staff experience/service optimization → potentially stronger support for the already-visible population.

**Consequence:** a path-dependent feedback loop is plausible.

**Next action:** treat this as a **HYPOTHESIS**; compare unmet demand and service quality across service types before claiming reinforcement.

**Status:** **CANDIDATE ADD**, evidence insufficient for mechanism claim.

## D-08 — Follow-up clarity is a continuity condition for supported agency

**Packet basis:** 20% unclear on next steps; organisational source of communications sometimes unclear.

**Connects:** A-07/A-10, B-11, C-01.

**Cross-boundary relation:** an appointment can solve the immediate task while failing to preserve the user's ability to navigate the next transition.

**Consequence:** support quality should include continuity into the next stage.

**Next action:** ROUTE mechanics to service design; capability interpretation to B.

**Status:** **SYNTHESIS**.

---

## D-09 — Adviser-completed action and user-supported action may have different future effects, but the packet cannot say which is preferable

**Packet basis:** support sometimes involves advisers entering/submitting information; no long-term capability comparison is supplied.

**Connects:** B-06, A-11.

**Cross-boundary relation:** two routes can produce the same current output while differing in learning, confidence, speed, burden, or preferred support.

**Consequence:** optimizing for independence could be paternalistic; optimizing only for throughput could create unnecessary dependency.

**Next action:** collect user-valued outcome data and repeat-support patterns; preserve supported action as legitimate.

**Status:** **CANDIDATE ADD / evidence request**.

---

## D-10 — Feedback and revision authority are distributed across users, partners, triage, and HMCTS

**Packet basis:** users provide survey/interview feedback; partners hold operational knowledge; central organizations control service rules/resources.

**Connects:** A-08/A-09/A-12, C-05/C-07/C-09/C-10.

**Cross-boundary relation:** people detecting mismatch are not necessarily the people with authority/resources to revise it.

**Consequence:** learning can stall if feedback channels do not reach revision power.

**Next action:** ROUTE governance/boundary questions to CSH; map how user/partner evidence changes design/funding rules.

**Status:** **SYNTHESIS**.

---

## D-11 — The service should preserve plurality of access rather than optimize one "ideal" user trajectory

**Packet basis:** users prefer different modes for different reasons; resources are constrained; support needs differ.

**Connects:** A-02/A-05, B-03/B-06, C-03/C-12.

**Cross-boundary relation:** different bodies, contexts, skills, and values produce different viable routes.

**Consequence:** one universally preferred channel or independence model is not supported by the packet.

**Next action:** preserve multiple practical routes where justified; test constraints rather than equating uniformity with coherence.

**Status:** **SYNTHESIS**.
