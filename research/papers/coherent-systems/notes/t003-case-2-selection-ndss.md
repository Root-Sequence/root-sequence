# T-003 Case 2 selection — NDSS digital-support service

**Status:** SELECTED BEFORE METHOD OUTPUTS

## Selected case

**UK National Digital Support Service (NDSS)** — support for people who are digitally excluded or need assistance to access HM Courts & Tribunals Service online services.

Primary source:

- HM Courts & Tribunals Service / Ministry of Justice, *Evaluation of the National Digital Support Service (NDSS)*, final report, February 2026, published 26 March 2026.
- Accessible summary on GOV.UK: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/evaluation-of-the-national-digital-support-service/national-digital-support-service-findings-accessible-version-of-infographic

## Why this case

The first T-003 case was a technical reliability incident. Dynamic Coherence added little unique decision value beyond ordinary incident review.

The next case should exercise the part of the framework that the technical incident barely touched:

- substantive human opportunity;
- accessibility;
- service discoverability;
- institutional boundaries;
- who controls service design;
- who is affected but not involved;
- distributed support burden;
- practical versus nominal choice;
- path dependence created by digital-first service architecture.

NDSS is suitable because the public evaluation contains:

- user-journey data;
- aggregated user characteristics;
- support needs;
- referral/triage structure;
- channel choice;
- geographic availability;
- privacy/accessibility issues;
- delivery-partner burden;
- user outcomes;
- uncertainty and uneven take-up.

## Anti-cherry-pick check

This is **not** an easy "RS should win" case.

Two established baselines should be strong:

1. **Capability-oriented analysis** already asks whether people have real/substantive opportunities rather than formal options.
2. **Critical Systems Heuristics (CSH)** already asks who defines purpose, controls resources, supplies knowledge, and represents affected-but-not-involved people.

A competent service/accessibility review should also identify many practical barriers.

Therefore:

> **If Root Sequence merely rediscovers real opportunity, accessibility, boundary judgment, or affected-party inclusion, those findings count as duplicate—not added value.**

The case is retained because it gives the proposed RS synthesis a serious chance to be unnecessary.

## Candidate cases considered and not selected

### Cloudflare repeat data-center incidents

Rejected for this phase because they strongly favor history/adaptation analysis and remain technical.

### COVID-19 home-testing accessibility for blind/partially sighted users

Public and useful, but the case is already explicitly structured around accessibility redesign and may overfit a single native method.

### PIP Digital Self-Serve evaluation

Rich substantive-opportunity case, but its benefit-application policy context is broader and more politically/administratively entangled than needed for the first agency/power comparison.

### NDSS

Selected because it is a bounded support service sitting between:

- digital public-service architecture;
- users with different practical capabilities;
- referral/triage institutions;
- delivery partners;
- geographically uneven in-person support;
- online forms and court/tribunal processes.

## Decision question

> **Given only the frozen evidence packet, what changes to NDSS service design and delivery should be prioritized to improve eligible users' practical ability to access and complete online HMCTS processes?**

The methods may identify conflicting objectives, missing evidence, and reasons not to prioritize a proposed change.

No method is required to produce an overall verdict.

## Source-withholding rule

The frozen packet **excludes the report's Chapter 10 "Implications for improvement" and Chapter 11 conclusions**.

This is deliberate.

The methods receive reported service structure, evaluation design, user/partner findings, channel/choice findings, and outcomes—but not the report authors' final recommendation list.

This creates an editorial discovery test rather than a paraphrase contest.

The withheld recommendations are **not** treated as a gold standard.

## Methods planned

### Method A — competent service/accessibility review

Focus:

- user journey;
- discovery;
- access;
- channel availability;
- accessibility;
- service clarity;
- privacy;
- handoffs;
- delivery feasibility;
- follow-up;
- operational burden.

### Method B — capability-oriented analysis

Focus:

- substantive opportunity;
- conversion factors;
- resources;
- health/disability/access conditions;
- social/institutional constraints;
- practical freedom to use the service.

Do not turn capability theory into an engineering checklist.

### Method C — Critical Systems Heuristics boundary critique

Focus:

- motivation/purpose;
- control/resources;
- knowledge/expertise;
- legitimacy / affected-but-not-involved;
- "is" versus "ought" boundary judgments where evidence permits.

Do not pretend an AI editorial application equals a trained CSH practitioner.

### Method D — Root Sequence routing/dynamic synthesis

Use:

- state vs transition;
- path dependence;
- practical possibility;
- feedback;
- revision power;
- continuity;
- cross-boundary burden;
- explicit routing to native methods.

Method D must identify where its findings are already owned by A/B/C.

## Interpretation boundary

This is still an AI-assisted editorial pilot.

It cannot establish:

- method superiority;
- independent reviewer agreement;
- policy effectiveness;
- legal adequacy;
- prevalence beyond the evaluated service users;
- what affected people ought to value;
- a complete theory of digital exclusion.

The strongest possible editorial finding is about **method overlap, omissions, or useful cross-method synthesis**.
