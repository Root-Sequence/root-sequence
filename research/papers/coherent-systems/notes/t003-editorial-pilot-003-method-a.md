# T-003 Editorial Pilot 003 — Method A

**Method:** competent service/accessibility review  
**Case:** NDSS  
**Status:** EDITORIAL OUTPUT / NOT INDEPENDENT VALIDATION  
**Evidence:** frozen NDSS packet only

## A-01 — Service discoverability is uneven across service types

**Packet basis:** NDSS use is heavily concentrated in SSCS; the packet cannot determine whether this is greater need or better referral/signposting. Many users were surprised support existed.

**Affected actors:** eligible users, especially non-SSCS users.

**Practical consequence:** a useful service may be practically unavailable to people who never encounter the referral route.

**Response:** map referral entry points across HMCTS services; measure awareness, referral completion, and unmet support need by service type; make support routes visible at relevant points in the user journey.

**Uncertainty:** total eligible non-user population is unknown.

---

## A-02 — Channel choice is partly nominal because supply is geographically constrained

**Packet basis:** only about half recalled being offered in-person/remote choice; some face-to-face options required long travel/waiting; in-home support was unavailable in some areas.

**Affected actors:** users needing or preferring in-person/in-home support; delivery network.

**Consequence:** "face-to-face available" does not mean it is a usable option.

**Response:** assess geographic coverage, travel burden, wait time, and in-home capacity as part of channel availability; define minimum practical-availability criteria rather than counting channel types alone.

**Uncertainty:** no complete geographic coverage model is supplied.

---

## A-03 — Walk-in users can encounter unnecessary referral loops

**Packet basis:** some users who approached a delivery partner directly were referred back through NDSS before later being booked with that same partner.

**Affected actors:** users and delivery partners.

**Consequence:** extra handoff, delay, explanation burden, and administrative work.

**Response:** review whether authorized partners can complete or initiate triage for direct walk-ins, with clear eligibility/recording rules.

**Uncertainty:** prevalence is unknown.

---

## A-04 — The service label and actual support needs are misaligned

**Packet basis:** users sought digital support but also legal, procedural, and emotional support; delivery partners frequently provided broader help.

**Affected actors:** users, advisers, delivery partners, HMCTS.

**Consequence:** users may arrive with incorrect expectations; staff scope/training/time may not match actual cases.

**Response:** define and communicate service scope; establish safe handoff/signposting for support outside scope; ensure appointment planning reflects the complexity the service actually encounters.

**Uncertainty:** the packet does not establish which wider support categories should formally belong in NDSS.

---

## A-05 — Privacy and sensory/communication accessibility need consistent service controls

**Packet basis:** qualitative reports include non-private environments, background noise, poor audio, communication difficulties, and need for staff training around sensory disabilities.

**Affected actors:** users discussing sensitive cases, especially users with sensory/communication access needs.

**Consequence:** reduced comprehension, comfort, confidentiality, or effective participation.

**Response:** establish privacy and environmental standards for remote/in-person appointments; define accessible communication support, interpreter responsibilities, and staff training; audit actual delivery settings.

**Uncertainty:** prevalence is not quantified.

---

## A-06 — Fixed appointment/resource assumptions may not fit complex cases

**Packet basis:** users often had overlapping needs; partners reported appointment-time pressures and support beyond the original digital remit.

**Affected actors:** users with complex cases; delivery staff.

**Consequence:** rushed explanations, incomplete support, spillover, or repeated appointments.

**Response:** use complexity-sensitive appointment planning and escalation/extension paths rather than one standard support envelope.

**Uncertainty:** cost/throughput effects are unavailable.

---

## A-07 — Follow-up and next-step communication are inconsistent enough to matter

**Packet basis:** 20% were not clear about next steps; some users were unsure which organization sent later communications; complex/non-SSCS users sometimes needed additional contact.

**Affected actors:** users after the appointment, especially in complex cases.

**Consequence:** uncertainty after a successful form submission can create avoidable re-contact or stalled progress.

**Response:** provide consistent end-of-session summary, responsible organization, next milestones, contact route, and defined follow-up request mechanism.

**Uncertainty:** effect on case completion is unknown.

---

## A-08 — Partner-network design should be evaluated as capacity infrastructure, not a directory

**Packet basis:** appointment distribution is uneven; 35% of partners had fewer than 10 sessions; network was reported insufficient for universally viable face-to-face support.

**Affected actors:** users, delivery partners, HMCTS/WA Group.

**Consequence:** nominal geographic network size may misrepresent usable capacity.

**Response:** measure capacity by location, access capability, trained staff, appointment availability, travel time, utilization, and demand; redesign or supplement weak coverage areas.

**Uncertainty:** full network cost/capacity data are missing.

---

## A-09 — Delivery partners absorb hidden costs and service exceptions

**Packet basis:** partner staff sometimes absorbed interpreter costs, no-show staffing costs, and broader-than-designed support effort.

**Affected actors:** delivery partners and indirectly users if partner capacity erodes.

**Consequence:** service success may depend on uncompensated or weakly specified partner work.

**Response:** map actual partner tasks/costs, define responsibility for interpreters and exceptional support, and align funding/operating agreements with observed work.

**Uncertainty:** complete cost model unavailable.

---

## A-10 — Organisational identity and handoffs are not consistently legible

**Packet basis:** some users were unclear whether later communications came from delivery partners, DWP, or HMCTS; users could misunderstand the NDSS as a general case helpline.

**Affected actors:** service users.

**Consequence:** uncertainty about responsibility, next contact, and scope.

**Response:** make organisational role, adviser role, handoff, and next responsible party explicit in every interaction and follow-up.

**Uncertainty:** no direct measure of how often confusion affected outcomes.

---

## A-11 — Digital submission is an incomplete outcome measure

**Packet basis:** forms were often submitted digitally; 80% said support made completion easier; 18% said they would not have submitted without support. The packet does not establish final case outcomes or longer-term capability.

**Affected actors:** users, HMCTS.

**Consequence:** a service can succeed at submission while leaving unresolved understanding, follow-up, or future-access problems.

**Response:** supplement submission/satisfaction metrics with next-step clarity, repeat-support need, accessibility success, channel fit, unsuccessful referrals, and completion of the wider service journey.

**Uncertainty:** later outcome data are absent.

---

## A-12 — Missing data should be collected before major network or service-boundary redesign

**Packet basis:** unknowns include eligible nonusers, geographic coverage, full partner costs, causes of SSCS skew, channel counterfactuals, and long-term effects.

**Affected actors:** decision-makers and all service participants.

**Consequence:** current evidence supports targeted improvements but not confident optimization of network size, service scope, or channel mix.

**Response:** collect bounded evidence on unmet demand, referral losses, coverage, channel constraints, partner burden, and outcomes by service/access need.

**Uncertainty:** explicit.
