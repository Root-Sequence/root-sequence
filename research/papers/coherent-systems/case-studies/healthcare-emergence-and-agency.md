# Patchwork healthcare: emergence, design, and accountability

**Role:** Bounded conceptual case for the living paper; the broader interpretation is maintained in [Externalized Responsibility](../../../../analysis/externalized-responsibility.md#case-reading-no-one-designed-american-healthcare).  
**Status:** AI-assisted retrospective reading, 2026-09-24; author review pending. No interview, patient-level case reconstruction, independent causal study, or validation of the paper's review procedure.

## Catalyst and provenance

Andrew Witty, then CEO of UnitedHealth Group, argued in a December 13, 2024 *New York Times* guest essay[^witty2024] that no one designed the US health system as a whole and described it as a decades-old patchwork. A republication preserves the passage. The prior conversation initially assigned this remark to the December 4 investor conference; that attribution was wrong. The 2024 UnitedHealth Group investor booklet[^uhg2024] is used here only to document the company's stated integration and growth strategy, not as the source of the quotation.

The author's observation for this case was that the statement can describe genuine emergence while also serving as a possible excuse for an institution with substantial present-day power. The second part is an interpretation of its possible rhetorical effect, not a verified claim about Witty's intent.

## Distinguish the propositions

1. **Historical explanation:** No one organization designed the whole arrangement. This does not resolve the precise history of each subsystem.
2. **Contemporary agency:** UnitedHealth Group describes deliberate strategy across health benefits, care delivery, technology, payments, and pharmacy. Other firms, public bodies, clinicians, and employers also shape different parts. A specific decision must be traced before assigning its effects.
3. **Accountability:** Ask who chose, maintained, or could change a specified rule; what feedback they received; who bore its costs; and who had recourse. Unequal power matters, but corporate position alone does not establish intent or fault.

The statement about the whole is compatible with the existence of component-level design. An emergent account should therefore name the level and period it explains. It should not become a substitute for examining present decisions.

## A bounded observation, not a verdict

KFF's analysis[^kff2026] of insurer-submitted 2024 Medicare Advantage data reports 12.8% of UnitedHealth Group plans' prior authorization requests denied in full or part, versus 7.7% across all Medicare Advantage plans. The denominator is submitted requests for services subject to authorization. Rates can reflect plan rules, service mix, provider exemptions, and enrollee circumstances; they do not identify inappropriate decisions or quantify avoidable harm on their own. KFF's data cannot establish what the essay meant or why a particular rule persisted.

The adjacent [healthcare cost-proxy case](healthcare-cost-proxy.md) analyzes a separate published study of a commercial care-management algorithm. That study does **not** identify this company in this note and must not be used as evidence about its prior authorization practice.

## Use with C-001 and C-012

- **C-001:** A coherent explanation of a system's origin is not evidence that each participant's current objective produces acceptable system outcomes. The example is a logical distinction, not an estimate of prevalence.
- **C-012:** Apply the proposed procedure to one documented authorization or appeal rule, not to the entire US health system at once. Specify boundary, purpose, legal and material constraints, patients and clinicians affected, decision rights, a competent existing review baseline, and evidence that would contradict the assessment. This exercise is proposed, not run.
- **C-003 caution:** A convenient global description can hide a relevant internal contradiction, but this note does not label an insurer or a policy “false coherence” without tracing the actual mechanism and affected relationship.

A testable intermediary hypothesis is that integration can reduce some burdens and concentrate other dependencies. Compare access, time spent navigating, total administrative work, appeal outcomes, transparency, and exit options around a particular change. The investor booklet states a strategy, not the outcome of that comparison.
