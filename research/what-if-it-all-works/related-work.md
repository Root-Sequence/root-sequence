# Related work and source trail

**Document role:** annotated starting bibliography, not a systematic literature review  
**Status:** preliminary / source descriptions require deeper reading and author review  
**Source role:** the sources below establish existing disciplines, frameworks, legal commitments, and arguments relevant to the questions. They do not validate a new Root Sequence theory.

## Capabilities and meaningful access

- **Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "The Capability Approach"** — https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/capability-approach/  
  A major orientation to Amartya Sen's and Martha Nussbaum's work on substantive freedom, opportunities, functionings, and conversion factors. Supports asking whether resources or formal access actually become meaningful opportunities; does not select an uncontested list of universally required capabilities.

- **UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities, Article 9 (Accessibility)** — https://www.un.org/development/desa/disabilities/convention-on-the-rights-of-persons-with-disabilities/article-9-%20accessibility.html  
  Legal and policy reference for access across physical, transport, information, technology, and public-service settings. Its obligations apply in the Convention's specific legal context; it is not a full definition of all social flourishing.

- **UNDP, Human Development Report 2025, "A Matter of Choice: People and Possibilities in the Age of AI"** — https://hdr.undp.org/content/human-development-report-2025  
  Contemporary development framing of AI in terms of people's choices and opportunities, not raw technological performance. A policy report, not experimental proof that any particular AI deployment produces flourishing.

## Plural values, ethics, and interpretation

- **Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Isaiah Berlin" (value pluralism)** — https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/berlin/  
  Introduces Berlin's argument that some authentic values conflict and may not be reduced to one measure. Critical responses and disagreements within pluralism should be part of the review.

- **Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Moral Realism"** — https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-realism/  
  Introductory treatment of whether moral claims are truth-apt and whether some are true. Helps prevent "who counts ethics?" from becoming unexamined moral relativism.

- **Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Feminist Social Epistemology"** — https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/feminist-social-epistemology/  
  Reviews knowledge, testimonial credibility, interpretive exclusions, and epistemic injustice. Relevant to whose experience is recognized and which collective categories are missing.

## Governance and care

- **Design Justice Network, "Design Justice Principles"** — https://designjustice.org/read-the-principles  
  Community-oriented normative principles emphasizing directly affected people, outcomes, and accountability. A commitment/practice framework, not controlled evidence that every participatory process succeeds.

- **Elinor Ostrom, "Beyond Markets and States: Polycentric Governance of Complex Economic Systems" (2009 Prize Lecture)** — https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/economic-sciences/2009/ostrom/lecture/  
  Grounding for institutional diversity, commons, polycentric systems, and governance alternatives. Her observations concern particular conditions; they do not prove every commons arrangement will work.

- **Joan C. Tronto, *Caring Democracy: Markets, Equality, and Justice* (2013), publisher** — https://nyupress.org/9780814782774/caring-democracy/  
  Care as a political and institutional concern rather than simply personal virtue. Requires reading the full argument before drawing specific operational prescriptions.

## Systems and adaptive change

- **Donella H. Meadows, "Leverage Points: Places to Intervene in a System"** — https://donellameadows.org/archives/leverage-points-places-to-intervene-in-a-system/  
  Orients questions about information flows, rules, goals, paradigm assumptions, and opportunities to intervene. Meadows herself emphasizes uncertainty and cautions against treating the list as a mechanical recipe.
