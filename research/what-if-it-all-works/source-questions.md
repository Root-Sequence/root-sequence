## First prompt — What if it all works?

> What if it all works? Who gets to decide if and who gets to freely benefit? What counts as freely benefit? Who counts what? What counts what? What is what? What is anything?
>
> What if the world we’ve all been imagining gets built? Some call it utopia, some world peace, some nirvana, some heaven, some “42”, some etc etc etc (list more). Would we even recognize it if it existed at all, if it was always possible, if we had all the answers the whole time? What made it possible? What’s possible? What’s missing? Who benefits? Who’s left out? Will everyone have access? Will everything be accessible? Will people understand? Who can understand? Who can’t? What can we do to ensure everyone benefits? What can we do to ensure we are benefiting mindfully, carefully, compassionately, empathetically, lovingly, etc etc etc. How do we love each other? How do we share? How do we care? How do we etc etc etc? Who etc etc etc? What etc etc etc? When etc etc etc. Why etc etc etc. Etc etc etc? Etc.

## Second prompt — What if it is still not perfect?

> And what if it’s still not “perfect”? And what if it didn’t matter? And what if “perfection” wasn’t even possible in our reality? What even is reality? What if perfection only exists in one’s own mind? In one’s own created, imagined, curated, limited individual, single-minded, single-focused, single-issued, sole-defined world? What if humans are too complex for their own good? What if there’s no ethical way to be “perfect”. What counts as ethical anyway? Who counts it? What is it? Etc etc etc.
