# Future Trails

**Document role:** Curated discovery backlog  
**Status:** Proposed routes; AI-assisted; author review pending  
**Updated:** 2026-09-25

These are **questions worth opening**, not completed reading lists, validated theories, or commitments to create twelve new projects. The original eight future topics now have [starter trails](README.md#new-starter-trails).

The vocabulary below consists of search leads. No claim is made that the literatures are equivalent or that every named approach will fit the question. When a route becomes useful, check its native literature and record actual resources before calling it a developed trail.

## Evidence, models, and living systems

### Research methods, measurement, and epistemic quality

**Question:** How do we tell discovery from a persuasive restatement of what we already believed?

**Search vocabulary:** philosophy of science, construct validity, causal inference, replication, metascience, adversarial collaboration, qualitative research, open science.

**First expedition:** take one disputed claim and compare how two relevant disciplines would investigate it. Find a methods primer, an actual study, and a substantive critique. Record what could change the conclusion.

**Keep the tension:** openness and polished documentation do not by themselves make an inference sound. Different methods need not answer the same question.

**Start nearby:** [Information discovery](information-discovery-sensemaking.md) and the [research method](../method.md).

### Simulation, games, and causal models

**Question:** What can a simulation help us understand, and what can it only make us imagine?

**Search vocabulary:** agent-based modeling, participatory modeling, system dynamics, serious games, causal models, calibration, sensitivity analysis, validation, model documentation.

**First expedition:** choose one small world mechanism, find a documented model, and inspect its assumptions and validation claims before building a variation. Identify at least one different model that could produce similar visible behavior.

**Keep the tension:** a compelling experience, a useful teaching model, a calibrated model, and a predictive model are different achievements. Do not promote game outcomes into facts about society.

**Start nearby:** [Systems and complexity](systems-cybernetics-complexity.md), [urban systems](urban-accessibility-mobility.md), and [commons](community-commons-mutual-aid.md).

### Life, individuality, and more-than-human worlds

**Question:** How do living systems maintain themselves, relate to an environment, and become units of explanation?

**Search vocabulary:** theoretical biology, biological individuality, symbiosis, developmental systems, ecological psychology, enactivism, animal cognition, biosemiotics.

**First expedition:** compare two organisms or organism–environment relations using sources native to the relevant biology. Ask which capacities are measured and which descriptions are interpretive.

**Keep the tension:** individuality, adaptive regulation, cognition, and conscious experience are not interchangeable. Avoid turning an intriguing organism into evidence for a universal social or computational theory.

**Start nearby:** [Consciousness and neuroscience](consciousness-neuroscience-mind.md) and [systems](systems-cybernetics-complexity.md).

## Provisioning, limits, and hidden work

### Ecological economics, sufficiency, and post-growth

**Question:** What would it mean to meet needs without defining success as endlessly increasing throughput?

**Search vocabulary:** ecological economics, sufficiency, provisioning systems, degrowth, steady-state economics, wellbeing indicators, material footprint, distribution.

**First expedition:** select one need and compare two ways of meeting it. Find empirical work on resource use and access, not only competing statements of ideals.

**Keep the tension:** distinguish normative goals, measured outcomes, modeling assumptions, and disagreements about transition. A smaller footprint for one activity may conceal costs elsewhere.

**Start nearby:** [Repair and appropriate technology](repair-appropriate-technology.md) and [commons](community-commons-mutual-aid.md).

### Energy, materials, and provisioning systems

**Question:** What physically sustains a supposedly frictionless service, device, or community?

**Search vocabulary:** industrial ecology, material-flow analysis, lifecycle assessment, energy systems, thermodynamics, water infrastructure, food systems, logistics, waste.

**First expedition:** trace one service through energy, materials, transport, maintenance, and disposal. Find a technical reference and a real inventory or case study; mark quantities that remain unknown.

**Keep the tension:** choose explicit boundaries and units. Do not use entropy as a synonym for social disorder or treat lifecycle estimates as precise when the underlying inventory is uncertain.

**Start nearby:** [Repair](repair-appropriate-technology.md), [urban systems](urban-accessibility-mobility.md), and [systems](systems-cybernetics-complexity.md).

### Maintenance, care, labor, and post-work

**Question:** When work disappears from view, has it been eliminated, automated, or handed to someone else?

**Search vocabulary:** maintenance studies, care ethics, social reproduction, labor-process research, time-use research, infrastructural labor, automation, disability and interdependence.

**First expedition:** take one automated or volunteer-supported service and trace who performs the remaining work, including exception handling, training, emotional effort, and recovery.

**Keep the tension:** do not romanticize either work or self-sufficiency. Reduced effort can be liberating; unpaid or inaccessible maintenance can also become a hidden dependency.

**Start nearby:** [HCI and automation](human-factors-hci-accessibility.md), [commons](community-commons-mutual-aid.md), and [repair](repair-appropriate-technology.md).

## Power, coordination, and technical autonomy

### Political economy and science-and-technology studies

**Question:** How do ownership, institutions, and incentives shape what gets built and what counts as a problem?

**Search vocabulary:** science-and-technology studies, political economy, infrastructure studies, historical institutionalism, path dependence, standards, procurement, institutional ethnography.

**First expedition:** trace one technology through a decision history, relevant institutions, and affected people's accounts. Separate documented relationships from inferred influence.

**Keep the tension:** neither a technical account without power nor a power account without technical detail is sufficient. Avoid assigning shared beliefs from a network connection alone.

**Start nearby:** [AI institutions](ai-institutions-governance-alignment.md) and [alternative institutions](alternative-institutions-futures.md).

### Governance, disagreement, conflict, and exit

**Question:** How can people cooperate while retaining the ability to dissent, refuse, revise rules, and leave?

**Search vocabulary:** institutional design, collective choice, deliberation, conflict transformation, restorative and transformative justice, informal hierarchy, exit and voice, governance failure.

**First expedition:** compare two documented decision processes around a concrete disagreement. Seek accounts from people who lost the decision or left, not only designers and satisfied participants.

**Keep the tension:** consensus is not automatically consent; procedural equality is not automatically equal influence. Treat harm response and safety as substantive questions, not facilitation problems alone.

**Start nearby:** [Commons](community-commons-mutual-aid.md) and [alternative institutions](alternative-institutions-futures.md).

### Communication, local-first systems, and resilient networks

**Question:** How can communication continue across unreliable infrastructure without making everyone dependent on one provider or coordinator?

**Search vocabulary:** distributed systems, local-first software, delay-tolerant networking, store-and-forward, community networks, interoperability, federation, protocol design, threat modeling.

**First expedition:** model one benign communication need under an outage. Compare a simple existing protocol with a proposed system; inspect failure, recovery, metadata exposure, and maintenance requirements.

**Keep the tension:** decentralization, privacy, reliability, usability, and accountability are different properties. An offline option is not useful merely because it exists on paper.

**Start nearby:** [Repair and autonomy](repair-appropriate-technology.md), [HCI](human-factors-hci-accessibility.md), and [commons](community-commons-mutual-aid.md).

## Learning, belonging, and possible worlds

### Communities of inquiry, art, and learning

**Question:** What conditions let people live, learn, make art, and investigate questions together without reproducing exclusion or prestige hierarchies?

**Search vocabulary:** communities of practice, inquiry-based learning, popular education, participatory action research, experimental education, independent research institutes, artist-run spaces, cooperative learning.

**First expedition:** compare a historical experiment and a documented contemporary practice on entry, funding, space, maintenance, mentorship, accessibility, authorship, and exit. Preserve differences rather than assembling an idealized campus from their best features.

**Keep the tension:** inspiring output does not establish humane conditions. Institutional independence does not establish epistemic independence or equitable participation.

**Start nearby:** [Alternative institutions](alternative-institutions-futures.md), [collaborative computing environments](human-factors-hci-accessibility.md), and [commons](community-commons-mutual-aid.md).

### Attention, persuasion, belonging, and epistemic dependence

**Question:** When does a community help people think, and when does belonging make questioning costly?

**Search vocabulary:** social epistemology, information behavior, conformity, persuasion, social identity, coercive control, epistemic dependence, group decision-making.

**First expedition:** investigate a specific practice—such as handling disagreement or correcting an error—using research and documented behavior. Compare benign and harmful interpretations rather than diagnosing a group from its aesthetics or reputation.

**Keep the tension:** strong community attachment is not itself evidence of coercion. Labels are not substitutes for mechanisms, documentation, and the perspectives of affected people.

**Start nearby:** [Information discovery](information-discovery-sensemaking.md), [AI institutions](ai-institutions-governance-alignment.md), and [alternative institutions](alternative-institutions-futures.md).

### Narrative, aesthetics, and lived futures

**Question:** What can stories, art, and designed experiences reveal that a systems diagram leaves out?

**Search vocabulary:** narratology, design fiction, experiential futures, speculative design, participatory futures, game studies, aesthetics, narrative ethics.

**First expedition:** express one proposed intervention as both a system model and an ordinary person's day. Compare what each form notices, omits, or makes emotionally persuasive; seek feedback without presenting the imagined day as testimony.

**Keep the tension:** felt plausibility is not a forecast. Fiction may explore possibilities but must not impersonate lived evidence or override real people's consent.

**Start nearby:** [Archives and ordinary life](archives-museums-ordinary-life.md), [alternative futures](alternative-institutions-futures.md), and the [simulation route](#simulation-games-and-causal-models).

## What to develop next

Select a route because it answers a live question, offers an unfamiliar perspective, or could revise a design—not because the map looks incomplete.

A useful next pass brings back a few annotated entry points, a genuine disagreement, and a small question that can be investigated. The methods route can strengthen research quality; the simulation route can inform a prototype; the communities-of-inquiry route can ground a shared research-and-creation space. These are editorial suggestions, not an imposed priority order.

Further expansion should deliberately seek geographic and linguistic breadth, disability-led work, Indigenous scholarship on its own terms, and accounts from maintainers or excluded participants. Do not add nominal representation without reading the work or respecting its context.
