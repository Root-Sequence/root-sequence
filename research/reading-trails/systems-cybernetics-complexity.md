# Systems, Cybernetics, Complexity & Adaptive Systems

**Status:** Developing reading trail, 2026-09-24  
**Related:** [Adaptive continuity / dynamic coherence lineage](../dynamic-coherence-lineage.md)

This is not a bibliography and not a ranked curriculum.

It is a **map of places worth getting lost** while exploring the systems/cybernetics rabbit hole that collided with the September 24 adaptive-continuity work.

## Fastest way in

If you want five tabs that immediately open five different doors:

1. **The Systems Thinker** — accessible archive of practical systems-thinking writing.
2. **W. Ross Ashby Digital Archive** — primary historical cybernetics rabbit hole.
3. **Resilience Engineering Association — Where Do I Start?** — unusually good curated reading path into modern resilience/safety thinking.
4. **Complexity Explorer** — free/on-demand complexity-science courses from the Santa Fe Institute ecosystem.
5. **Systemic Design Association / RSD** — systems thinking colliding directly with design practice, pluralism, multi-stakeholder complexity, and real-world interventions.

Then branch according to what grabs you.

# 1. The Systems Thinker

**Site:** https://thesystemsthinker.com/  
**Type:** archive / accessible systems-thinking magazine  
**Rabbit-hole value:** ★★★★★

Published by Pegasus Communications beginning in the early 1980s. The current site is an archive rather than an active new-publication stream.

Why it is delightful:

- systems archetypes;
- feedback loops;
- organizational learning;
- systems thinking in ordinary management/practice;
- short enough pieces to wander without committing to a textbook;
- decades of accumulated language and examples.

Good when you want:

> "Give me a systems idea I can understand over coffee and then spend three hours following references from."

The site's own history says the archive was preserved after Pegasus's 2013 bankruptcy so the accumulated material would remain accessible.

# 2. W. Ross Ashby Digital Archive

**Site:** https://ashby.info/  
**Type:** primary historical archive  
**Rabbit-hole value:** ★★★★★★★★★★

This is absurdly good.

Ashby's family donated his papers to the British Library and later made his journals available online.

The archive contains:

- roughly 7,400 journal pages;
- 25 volumes;
- around 1,600 index cards;
- bibliography;
- biography;
- correspondence;
- keyword-linked journal navigation;
- previously unpublished material.

Ashby is central to:

- requisite variety;
- ultrastability;
- adaptation;
- self-organization;
- cybernetics;
- the Homeostat;
- *Design for a Brain*;
- *An Introduction to Cybernetics*.

This is a place to **watch someone think**, not just read the polished theory afterward.

Start:

- archive home;
- keyword index;
- *An Introduction to Cybernetics*;
- adaptation / ultrastability;
- journal entries around organization, learning, intelligence, and regulation.

# 3. American Society for Cybernetics

**Site:** https://asc-cybernetics.org/  
**Type:** society / events / contemporary cybernetics community  
**Rabbit-hole value:** ★★★★★

Founded in 1964 and still active.

The ASC describes cybernetics in terms including:

- circularity;
- recursion;
- construction;
- reflexivity.

Useful current features:

- speaker series;
- Cybernetics Study Group;
- working groups;
- archives initiatives;
- contemporary conferences;
- links into the design/cybernetics community.

Particularly relevant for Root Sequence because cybernetics here is explicitly not only engineering—it crosses biology, arts, medicine, therapy, design, management, mathematics, physical science, and more.

The **#NewMacyMeetings** project is also an interesting contemporary attempt to revive the transdisciplinary conversational spirit of the early Macy cybernetics meetings.

**Resources:** https://systemdynamics.org/resources/  
**Type:** scholarly/practitioner society / modeling field  
**Rabbit-hole value:** ★★★★★★

If systems thinking feels intuitive but you want to push from:

> "feedback loops! cool!"

toward:

> "okay, how do people actually model this?"

this is an important next neighborhood.

Resources include:

- articles/books;
- conference proceedings;
- successful applications/cases;
- journals;
- modeling resources;
- university and career links.

System dynamics gives formal/modeled treatment to stocks, flows, feedback, delays, and behavior over time.

It is particularly relevant to Root Sequence's insistence that a beautiful causal diagram eventually needs to risk producing actual consequences.

# 9. Werner Ulrich / Critical Systems Heuristics

**Site:** https://wulrich.com/  
**CSH chapter:** https://wulrich.com/downloads/ulrich_reynolds_2010_prepub.pdf  
**Boundary critique:** https://wulrich.com/downloads/ulrich_2002a.pdf  
**Type:** scholar archive / primary conceptual work  
**Rabbit-hole value:** ★★★★★★★★

Go here when systems thinking starts sounding suspiciously neutral.

Critical Systems Heuristics asks questions around:

- motivation;
- control/power;
- knowledge;
- legitimacy;
- boundary judgments;
- people who are affected but not involved.

This is one of the strongest predecessors to Root Sequence's recurring:

> Who drew the system boundary?

> Who benefits?

> Who can change it?

> Who is affected without authority?

> Whose knowledge counts?

It is also an excellent inoculation against turning "systems" into an impersonal explanation that makes power disappear.

# 13. Philosophy of science — why unification feels so powerful

**Scientific Explanation (SEP):** https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2017/entries/scientific-explanation/  
**Mechanisms in Science (SEP):** https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/science-mechanisms/  
**Unity of Science (SEP):** https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/scientific-unity/  
**Kitcher, "Explanatory Unification":** https://web.mit.edu/bskow/www/215-S12/kitcher_explanatory-unification.pdf  
**Type:** philosophy of science / epistemology  
**Rabbit-hole value:** ★★★★★★★★

This is the place to go when you have the feeling:

> "Holy shit, one pattern suddenly makes a bunch of unrelated things make sense."

Philosophers of science have explicitly debated whether explanation gains power through **unification**: showing how many previously separate phenomena can be understood through a smaller number of reusable patterns.

Kitcher's influential unificationist account is one major version.

But mechanistic traditions push back:

> a pattern can unify descriptions without telling you **what actually produces the phenomenon**.

That tension is almost a perfect epistemic rule for Root Sequence:

    notice recurring pattern
        ↓
    gain explanatory compression
        ↓
    DO NOT STOP
        ↓
    ask what mechanism produces it in each domain
        ↓
    restore differences
        ↓
    keep only the unification that survives

This is the philosophical version of our anti-research-slop rule.

# How to use this without drowning

A good browsing rule:

> **Follow one concept until it creates a question, then follow the question—not every hyperlink.**

Capture interesting branches here.

You do not have to finish a field before being allowed to connect it to another one.

But before Root Sequence makes a consequential claim, come back out of the rabbit hole and ask:

- what did I actually learn?
- what is evidence versus interpretation?
- which native field owns the mechanism?
- what still seems connected after restoring the differences?

Then dive again. 🐇
