# Community Infrastructure, Commons & Mutual Aid

**Document role:** Reading Trail  
**Status:** Starter; AI-assisted curation; author review pending  
**Entry-point check:** 2026-09-25; discovery-level, not a literature review

> **How can people provision and govern shared resources without hiding the work, power, or exclusions that make the arrangement possible?**

Do not begin by treating every shared thing as the same kind of commons. Keep the resource, access rules, decision rights, maintenance, and actual participants visible.

## 1. Digital Library of the Commons

[Indiana University repository](https://dlc.dlib.indiana.edu/)  
**Type:** scholarly repository / archive

A doorway into papers and case material about collective resources and institutions. Search for a concrete resource or governance problem, then compare contexts instead of extracting a universal recipe from a famous example.

**Follow into:** common-pool resources, rules-in-use, collective action, institutional analysis, polycentric governance, enclosure.

**Limit:** repository inclusion is not a uniform peer-review status. Check the particular item's authors, date, publication venue, and evidence. The JavaScript landing interface was not rendered in this pass; indexed repository records were inspected.

## 2. International Association for the Study of the Commons

[Society and research-community hub](https://iasc-commons.org/)  
**Type:** scholarly community / conference and publication doorway

Useful for discovering researchers, thematic conversations, and literatures beyond a single canonical author. Move from a topic or event into the specific work being discussed.

**Follow into:** resource governance, knowledge commons, urban commons, institutional diversity, collective stewardship.

**Limit:** a shared research community can contain sharply different interpretations. Preserve those differences rather than citing the society as a collective authority.

## 3. Commons Social Change Library

[Resource library](https://commonslibrary.org/)  
**Type:** practitioner learning hub / organizing perspective

A route into the practical work of collaboration and social change: organizing, facilitation, strategy, and learning from experience. Use it to ask how participation is supported rather than assuming that an invitation creates the conditions to participate.

**Follow into:** collective care, facilitation, power mapping, group processes, campaign evaluation, accessible participation.

**Limit:** a toolkit is a proposed practice, not proof that it works equally well in every community.

## 4. Shareable

[Stories, resources, and community practice](https://www.shareable.net/)  
**Type:** practitioner publication / advocacy and discovery hub

An accessible entry into projects involving sharing, mutual aid, and common resources. Follow a project story back to the people running it and look for operational details that a celebratory summary may not include.

**Follow into:** libraries of things, mutual aid, cooperative provision, shared spaces, community resilience.

**Limit:** a reported success is a lead for investigation, not a representative sample or a ready-to-copy blueprint.

## 5. P2P Foundation Wiki

[Wiki](https://wiki.p2pfoundation.net/)  
**Type:** rabbit-hole site / community synthesis / advocacy perspective

A dense vocabulary map around peer production and commons-oriented alternatives. It is particularly useful for discovering terms and adjacent projects, then following their references out of the wiki.

**Follow into:** peer production, peer governance, commons-based institutions, shared infrastructure, open cooperativism.

**Limit:** page quality and provenance vary, and the site includes advocacy and promotional material. Treat its larger transition narratives as propositions to examine, not established descriptions of how society must evolve.

## Suggested expeditions

**Understand a shared resource:** a concrete case → Digital Library of the Commons → its governance arrangements → a contrasting case.

**Make participation practical:** Commons Library → one facilitation or care practice → a small, consent-based trial → participant feedback.

**Turn a promising story into a question:** Shareable or P2P Wiki → original project → its rules, workload, access conditions, and failure history.

## Bring back a bounded finding

Pick one shared resource: a tool library, meeting space, data collection, or mutual-aid process. Record who can use it, who maintains it, who changes the rules, how disagreement is handled, and what happens when a contributor leaves.

Ask what an excluded person or overworked maintainer would say. Do not assume that voluntary participation eliminates dependency, unequal bargaining power, or informal authority.
