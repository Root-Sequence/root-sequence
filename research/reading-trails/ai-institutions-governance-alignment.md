# AI Institutions, Governance & Alignment

**Document role:** Reading Trail  
**Status:** Starter; AI-assisted curation; author review pending  
**Entry-point check:** 2026-09-25; discovery-level, not a literature review

> **What makes an AI system safe, accountable, or governable—and who gets to define those goals?**

Keep several questions open at once: technical behavior, institutional incentives, distribution of benefits and harms, decision rights, and the ability of affected people to contest deployment. No single resource below represents the whole field.

## 1. ACM FAccT

[Conference hub and archive](https://facctconference.org/)  
**Type:** scholarly community / proceedings doorway

A research neighborhood around fairness, accountability, and transparency. Use conference programmes and paper references to discover the different methods brought to sociotechnical questions.

**Start with:** one application domain rather than fairness in the abstract. Compare papers asking what should be measured, who is affected, and whether deployment itself is justified.

**Follow into:** auditing, measurement, contestability, sociotechnical systems, discrimination, participatory research.

**Limit:** an accountability vocabulary is not itself evidence that a deployed system is accountable.

## 2. Center for Human-Compatible Artificial Intelligence

[Research publications](https://humancompatible.ai/research/)  
**Type:** research-group publication index / technical alignment doorway

Browse the actual papers behind proposals about how AI systems should behave and interact with people. Follow an attractive technical idea into the problem formulation, assumptions, evaluations, and stated limitations.

**Follow into:** assistance, uncertainty about objectives, human–AI interaction, safety, evaluation, formal and empirical models.

**Limit:** results established under a particular model do not automatically settle institutional incentives or real-world deployment decisions.

## 3. AI Now Institute

[Research and publications](https://ainowinstitute.org/)  
**Type:** policy research / advocacy perspective

A route into questions about concentrated power, accountability, and the conditions under which AI is built and adopted. Read its arguments as arguments with an identifiable institutional perspective, then inspect their evidence and compare competing explanations.

**Follow into:** political economy of AI, infrastructure, corporate power, public accountability, labor, procurement.

**Limit:** a forceful policy position and an empirical finding are different kinds of contribution. Preserve that distinction in notes.

## 4. Centre for the Governance of AI

[Research hub](https://www.governance.ai/)  
**Type:** governance research organization

A different doorway into institutions, policy, and the governance of increasingly capable AI. Use its research outputs to compare problem framings, policy instruments, and assumptions about future capabilities.

**Start with:** one specific proposal. Ask what behavior it is meant to change, which actor can implement it, and what would count as failure.

**Follow into:** governance mechanisms, institutional capacity, international coordination, evaluation, uncertainty.

**Limit:** neither an organization's name nor its research agenda makes its framing neutral or its recommendations a consensus.

## 5. NIST — AI Risk Management Framework

[Framework hub and supporting resources](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework)  
**Type:** official technical/governance reference

A concrete vocabulary for examining how an organization identifies, measures, and manages AI-related risks. Use the framework to ask what an operational process would actually require, including documentation and responsibility.

**Follow into:** risk identification, measurement, evaluation, governance, lifecycle management.

**Limit:** using a framework is not proof of safety or legal compliance. Verify the version and scope relevant to a particular use; this trail is not a current-law guide.

## Suggested expeditions

**Technical claim → institutional reality:** one CHAI paper → its assumptions → a deployment scenario → an institutional objection from FAccT or policy research.

**Who decides what matters?** Compare AI Now and GovAI on a specific problem. Record the question each is answering, evidence offered, affected parties, omissions, and genuine disagreements. Do not manufacture symmetry where the evidence differs.

**Policy → practice:** one proposed safeguard → NIST's operational vocabulary → a test of whether someone could actually implement and audit it.

## Bring back a bounded finding

Choose one real-world AI use. Make a small map of the technical claim, governing institution, decision rights, evidence, and unresolved questions. Keep affiliations, funding relationships, stated beliefs, and inferred influence as different relationship types. Association is not proof of agreement or misconduct.

Technical alignment does not replace institutional analysis; institutional critique does not by itself solve technical safety. Treat the interaction as a research question, not permission to collapse the two.
