# Information Discovery, Sensemaking & Epistemic Agency

**Status:** Developing Reading Trail, 2026-09-24  
**Related concept:** [Epistemic Discoverability and Knowledge Routing](../../concepts/epistemic-discoverability.md)

This Reading Trail is for the wonderfully recursive question:

> **How do people find knowledge when they don't yet know what to search for?**

## 1. Marcia Bates — Berrypicking

**Author page / paper:** https://pages.gseis.ucla.edu/faculty/bates/berrypicking.html  
**Type:** primary information-science paper / old-web author page  
**Rabbit-hole value:** ★★★★★★★★★★

Bates's model says real research often does **not** begin with one fixed query.

The query evolves as the searcher learns.

This is almost a documentary description of a good rabbit hole.

Follow from here into:

- browsing;
- evolving queries;
- citation chasing;
- footnote chasing;
- journal runs;
- information-seeking behavior.

## 2. The Vocabulary Problem

**Paper DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1145/32206.32212  
**Title:** "The Vocabulary Problem in Human-System Communication"  
**Authors:** Furnas, Landauer, Gomez, Dumais  
**Year:** 1987  
**Type:** foundational HCI / information-access paper  
**Rabbit-hole value:** ★★★★★★★★

The famous/important result: people use dramatically different words for the same thing.

This should permanently alter how you think about:

- search boxes;
- menus;
- command names;
- documentation;
- knowledge graphs;
- AI interfaces.

If a system recognizes only the designer's favorite term, **the user is made to look ignorant when the interface is actually linguistically brittle**.

## 4. Sensemaking

**Paper:** Russell, Stefik, Pirolli & Card, "The Cost Structure of Sensemaking"  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1145/169059.169209  
**Type:** HCI / cognition / analysis  
**Rabbit-hole value:** ★★★★★★★

Sensemaking is treated as constructing/searching for representations that let a person answer task-specific questions.

If you've ever gone:

## 5. Epistemic agency

**Overview paper:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10734-023-01142-5  
**Type:** education / learning science / knowledge-building  
**Rabbit-hole value:** ★★★★★★★

A contemporary formulation describes epistemic agency around people's ability to **evaluate, produce, use, and transform knowledge agentically**.

Related traditions go back through:

- knowledge building;
- Scardamalia & Bereiter;
- science education;
- students deciding what counts as evidence;
- learners becoming participants in disciplinary knowledge practices.

Search terms:

- shared epistemic agency;
- disciplinary epistemic agency;
- knowledge building communities;

## 7. Exploratory search

Search term:

> **exploratory search**

Useful distinction:

- lookup: "find the answer/object I already know how to name";
- exploratory search: learn, investigate, compare, discover vocabulary, and reformulate the problem.

This is much closer to the way Root Sequence research usually works.

Follow into:

- faceted search;
- visual search;
- query suggestions;
- result diversification;
- interactive information retrieval.

## 8. Citation chaining

A wonderfully simple research superpower.

### Backward chaining

What did this paper cite?

### Forward chaining

Who later cited this paper?

### Author chaining

What else did these people write?

### Venue chaining

What else does this journal/conference/community publish?

Reading Trails should make these moves explicit.

A DOI is not the end of a search.

It is a junction.

## 9. Scholar and author pages

A weirdly valuable category of web resource.

Many of the best trails in this project came from:

- personal academic pages;
- institutional publication lists;
- old HTML paper copies;
- scholar-maintained archives.

These often expose **intellectual neighborhoods** better than commercial search interfaces.

Examples already encountered:

- Marcia Bates's UCLA page;
- Werner Ulrich's site;
- W. Ross Ashby Digital Archive;
- Erik Hollnagel's site.

Do not automatically treat personal hosting as weaker evidence when it is the author's legitimate copy of published work—but verify publication metadata for consequential claims.

## 10. Search as learning, not retrieval

A useful mental-model inversion:

Traditional search:

    need
      ↓
    query
      ↓
    result
      ↓
    done

Exploratory research:

    curiosity
       ↓
    partial query
       ↓
    encounter
       ↓
    new vocabulary
       ↓
    revised question
       ↓
    new source type
       ↓
    field discovered
       ↓
    disagreement
       ↓
    better question
       ↺

This is why "just give me the answer" and "help me research" are fundamentally different interface goals.
