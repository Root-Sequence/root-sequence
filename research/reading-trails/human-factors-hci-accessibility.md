# Human Factors, HCI & Accessibility

**Document role:** Reading Trail  
**Status:** Starter; AI-assisted curation; author review pending  
**Entry-point check:** 2026-09-25; discovery-level, not a usability evaluation

> **When does a tool expand what people can do, and when does it quietly take away understanding, control, or the ability to recover?**

Explore interfaces as parts of activities, environments, relationships, and institutions—not just screens. Keep accessibility and affected people's decision-making power in the question from the start.

## 1. W3C Web Accessibility Initiative

[Standards, guidance, and learning resources](https://www.w3.org/WAI/)  
**Type:** official technical reference / learning hub

A practical entry into accessibility requirements, evaluation, and the reasons behind them. Start with an introductory resource, then connect a specific barrier to the relevant guidance and an actual user task.

**Follow into:** keyboard access, assistive technology, content structure, evaluation, cognitive accessibility, involving users.

**Limit:** passing a checklist is not a complete account of someone's ability to use an end-to-end service. Confirm the applicable document and version when evaluating a particular system.

## 2. ACM SIGACCESS

[Accessibility research community](https://www.sigaccess.org/)  
**Type:** scholarly community / research and conference doorway

A route from generic accessibility claims into specific research questions and methods. Follow conference and publication links to learn how researchers work with different access needs, technologies, and participation arrangements.

**Follow into:** accessible computing, assistive technology, participatory methods, evaluation, disability and technology.

**Limit:** do not treat one participant group or successful prototype as representing all disabled people or all contexts.

## 3. Human Factors and Ergonomics Society

[Society, technical groups, and publications](https://www.hfes.org/)  
**Type:** scholarly/practitioner community

Useful for moving from an intuitive complaint about a tool into questions about work, error, attention, coordination, and system design. Browse technical groups or publications to find the appropriate native discipline.

**Follow into:** workload, automation, situation awareness, human–system interaction, error recovery, joint work.

**Limit:** improved task performance is not automatically improved autonomy or a fairer distribution of effort. Ask which outcome the evaluation actually addresses.

## 4. Design Justice Network

[Principles](https://designjustice.org/read-the-principles)  
**Type:** normative framework / community and practitioner perspective

A starting point for asking who leads design, who benefits, and whose existing knowledge is recognized. Read the principles as commitments to examine in a design process, not as empirical proof about a particular method.

**Follow into:** community-led design, affected people's authority, accountability, participation, ownership.

**Limit:** consulting people is not necessarily giving them decision rights. Document what participants can actually change, refuse, or retain.

## 5. Dynamicland

[Research introductions and project accounts](https://dynamicland.org/)  
**Type:** experimental research / self-documented computing environment

A particularly generative route into computing as a shared physical environment rather than a collection of private app screens. Begin with the introductory material, then the account of the laboratory as part of its own experiment.

**Follow into:** collaborative media, spatial interaction, shared understanding, research environments, computing as community practice.

**Limit:** the project's own demonstrations explain a design vision; they are not independent evidence of general effectiveness or accessibility for every group.

## Suggested expeditions

**Agency versus automation:** an HFES research question → one automated task → examine comprehension, override, recovery, and who carries the remaining work.

**Accessibility versus checkbox completion:** WAI guidance → an actual service journey → SIGACCESS research → feedback from people with relevant lived expertise.

**A different computing environment:** Dynamicland → one interaction worth testing → Design Justice's questions about control → a small participatory prototype.

## Bring back a bounded finding

Choose one task. Compare a manual path, an assisted path, and a more automated path. Ask who can complete it, understand what happened, change course, recover from a mistake, and refuse automation. Do not assume the same arrangement serves every person or every energy level.

Keep support distinct from compulsory self-sufficiency: preserving agency does not require making someone perform avoidable work just to prove they can.
