# Knowledge, institutional power, and stewardship

**Document role:** bounded research question and proposed comparison method.  
**Status:** exploratory, AI-assisted seed; author review pending.  
**Evidence status:** questions, hypotheses, and normative choices; no empirical findings or allegation about a particular organization.  
**Prepared:** 2026-10-22.

## Question

How can independent research remain useful and widely accessible when institutions with greater development, distribution, legal, or financial capacity act on it—and which arrangements preserve contributor agency, public benefit, and accountability without enclosing knowledge?

## Separate the outcomes

Influence, recognition, lawful reuse, compensation, ownership of an implementation, project representation, and social benefit are different outcomes. One can occur without another. A missing citation, an exploitative commissioned task, a misleading endorsement, and a harmful application require different evidence and different responses.

A useful question is not just “who had the idea?” but “who could act on it, under which permissions, who bore the costs, and who had a meaningful opportunity to contest the consequences?” This is a proposed analytical lens, not a claim that every unequal collaboration is exploitative.

## Competing hypotheses

- Clear provenance and usable citation metadata may improve credit and discoverability, but may not change how benefits are distributed.
- Reciprocal terms may keep some adaptations accessible, while also increasing transaction or compatibility costs for small, beneficial users.
- Selective disclosure may protect agency or reduce harm, while also reducing scrutiny, participation, and the chance of useful independent development.
- An institution may generate genuine public benefit without hiring or paying the originator; that does not erase separate questions about honesty, consent, agreed obligations, or concentrated control.
- Multiple people may reach similar ideas independently. Similarity alone is not evidence of extraction.

## A bounded comparison

Use a public-safe, synthetic research example rather than unpublished work or unsuspecting participants. Compare four documented arrangements: unrestricted sharing with provenance; attribution-based sharing; reciprocal sharing; and limited review with an express agreement.

For each arrangement record: what is shared; who can inspect or modify it; who can build on it; attribution obligations versus requests; possible excluded beneficial uses; who maintains it; how participants can exit; what happens when goals diverge; and what recourse actually exists.

Before collecting real cases, specify inclusion criteria, consent/privacy handling, and an evidence standard. Measure outcomes separately—accuracy of credit, useful uptake, accessibility, contributor burden, decision influence, and distribution of benefits/costs—rather than collapsing them into a single “coherence” or “ethical” score. Record counterexamples and failures of the comparison itself.

No experiment has been run. No evidence of employer copying or harmful deployment is asserted.

## Legal and conceptual limits

The [licensing guide](../governance/licensing.md) separates existing rights, future choices, and agreements; the [stewardship procedure](../governance/README.md) is a practical proposal, not evidence that those goals have been achieved.
