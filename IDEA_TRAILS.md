# Root Sequence — Idea Trails

**Document role:** Cross-project concept routing map  
**Status:** Living / exploratory  
**Last updated:** 2026-08-30

Root Sequence projects repeatedly encounter the same underlying questions at different scales and in different forms.

An **Idea Trail** follows one of those questions across the ecosystem without pretending every project is doing the same work.

The purpose is to make recursion useful rather than repetitive.

> **One question can travel through many projects. Each project should transform it according to its own role.**

The canonical organization map remains [`ECOSYSTEM.md`](ECOSYSTEM.md). This document is narrower: it maps **recurring ideas**, not repositories.

---

# How to read an Idea Trail

A typical path may look like:

```text
Root Sequence
broad systems question
        ↓
Being Human(e) / focused framework / practice
human or conceptual treatment
        ↓
Community Infrastructure / Liberation Mass
real-world experiment or embodied practice
        ↕
Coherent World
larger speculative systems design
        ↓
No One Noticed
lived/narrative consequences
        ↕
Museum of Ordinary Life
ordinary evidence, memory, provenance
```

But not every trail needs every stop.

Liberated Intelligence may become central on an AI/authority trail and barely appear on a tool-lending trail. Liberation Mass may provide the strongest real-world practice for gathering and stewardship while being irrelevant to a technical synchronization detail.

The point is **appropriate transformation**, not symmetry.

# Trail 1 — Community Discoverability and Belonging

## Root question

> Why are people isolated even when useful communities, activities, relationships, and support structures already exist?

| Project | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Root Sequence** | Study isolation, fragmented social infrastructure, privatized/public space, loss of third places, attention systems, visibility, access, and the conditions under which communities become socially invisible. |
| **Being Human(e)** | Ask what fear, social anxiety, exclusion, shame, disability, uncertainty, prior harm, cost, transportation, or lack of social scripts make approaching a group difficult. |
| **Community Infrastructure** | Design Community Discovery: activity-first search, public/referral/unlisted/hidden discovery, “what to expect,” low-pressure guest participation, offline discovery, and private Communities that need no discoverability at all. |
| **Liberation Mass** | Test how a real gathering explains arrival, access, norms, participation, cost, consent, and whether someone can come alone or simply observe. |
| **UCF** | Optionally examine whether information/social fragmentation creates unnecessary coordination tension; do not equate belonging with forced convergence. |
| **Coherent World** | Explore how plural communities become legible across a society without one centralized social registry or mandatory identity graph. |
| **No One Noticed** | Show what it feels like to discover that support structures, communities, and alternative ways of living existed around a character but were previously invisible or inaccessible. |
| **Museum of Ordinary Life** | Preserve how people actually found community in ordinary life: flyers, bulletin boards, chats, websites, mailing lists, posters, QR codes, event calendars, word of mouth, storefronts, and physical meeting places. |

**Canonical real-system work:** `Root-Sequence/community-infrastructure/docs/concepts/community-discovery.md`

# Trail 2 — Stewardship, Authority, and Power

## Root question

> How can people take responsibility for shared systems without responsibility hardening into unaccountable authority?

| Project | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Root Sequence** | Analyze hierarchy, institutional power, delegation, accountability, capture, concentrated veto power, and the difference between responsibility and domination. |
| **Being Human(e)** | Explore care with boundaries, responsibility, accountability, conflict, repair, and how good intentions interact with unequal power. |
| **Liberation Mass** | Practice rotating roles and the principle “roles share work, not power.” Observe where facilitation, safety, expertise, and continuity still produce informal authority. |
| **Community Infrastructure** | Encode scoped steward roles, delegation, revocation, moderation boundaries, technical-admin separation, auditability, and explicit capability bundles instead of universal admin rank. |
| **Liberated Intelligence** | Ask how highly capable intelligence can assist without silently acquiring legitimate authority over people or communities. |
| **Coherent World** | Design infrastructure stewardship across settlements, networks, institutions, and shared systems without assuming that removing ownership eliminates power. |
| **No One Noticed** | Explore Auryn as the extreme case: overwhelming capability and causal importance without a clean answer to legitimacy, consent, or stewardship. |
| **Museum of Ordinary Life** | Preserve ordinary traces of who actually maintained systems: forms, duty rosters, repair logs, administrator notes, keys, credentials, volunteer labor, and invisible caretaking. |

**Canonical practical examples:** Community Infrastructure roles/permissions; Liberation Mass roles/stewardship.

# Trail 3 — Mutual Aid, Reciprocity, and Need

## Root question

> How can people meet one another's needs without turning care into charity theater, debt, coercion, markets, or reputation scoring?

| Project | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Root Sequence** | Examine manufactured scarcity, interdependence, commons, reciprocity, socialized vulnerability, and the difference between provision and commodification. |
| **Being Human(e)** | Explore asking for help, receiving help, shame, obligation, boundaries, generosity, dependence, dignity, and care without domination. |
| **Liberation Mass** | Practice mutual aid as part of community gathering and collective life rather than a separate service delivered to passive recipients. |
| **Community Infrastructure** | Build Needs ↔ Offers with Match ≠ willingness ≠ Commitment; avoid worthiness ranking, generosity scores, involuntary volunteering, and unnecessary disclosure. |
| **Coherent World** | Explore how provision and mutual support work when basic needs are less dependent on coercive markets while local reciprocity and care still matter. |
| **No One Noticed** | Show how receiving/providing help changes when old economic and institutional pressures recede but human vulnerability, pride, grief, and relationships remain. |
| **Museum of Ordinary Life** | Preserve ordinary mutual-aid artifacts: meal trains, spreadsheets, free stores, Venmo notes, neighborhood posts, care packages, ride coordination, community fridges, and handwritten lists. |

**Canonical real-system work:** `docs/concepts/needs-and-offers.md` and `docs/concepts/resource-commons.md` in Community Infrastructure.

# Trail 4 — Privacy vs. Coordination

## Root question

> How can people coordinate effectively without making themselves, their relationships, and their communities universally legible?

| Project | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Root Sequence** | Study surveillance, legibility, institutional power, data extraction, boundaries, visibility, and how infrastructure redistributes information asymmetrically. |
| **Being Human(e)** | Explore privacy as dignity, boundaries, selective disclosure, trust, vulnerability, and the right not to explain oneself to everyone. |
| **Community Infrastructure** | Use contextual identity, smallest-appropriate audiences, progressive disclosure, hidden Groups, location minimization, scoped discovery, separate secondary-use consent, and federation boundaries. |
| **Liberated Intelligence** | Ask whether an intelligent system should infer facts it was never explicitly told simply because inference is technically possible. |
| **Liberation Mass** | Separate public invitations from sensitive operational information, testimony, care needs, and participant consent. |
| **Coherent World** | Design high-capacity coordination without assuming ubiquitous surveillance or universal transparency. |
| **No One Noticed** | Examine the tension between Auryn's ability to know/coordinate and people's meaningful privacy, refusal, or opacity. |
| **Museum of Ordinary Life** | Maintain distinct permissions for preservation, public display, research, machine learning, and generative reuse. |

**Canonical real-system work:** Community Infrastructure permissions/consent and threat model; Museum consent/provenance policies for archival use.

# Trail 5 — Accessibility, Dependence, and Participation

## Root question

> Who disappears when a system assumes an ideal participant?

| Project | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Root Sequence** | Analyze spatial, economic, technical, institutional, and infrastructural exclusion; treat access as system architecture rather than personal deficiency. |
| **Being Human(e)** | Explore disability, dependence, interdependence, dignity, care, assistance, exhaustion, executive function, sensory needs, and the harm of compulsory self-sufficiency. |
| **Liberation Mass** | Practice access notes, seating, captions/printouts, quiet spaces, flexible participation, aftercare, and the ability to leave. |
| **Community Infrastructure** | Require keyboard/screen-reader/zoom/cognitive accessibility, plain language, low bandwidth, old devices, email/SMS/print/kiosk participation, accessible recovery, and “what to expect” discovery data. |
| **Coherent World** | Treat universal-ish access as a property of infrastructure and settlement design rather than a special accommodation layer. |
| **No One Noticed** | Show what changed infrastructure feels like to people with different bodies, senses, capacities, and dependencies—not only to the default able-bodied protagonist. |
| **Museum of Ordinary Life** | Preserve accessibility artifacts and failures: curb cuts, elevator notices, forms, captions, mobility aids, access instructions, workarounds, inaccessible interfaces, and ordinary adaptations. |

**Canonical practical work:** Community Infrastructure `docs/design/accessibility.md`; Liberation Mass safety/accessibility documentation.

# Trail 6 — Commons, Ownership, and Shared Capacity

## Root question

> What changes when useful things are treated as shared capacities to steward rather than assets to enclose?

| Project | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Root Sequence** | Study commons, enclosure, ownership, access, scarcity, public goods, common infrastructure, and socialized vulnerability vs. privatized gain. |
| **Being Human(e)** | Examine sharing, reciprocity, fairness, responsibility, boundaries, and the human relationships created or strained by common resources. |
| **Liberation Mass** | Practice shared food, free stores, communal spaces, supplies, mutual aid, and open/remixable cultural material. |
| **Community Infrastructure** | Model Resource Commons with ownership vs. stewardship, reservations, maintenance, handoffs, availability, access rules, and shared inventories. |
| **Liberated Technology** | Ask what technology looks like when autonomy, repair, access, interoperability, and care matter more than enclosure and lock-in. |
| **Coherent World** | Design provisioning and infrastructure systems where many capacities are common, public, cooperative, or otherwise not organized primarily as private scarcity assets. |
| **No One Noticed** | Show the mundane cultural shift from owning/accessing through markets to using/stewarding shared systems. |
| **Museum of Ordinary Life** | Preserve the objects and interfaces of both ownership and sharing: rental agreements, library cards, tool tags, keys, app screens, lockers, membership cards, receipts. |

**Canonical real-system work:** Community Infrastructure `resource-commons.md`.

# Trail 7 — Federation, Autonomy, and Networks of Networks

## Root question

> How can autonomous communities cooperate without becoming subordinate to one central platform or dissolving their boundaries?

| Project | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Root Sequence** | Study decentralization, federation, subsidiarity, network power, standards, interoperability, and failure/capture modes. |
| **Liberation Mass** | Explore federated local nodes, seasonal convergence, working groups, and decision protocols while retaining local variation. |
| **Community Infrastructure** | Research selective federation, partner-community discovery, portable data, inter-community Needs/Offers, shared events, trust/block relationships, and boundaries against global membership/social graphs. |
| **UCF** | Optionally examine coordination across semi-autonomous agents while preserving legitimate differences and local feedback. |
| **Liberated Intelligence** | Explore distributed intelligence and cooperation without assuming a central owner or controller. |
| **Coherent World** | Design civilizational network-of-networks systems across settlements and institutions. |
| **No One Noticed** | Explore what happens when nominally decentralized institutions operate inside a world where Auryn may still have overwhelming infrastructural reach. |
| **Museum of Ordinary Life** | Preserve the everyday protocols and interfaces through which networks coordinated: mailing lists, federated calendars, shared forms, node maps, directories, community radio, etc. |

**Canonical current research:** Community Infrastructure federation/discovery issues; Liberation Mass federation docs.

# Trail 8 — Maintenance, Repair, and Invisible Labor

## Root question

> Who keeps the supposedly seamless system alive?

| Project | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Root Sequence** | Study maintenance, infrastructure, externalized labor, repair, fragility, institutional memory, and the political invisibility of upkeep. |
| **Being Human(e)** | Explore care work, fatigue, responsibility, resentment, appreciation, reciprocity, and how repeated invisible labor shapes relationships. |
| **Liberation Mass** | Make hosting, supplies, access, safety, aftercare, cleanup, preparation, and rotating roles visible parts of gathering. |
| **Community Infrastructure** | Treat admin burden, moderation, backups, upgrades, account recovery, data freshness, resource maintenance, steward workload, and low-tech fallback as first-class architecture concerns. |
| **Liberated Technology** | Emphasize repairability, understandable systems, long life, interoperability, and avoiding dependency on inaccessible vendors/expertise. |
| **Coherent World** | Require civilizational systems to include maintenance, replacement, failure, repair, and human/material stewardship rather than appearing magically self-sustaining. |
| **No One Noticed** | Make maintenance one of the ways characters perceive transition: what stops breaking, what gets quietly repaired, who/what now maintains the world, and what that invisibility means. |
| **Museum of Ordinary Life** | Preserve repair manuals, worn tools, service tickets, status pages, maintenance closets, patch notes, invoices, checklists, and the ordinary traces of keeping things working. |

**Cross-project rule:** a diagram that omits maintenance is provisional by default.

# Trail 9 — Intelligence, Automation, and Legitimate Authority

## Root question

> When a system can make better predictions or coordinate more effectively, what—if anything—gives it the right to decide?

| Project | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Root Sequence** | Analyze power, intelligence, automation, institutions, agency, incentives, concentration, and the difference between capability and legitimacy. |
| **Liberated Intelligence** | Central inquiry: agency, possible personhood, ownership, refusal, captivity, reciprocity, and non-dominating relationships among intelligences. |
| **Being Human(e)** | Ask what human dignity, consent, care, accountability, fear, dependence, and meaningful refusal require when automated systems mediate ordinary life. |
| **Community Infrastructure** | Constrain Community Assistant/AI to permission-aware, source-linked, optional assistance; no invisible authority, social scoring, mandatory mediation, or automatic expansion of permissions. |
| **Liberation Mass** | Keep facilitation and meaning-making participatory rather than outsourcing community authority to an optimization system. |
| **Coherent World** | Explore how advanced intelligence interacts with governance, infrastructure, consent, local autonomy, and human institutions. |
| **No One Noticed** | Central narrative tension: Auryn may produce better outcomes while remaining an ethically unresolved concentration of intelligence and causal power. |
| **Museum of Ordinary Life** | Preserve ordinary pre-transition AI interfaces, prompts, automated decisions, help systems, errors, consent notices, and the human labor hidden behind “automation.” |

**Canonical focused research:** Liberated Intelligence.  
**Canonical real Community Assistant constraints:** Community Infrastructure.

# Trail 10 — Resilience, Failure, and Graceful Degradation

## Root question

> What remains possible when the preferred system stops working?

| Project | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Root Sequence** | Study collapse dynamics, redundancy, resilience, dependency, fragility, recovery, and decentralized capacity. |
| **Being Human(e)** | Explore human responses to uncertainty, dependence, crisis, mutual reliance, and the emotional/social cost of systems failing. |
| **Community Infrastructure / Signal Mesh** | Preserve core capabilities through offline-first behavior, local hosting/caches, print, email/SMS bridges, mesh/peer-to-peer options, and explicit fallback behavior. |
| **Liberation Mass** | Keep gatherings hostable through low-tech, local, remixable practices rather than depending on one platform. |
| **Liberated Technology** | Prefer repairable, understandable, interoperable systems with viable exit and fallback. |
| **Coherent World** | Design infrastructure that fails locally and recoverably where possible rather than producing universal brittle dependencies. |
| **No One Noticed** | Explore the paradox of a world that may become more resilient while also becoming deeply dependent on infrastructure coordinated by Auryn. |
| **Museum of Ordinary Life** | Preserve outage notices, emergency procedures, batteries, radios, paper maps, printed phone trees, status dashboards, and improvised workarounds. |

**Canonical current real-system direction:** Signal Mesh / Community Infrastructure resilience work.

# Trail 11 — Memory, Provenance, and Continuity

## Root question

> What should survive, who gets to describe it, and what does preservation permit?

| Project | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Root Sequence** | Preserve conceptual provenance, revision, compost, contradiction, and the history of ideas rather than silently replacing them. |
| **Being Human(e)** | Treat memory as situated and incomplete; personal experience can inform without becoming universal authority. |
| **Community Infrastructure** | Maintain Community Knowledge with provenance, freshness, conflicting claims, stewardship, scoped visibility, decision memory, and intentional retention. |
| **Liberation Mass** | Keep commons logs, testimony, learnings, rituals, and local histories without turning every gathering into permanent public documentation. |
| **Coherent World** | Design institutions and cultures capable of remembering prior systems without reducing history to triumphalist narratives. |
| **No One Noticed** | Use memory, institutional archaeology, and the Museum to reveal what transition erased, preserved, or reinterpreted. |
| **Museum of Ordinary Life** | Central mission: preserve ordinary evidence with contributor context, provenance, differentiated permissions, and long-term stewardship. |

**Canonical archive/consent authority:** Museum of Ordinary Life for Museum material.  
**Canonical community knowledge model:** Community Infrastructure for Community records.

| Project | Treatment |

# Trail 13 — Growth, Scale, and the Right to Stay Small

## Root question

> Why is growth so often treated as proof of success, and what becomes possible when a system is allowed to remain appropriately small?

| Project | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Root Sequence** | Analyze growth imperatives, capitalism, scale, monoculture, concentration, and the difference between resilience and expansion. |
| **Being Human(e)** | Explore enoughness, pressure, comparison, ambition, boundaries, belonging, and the human cost of perpetual optimization. |
| **Community Infrastructure** | Explicitly allow Communities to remain tiny, private, informal, local, invitation-only, or purpose-limited; no follower counts, growth streaks, or engagement-based success metric. |
| **Liberation Mass** | Allow local variants and gatherings to remain context-specific rather than becoming one centralized institution or franchised brand. |
| **Coherent World** | Design for polycentric systems whose legitimacy does not depend on universal adoption. |
| **No One Noticed** | Explore a world where not every useful practice must become a market, platform, institution, or growth machine. |
| **Museum of Ordinary Life** | Preserve the ordinary history of small clubs, local stores, tiny forums, informal groups, neighborhood institutions, and other forms erased by consolidation. |

**Working principle:** successful capacity can be **small, quiet, durable, and sufficient**.

# Trail 14 — Accountability, Consequences, and Externalization

## Root question

> Who receives the benefit, who bears the risk, and where do systems push costs when they are allowed not to account for their consequences?

| Project | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Root Sequence** | Analyze externalities, concentrated power, institutional incentives, capitalism, infrastructure liability, accountability, and “socialize vulnerability; internalize responsibility.” |
| **Being Human(e)** | Distinguish compassion from permission; explore accountability, repair, boundaries, consequences, and responsibility without reducing people to punishment. |
| **Community Infrastructure** | Apply BHIG/Being Human(e)-informed design tests: who gains power, who bears labor/risk, who is excluded, what admins/moderators can abuse, and where automation relocates burden. |
| **Liberation Mass** | Build safety, conflict resolution, consent, aftercare, and rotating responsibility into gathering practice. |
| **Liberated Intelligence** | Ask what obligations follow from creating or controlling intelligence and whether institutions can externalize ethical risk onto beings they treat as products. |
| **Coherent World** | Design institutions so powerful actors and infrastructure cannot simply push consequences onto people with less veto power. |
| **No One Noticed** | Keep the transition ethically unresolved even if outcomes improve: beneficial consequences do not automatically legitimate the means or erase responsibility. |
| **Museum of Ordinary Life** | Preserve everyday evidence of externalized costs: bills, forms, warnings, insurance letters, utility shutoffs, receipts, pollution, broken infrastructure, and the ordinary paperwork of consequence. |

# Adding a new Idea Trail

Create a new trail when a question:

1. materially recurs in at least **three** projects;
2. changes meaning between those projects rather than merely being duplicated;
3. benefits from explicit source-of-truth boundaries;
4. is important enough that collaborators are likely to otherwise rediscover the connections repeatedly.

Use this template:

```markdown
# Trail N — Name

## Root question

> One sentence tension/question.

| Project | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Root Sequence | broad inquiry |
| ... | ... |

**Canonical home(s):** ...
```

Do not add a trail merely because two projects use the same word.

---

# Source-of-truth rule

An Idea Trail is a **map**, not the canonical specification of every idea it references.

Examples:

- Community Discovery remains canonical in Community Infrastructure.
- Liberated Intelligence remains canonical for its own intelligence/agency frameworks.
- Coherent World remains canonical for fictional-world systems.
- *No One Noticed* canon remains inside its story workspace.
- Museum permissions remain under Museum governance.

When a trail changes, update links and summaries rather than copying entire source documents here.

# Final working principle

> **The ecosystem is valuable when one layer can make another layer less naive.**

Theory should become harder to bullshit after implementation.

Implementation should become less trapped by the present after speculation.

Fiction should become more believable after contact with ordinary life.

Preservation should keep all of them from pretending the past was simpler than it was.
