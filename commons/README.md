## Collaboration as a living experiment

**Status:** proposed collaboration principles for review; normative commitments and design questions, not evidence of an operating institution or a validated governance model.

Root Sequence is an experiment in creating better conditions for asking questions, sharing knowledge, discovering patterns, testing possibilities, and adapting together. Its ideas can be challenged, narrowed, remixed, or rejected, including by people who disagree with its originators. Useful participation does not require adopting the whole ecosystem or one account of what is good.

The objective is the continued ability to learn together. A modest success question is whether something we made helped someone understand, connect, choose, adapt, repair, create, or cooperate. Adoption, agreement, growth, and document count cannot answer that question on their own. Structural conditions and unequal burdens matter alongside individual capacity.

### Bounded openness

Welcome questions, corrections, counterexamples, alternative designs, domain expertise, and lived experience through visible contribution paths. Keep factual claims answerable to evidence while allowing values and interpretations to remain contested. Coherence does not require consensus; pluralism does not make every factual claim equally supported.

Contribution is distinct from merge, moderation, infrastructure access, data access, and publication authority. Use branches and review to contain consequential changes. Each project retains its scope, local process, privacy boundaries, and current priorities. Openness does not require tolerating harassment, coercion, manipulation, sabotage, or conduct that prevents others from participating.

### Power, disagreement, and repair

For consequential decisions, make actual powers visible: who can act, on what, under whose authorization, for how long, with which review or appeal, and how that responsibility can be transferred or revoked. Expertise, authorship, funding, maintenance labor, and technical access do not confer unlimited authority. Originators and maintainers remain open to correction.

Preserve objections, alternatives, uncertainty, and reasons for reopening a decision. Criticism of an idea or a maintainer is not by itself misconduct. Where boundaries are needed, connect them to conduct and consequences, state their scope, and provide review where feasible without exposing reporters or private records.

Repair should be voluntary and safe. It must not require disclosure, forgiveness, reconciliation, or renewed contact with someone who caused harm. Protective boundaries can remain necessary even when repair is unavailable. Concrete safety processes belong to the project or community responsible for them; this text creates no new moderation body.

### Maintenance, departure, and forks

Make small contributions, pauses, handoffs, retirement, and departure legitimate. Document the minimum needed to continue useful work without making one person irreplaceable or requiring unpaid self-sacrifice. Examine invisible maintenance and whose time, access, money, or energy a process consumes. Smaller scope or deliberate retirement may be a better outcome than growth.

Forks and local adaptations can preserve useful disagreement. They remain subject to the relevant license, third-party rights, and privacy constraints; a fork is not permission to publish private drafts, participant information, or unpublished canon. Exchange public-safe lessons voluntarily without requiring reunion or treating divergence as betrayal.

### Signal, reflection, adaptation, feedback

Use lightweight, purpose-bound observation to ask what the collaboration is selecting for: can newcomers contribute, can dissent survive, who carries maintenance, where does authority accumulate, and what makes departure costly? Prefer voluntary feedback and necessary decision records to behavioral surveillance, universal reputation scores, or participation quotas. Absence and withdrawal are ambiguous signals, not consent or diagnoses.

For a bounded experiment, record the question, affected perspectives, assumptions, alternatives, scope, authority, privacy limits, expected burdens, observations, uncertainty, and what would prompt revision or stopping. Compare expected and actual consequences. Include failures caused by ordinary incentives, overload, missing information, and unequal capacity even when no actor is malicious. A useful finding can be that a rule or model should be abandoned.

This loop is a working analogy across projects, not a claim that people, ecosystems, software, institutions, and intelligences share one causal mechanism. Existing [Epistemic Contrast](../research/methods/epistemic-contrast.md), [Deliberative Inquiry](../research/methods/deliberative-inquiry.md), and [Human(e) Design review](humane-design-review.md) provide deeper methods; extend them where appropriate rather than inventing a second framework.

### Where these principles belong

This section is the shared substantive home. [Organization contribution guidance](https://github.com/Root-Sequence/.github/blob/main/CONTRIBUTING.md) translates it into repository work; [Project Stewardship](https://github.com/Root-Sequence/.github/blob/main/PROJECT_STEWARDSHIP.md) addresses maintenance. Project-specific designs remain local, and the Wiki provides routes rather than a second charter.

The [Commons of Inquiry & Creation seed](../incubator/commons-of-inquiry/README.md) is a distinct incubating project. These collaboration principles do not establish its institution, create a new Commons repository, or change any project's governance or release status by implication.

Open questions include actual stewards and review routes, safe participation under conflicting needs, sustainable maintenance, practically usable exit, and which observations would demonstrate benefit. Those require project-local decisions and evidence, not answers invented by this overview.
