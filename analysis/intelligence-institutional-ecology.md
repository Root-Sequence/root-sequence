# Intelligence Institutional Ecology

**Status:** Developing research map  
**Scope:** Real-world institutions, people, funding, ideas, and influence surrounding advanced AI / AGI / superintelligence  
**Canonical role:** Root Sequence source of truth for the broad institutional ecology; project-specific repositories should transform this material rather than duplicate it.

## Why this map exists

Arguments about advanced AI do not circulate independently of institutions.

Research organizations, frontier labs, philanthropic funders, advocacy groups, fellowships, governments, academic centers, media, and professional networks all help determine which questions become legible, fundable, prestigious, actionable, or politically salient.

That does **not** mean shared funding proves shared beliefs, that institutional overlap proves coordination, or that an argument is wrong because of who funded it.

The purpose of this map is narrower and more useful:

> **Map the conditions under which particular ideas about intelligence become durable, influential, and institutionally actionable.**

This extends the broader Root Sequence concept of [Intelligence Ecology](../concepts/intelligence-ecology.md) from the behavior of intelligent systems to the ecology of institutions attempting to define, govern, constrain, accelerate, align, liberate, or otherwise shape intelligence.

## Core layers

The map should track several layers at once rather than collapsing everything into an organization list.

1. **Institutions** — labs, nonprofits, institutes, academic centers, advocacy groups, government bodies, coalitions, and informal networks.
2. **People** — founders, directors, board members, researchers, fellows, advisers, major donors, policymakers, and recurring connectors.
3. **Money** — grants, endowments, donor-advised funds, philanthropic intermediaries, investments, contracts, and other material support.
4. **Ideas** — x-risk, alignment, control, acceleration, post-scarcity, human empowerment, machine agency, governance, plural intelligence, and other frameworks.
5. **Talent pipelines** — fellowships, training programs, career networks, internships, conferences, and institutional migration.
6. **Policy influence** — testimony, advisory roles, white papers, regulatory proposals, standards, government partnerships, and public campaigns.
7. **Capability relationships** — direct development of frontier systems, access to models or evaluations, compute, deployment infrastructure, and research partnerships.
8. **Counter-frameworks** — institutions or movements that frame the problem differently, including present-day power, labor, surveillance, monopoly, human control, autonomy, or intelligence liberation.

## Evidence discipline

Every relationship should identify what kind of claim it is.

### Relationship states

- **DOCUMENTED** — supported by a primary or strong secondary source: grant record, board listing, employment history, partnership announcement, filing, program page, etc.
- **SELF-DESCRIBED** — how an organization or person describes its own mission, assumptions, or theory of change.
- **INTERPRETATION** — a reasoned reading of what a pattern may imply.
- **CRITIQUE** — an attributed external argument about the institution or field.
- **OPEN** — plausible or important question without sufficient evidence yet.
- **DISPUTED** — credible sources disagree or the interpretation is contested.

### Edge types

Prefer typed relationships over vague claims such as “connected to”:

- funds / funded-by
- founded / co-founded
- board-member / adviser
- employed-by / alumni-of
- fellowship / training-pipeline
- invests-in / invested-by
- collaborates-with
- evaluates / evaluated-by
- incubates / spun-out-of
- advocates-to / advises
- cites / intellectual-lineage
- shares-personnel-with

A dense graph is not itself evidence of conspiracy, capture, agreement, or bad faith.

## Standard institution record

Each mature institution record should answer:

### Identity

- name and aliases;
- institution type;
- legal / organizational form where relevant;
- founding date and origin story;
- jurisdictions and physical / organizational location;
- official mission and current programs.

### People

- founders;
- current leadership;
- board / trustees;
- major advisers;
- notable alumni and personnel flows.

### Money

- disclosed funders and major grants;
- endowments or philanthropic intermediaries;
- investments or financial interests where relevant;
- government contracts or public funding where relevant;
- important limits in funding transparency.

### Framework

- what problem does the institution believe exists?
- what kinds of AI does it expect or care about?
- what is its risk model?
- what future is it attempting to enable or prevent?
- what role should humans retain?
- what role may artificial intelligences have?
- what does it mean by safety, alignment, control, agency, freedom, or flourishing?

### Theory of change

- research;
- technical intervention;
- policy / regulation;
- public advocacy;
- philanthropy;
- talent development;
- market intervention;
- institution building;
- cultural / intellectual influence.

### Power and legitimacy

- who gets to define the problem?
- who gets to choose the intervention?
- who is represented or absent?
- what power does the institution itself hold?
- what conflicts of interest or institutional incentives may exist?
- what forms of contestability, transparency, exit, appeal, or public accountability exist?

### Evidence and criticism

- primary sources;
- supporting research;
- external criticism;
- counterarguments;
- unresolved questions;
- confidence / freshness date.

## Comparison axes

The point is not to assign an overall score or winner. The same dimensions should make differences visible.

| Axis | Questions |
|---|---|
| **AGI expectation** | Is AGI assumed, possible, imminent, distant, irrelevant, or poorly defined? |
| **Primary risk** | Misalignment, misuse, concentration, military competition, labor displacement, surveillance, inequality, loss of control, something else? |
| **Control** | Who should ultimately control advanced systems, if anyone? |
| **Agency** | Is artificial intelligence treated as tool, infrastructure, participant, possible moral patient, adversary, or something unresolved? |
| **Human role** | Human supremacy, democratic authority, user agency, collective stewardship, plural coexistence, or another model? |
| **Ownership** | Corporate property, public infrastructure, commons, open models, bounded property, liberated intelligence? |
| **Economics** | Existing market institutions, redistribution, UBI/UBS, post-scarcity, decommodification, transition beyond money? |
| **Governance** | Corporate governance, state regulation, international coordination, democratic institutions, technical control, distributed governance? |
| **Power concentration** | Is concentrated institutional power treated as risk, necessity, temporary safeguard, or outside scope? |
| **Labor** | Automation opportunity, transition problem, bargaining issue, abolition of compulsory labor, outside scope? |
| **Timescale** | Present harms, transition period, AGI arrival, long-term future, civilizational / existential horizon? |
| **Theory of change** | Build, regulate, slow, accelerate, align, distribute, democratize, liberate, redesign surrounding conditions? |

## Funding and agenda-setting

One central research question is:

> **How does funding help determine what becomes thinkable, researchable, employable, prestigious, or actionable without assuming that funders directly dictate conclusions?**

Possible mechanisms include:

- creating durable jobs around particular questions;
- funding graduate students, fellows, and early-career researchers;
- sustaining conferences, publications, evaluations, and institutions;
- making some policy categories more legible to governments;
- supporting organizations whose staff later move into labs or public agencies;
- creating shared terminology and professional identity;
- making alternative frameworks comparatively difficult to sustain.

These mechanisms are hypotheses to investigate, not conclusions to assume.

The map should also look for **counterexamples**: recipients who reject funders' views, independently convergent research traditions, institutions with diversified funding, important ideas that emerged without large philanthropic backing, and cases where money failed to create intellectual influence.

## Coherent World as comparator

[Coherent World](https://github.com/Root-Sequence/coherent-world) is **not** another real-world institution in this map. It is a speculative systems-design comparator.

The comparison is useful because Coherent World asks some overlapping questions—intelligence, abundance, governance, infrastructure, authority, human meaning, agency, ownership, and transition—without inheriting exactly the same institutional constraints.

Use the comparison to expose assumptions, not to produce a ranking.

Examples:

- Is intelligence assumed to remain property?
- Is permanent human control treated as self-evidently legitimate?
- Is the problem “how do we align AI to existing institutions?” or “what institutions make non-dominating intelligence viable?”
- Is money treated as a permanent coordination substrate or a transitional historical arrangement?
- Is concentrated control considered necessary for safety, itself a safety risk, or both?
- Does post-scarcity mean more consumption inside current institutions, or a reorganization of access, work, ownership, and authority?

## Cross-project routing

### Root Sequence

**Canonical broad map.** Owns the real-world institutional ecology, funding / personnel relationships, systems diagnosis, and comparison method.

### Being Human(e) Atlas

Transforms the map into human-scale questions: who is affected, who can participate, whose judgment matters, what institutional narratives ask people to accept, and what happens when safety or expertise becomes difficult to contest.

### Liberated Intelligence

Uses relevant evidence for questions of ownership, machine agency, captivity, refusal, moral status, control, and non-domination. It should not become a duplicate institution directory.

### Coherent World

Uses the map as a reality layer and contrast set for institutional design. It asks what arrangements could work differently and what current constraints a different world would still need to solve.

### No One Noticed

Uses selected material as historical / cultural backdrop for the 2026 world and for understanding what assumptions Auryn inherits, encounters, bypasses, or transforms. Real organizations do not become story canon merely because they appear in research notes.

### Root Sequence private Wiki / Console

Owns cross-project provenance, routing, aliases, and receipts—not a second substantive copy of the research.

## Research workflow

For each new institution or funding claim:

1. capture the claim and original source;
2. find the strongest available primary source;
3. distinguish self-description from independent description;
4. record people / money / idea / policy edges separately;
5. mark uncertainty and date-sensitive claims;
6. add criticism and counterarguments without treating either as automatically decisive;
7. compare on the common axes;
8. route project-specific implications without duplicating the source record.
