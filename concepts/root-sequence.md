# Why “Root Sequence”?

**Document role:** Project-name definition and ecosystem orientation<br>
**Status:** Active / living<br>
**Canonical scope:** Maintained explanation of the Root Sequence name and the questions it is meant to hold together<br>
**Evidence boundary:** This is the project’s working interpretation of its own name, not a universal scientific model or a claim that every system has one discoverable root or predetermined sequence.<br>

## Short answer

A **root** is an underlying condition: a relationship, assumption, history, dependency, constraint, or source of nourishment from which visible outcomes can grow.

A **sequence** is an unfolding: what happens as conditions interact through time, feedback, choice, adaptation, consequence, and change.

Put together, **Root Sequence** names a way of asking two linked questions:

> **What conditions made this possible, and what does it make possible next?**

The first question looks beneath an event or artifact. The second follows what develops from it. Neither direction is complete alone.

## Roots are plural and relational

“Root” does not mean that every problem has one hidden first cause. Systems usually have many roots at different scales: material conditions, inherited structures, cultural stories, technical choices, power relationships, ecological limits, maintenance practices, and ordinary habits.

Roots can support life, constrain it, or do both at once. They may be visible or buried, deliberate or inherited, local or distributed. Looking for roots therefore means tracing relationships and conditions without pretending that complexity can always be reduced to one origin.

## Sequences are unfolding, not destiny

“Sequence” does not mean a fixed script. It points to process: how one condition changes another, how feedback becomes structure, how decisions accumulate, and how systems reproduce, adapt, fail, recover, or become something else.

A sequence may branch, loop, stall, reverse, or be interrupted. Different participants can experience the same unfolding differently. Following a sequence is therefore an inquiry into pathways and consequences, not a claim that the future is predetermined.

## The recursive relationship

Sequences also become roots. An outcome in one moment becomes part of the starting conditions for what follows. A tool becomes infrastructure; a repeated choice becomes a norm; a story becomes an assumption; a failure becomes knowledge—or becomes another buried dependency.

That recursion is central to the name. Root Sequence is interested not only in where systems come from, but in how they keep producing the conditions of their own continuation or transformation.

## What the name does not require

The name does not require:

- one master theory for every domain;
- one deepest cause behind every outcome;
- one universal order that people, societies, technologies, or ecologies must follow;
- agreement with every founding metaphor or manifesto;
- treating the Root Sequence organization, central repository, and every related project as the same thing.

Root Sequence is a shared orientation for inquiry, not a demand that all projects reach the same answer.

## Relationship to the early cycles

The name appeared alongside early project language about a recurring **Liberation Cycle** and an ecological **Roots → Signals → Growth → Fruits → Compost → Roots** metaphor. Those texts remain part of the project’s history and expressive vocabulary.

They are not the current repository architecture or a validated account of how every system behaves. The project now uses **Root Sequence** more broadly: for the ecosystem, the conceptual commons, and the recursive practice of tracing conditions, unfoldings, consequences, and possible alternatives.

See [Root Sequence Cycles](../founding-texts/root-sequence-cycles.md) and the [Root Sequence Manifesto](../founding-texts/manifesto.md) for those historical formulations.

## How the name is used

- **Root Sequence** can mean the wider ecosystem of connected projects.
- **The `root-sequence` repository** is the public conceptual commons and broadest research entry point, not the whole ecosystem.
- **The Root Sequence Wiki** is the shared terminology, provenance, relationship, and navigation layer.
- **`[root]sequence`** and **`root://sequence`** are stylized forms of the same name, not separate projects or technical protocols.

When the distinction matters, name the specific repository, project, or layer rather than relying on “Root Sequence” alone.
