The Universal Coherence Framework (UCF) describes how systems align—or fail to align—with reality over time.

It is not a fixed model. It is a way of observing whether a system holds together internally, interacts accurately with the world, and sustains itself without generating hidden instability.

## Core idea

Coherence is the degree to which:

- a system’s internal model matches reality
- its behavior aligns with that model
- its relationships with other systems remain stable over time

Incoherence appears when these fall out of alignment.

A system can be stable while still being incoherent.

## Layers

### 1. Reality
What is actually happening.

### 2. Model
How the system interprets reality.

Misclassification occurs here.

### 3. Behavior
What the system does in response.

Asymmetry appears here.

### 4. Dynamics
How actions feed back over time.

Feedback loops and reinforcement operate here.

### 5. Outcome
What stabilizes as normal.

This can be coherent or incoherent.

### 6. Transformation
What happens when the system is disrupted.

Non-reversal determines whether patterns end or continue.

## Coherence vs incoherence

Coherent systems:

- track reality accurately
- adapt without accumulating distortion
- sustain relationships without hidden instability

Incoherent systems:

- misinterpret reality
- reinforce distortion
- appear stable while accumulating harm

## Relationship to systems principles

The core system principles describe how coherence is produced or degraded:

- asymmetric systems -> shape conditions unevenly
- misclassification -> distorts the model
- feedback loops -> propagate patterns
- reinforcement -> stabilizes patterns
- non-reversal -> determines whether patterns persist after disruption

## Key insight

Coherence is not automatic.

Systems naturally drift toward incoherence when distortion is repeated and reinforced.

## Implication

Improving a system is not only about changing ideas.

It requires:

- improving alignment with reality
- interrupting harmful feedback loops
- changing what gets reinforced
- preventing the reintroduction of harmful patterns after disruption

## Questions

- does the system’s model match reality?
- are behaviors aligned with that model?
- what patterns are being reinforced?
- is the system becoming more or less coherent over time?
